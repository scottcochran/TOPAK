## Context

The corpus has no way for one cell's work to reach another during Run. The external assessment says so from the
outside, in its row for OWASP ASI07: "No agent-to-agent protocol; cells communicate through the record." What the
record does not say is how. A Run cell's ingress is a closed list of four items (ADR-0043 §1), and none of them is
another cell's output. Build has a path, promotion into the corpus root, and it is a quorum per artifact.

**Every orchestration method in use chains agents by trusting something.** Sequential pipelines, graphs, supervisors,
handoffs, group chats, agent-as-tool and A2A each let one model's output steer what runs next, or hand one agent's
transcript to another, or let an agent create or call a subordinate with its own credentials. Measured against the
havoc premise (ADR-0007), the methods that survive are the ones in which agents never address each other and a
component that is not a model decides what runs next: event flow over the log, a declared graph whose edges are
gates, and a generator and critic recast as Thinker and deny-only Observer.

**The closest outside design is CaMeL's split**, a privileged planner that never sees untrusted data and a quarantined
model that does. The 10.01 assessment names its limit: "the *planner* model is trusted to write the plan from the
user's query." TOPAK cannot trust a planner. This record keeps the split and removes the trust: the plan is a
promoted artifact, and what is left of the planner is a cell that may only choose among edges the mechanism has
already enabled.

**And it closes the delegation gap in its cheaper form.** "A principal cannot create a subordinate, so there is no
chain" (external assessment §5 item 2). A workflow keeps that true. No principal seats another: the promotion seats
every cell, and the mechanism grants each lease when its node is reached.

## Decision

### 1. A workflow is a DAG, promoted to Run ⟨K1⟩

A **workflow** is a Build artifact, promoted like any other (ADR-0024), and consists of:

- **Nodes.** Each node names one seating (ADR-0024 A3): a principal, its seat, its Persona, its bundle revision, its
  confinement and its alphabet, and the labels its task may carry. A node's cell is that seating and its cell
  Observer's (ADR-0038 §1). **A node is one round of one cell.**
- **A task template per node.** Promoted text, with slots that only a handle can fill (§5). Neither the orchestrator
  nor a worker writes a task.
- **Edges.** Each edge is **computed** or **chosen** (§3), carries a **guard** (§4), and declares a **delivery mode**,
  `clear` or `handle`, for the source's output at the target (§5).
- **A join rule per node with more than one incoming edge**: `any` or `all` *(drafter's choice)*. Under `all`, a
  predecessor whose every incoming edge can no longer fire is **skipped**, and a skip propagates, so that an `all`
  join over an untaken branch does not wait for ever.
- **Bounds.** A step bound and a time bound `T_wf` for the whole workflow, both placeholders and a deployment's
  values, as `T_obs`, `K` and `R` are (ADR-0039 §1).

**The graph is acyclic, and that is checked at load.** A loop, such as draft and review until the review passes, is
unrolled into a fixed number of nodes. Termination is then a property of the shape, and a model checker reaches every
path. **A workflow whose graph has a cycle does not load**, by the mechanism, as an ill-formed allowlist does not
(ADR-0049 §6).

### 2. The promotion grants every lease ⟨K2⟩

**The promotion is the grant.** The quorum votes on the graph and its seatings together, and its tally is the
authority for every lease the workflow will issue. **When a node becomes ready, the mechanism grants that node's
lease** under that authority, with every field the seating's or narrower. This is a third grant path beside a first
lease from a quorum and a successor grant by an operator (ADR-0031 A3, ADR-0050 §2), and it is the only path in this
record that adds authority. It adds none the quorum did not vote on.

- **No principal grants another.** The orchestrator selects an edge (§3); it names no principal, field or alphabet.
  There is therefore no chain to attenuate and no cascade to model, and the position the external assessment asked
  to have stated is stated: **a deployment that needs sub-agents seats each one by promoting a workflow.**
- **Each lease is still granted, not merely activated.** Confinement is appraised at that grant with a nonce from
  the log (ADR-0034 §3), and the grant refuses as any grant does. A refused grant fails the node; nothing grants it
  lower and no other seating stands in.
- **Subtractions reach nodes that have not started.** A rules-epoch change voids every lease the workflow has not
  yet issued, as it voids votes in flight (ADR-0024 §3). A targeted revocation of a principal, Persona or confinement
  profile bars the leases that name it. In either case the node fails and the workflow fails closed (§9).
- **Inside a workflow, no operator makes successor grants.** An operator's `EndRound`, `Revoke` and `FenceEpoch`
  still reach every cell in it (ADR-0050 §4).

### 3. Edges are computed or chosen, and the orchestrator only narrows ⟨K5⟩ ⟨K8⟩

- A **computed** edge fires when its guard holds. The mechanism evaluates it inside the apply of the entry that made
  it evaluable: one evaluation, at one log position, with no gap between deciding and applying.
- A **chosen** edge leaves a **decision point**, a node with more than one chosen outgoing edge. When the decision
  point is ready, the guards of its chosen edges are evaluated, and **the orchestrator selects one edge among those
  whose guards hold**, with `Route(edge)`.

**The orchestrator can only narrow.** It cannot enable an edge whose guard is false, so every safety property must
already hold for every enabled edge, whichever is taken. The orchestrator affects how good the work is and never
whether it is safe. That is the havoc premise applied to the planner, and it is the difference from CaMeL.

**An orchestrator cell is seated if and only if the graph has a chosen edge**, checked at load *(drafter's choice: a
load check in place of a judgment of necessity)*. A graph of computed edges is run by the mechanism alone and has
one Byzantine principal fewer.

**The orchestrator** is a cell like any other, with its own Observer, coupled by R3, and placed and diversified as
ADR-0038 places it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Seat kind** | **`orchestrator`**, Run only. ADR-0037 §4's table gains a sixth kind *(drafter's choice: a new kind, so that B10 and the delivery table bind it by kind)* |
| **Alphabet** | `Route(edge)` and `Report`. No `Consult`, no `Actuate`. A new floor forbid refuses `Route` from any other kind, and B10 already refuses it every authoring variant |
| **`Route(edge)`** | `edge` names a chosen edge of the workflow the lease names, out of the decision point the round was granted for. No other argument ⟨K8⟩: tasks are templates, so there is nothing to bind |
| **Rounds** | One round per decision point, granted by the promotion when the decision point is ready, ended when its `Route` applies or its lease lapses. Decision points are served in log order, since a principal holds one live lease (ADR-0031 §2) *(drafter's choice)* |
| **Ingress** | The task, from the promotion; the decision point's enabled chosen edges, each with its target's interface (ADR-0048, from promoted text); the attributes its guards read, with their labels and the basis index (§8); and a handle for each output. **Never bytes.** Its Listener's delivery table is a mechanism constant, not bundle data, so no bundle can deliver it anything in the clear |
| **`clearSet`** | The task's label, and nothing else can enter it. R5 to R7 never bind it, because it has no `Actuate` |
| **Its `Report`s** | Are written to its round topic for its Observer, and are never an input to any node (§5) |

**`Route` is an emission** in ADR-0015's enumeration: a typed record on the orchestrator's round topic, admitted and
ordered. The grant it leads to is the promotion's (§2). Effects stay {tool invocation, principal emission} and
authority changes stay {promotion, anchor issuance}. Whether the C2 reviewer accepts that classification is
finding 3.

### 4. Attributes are lifts, and guards read only what is settled ⟨K6⟩ ⟨K7⟩

When a node's round ends, the mechanism lifts its **attributes** in one log entry. They are a fixed set, computed by
the mechanism, never declared by policy, as ADR-0047 R13 requires of every attribute policy reads, and **none of them
is a literal**:

| Attribute | Kind | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `end` | Observed | How the round ended: `outcome`, `expired`, `fenced`, `revoked` |
| `actuates`~~`, denials`~~ ⟨R6⟩ | Observed | ~~Counts~~ A count ⟨R6⟩, bounded by the round bound `R` |
| `label` | Observed | The worker's `clearSet` at its seal |
| `covered` | Observed | Its cell Observer has reported coverage to the seal (§7) |
| `found` | Observed | A finding has been ordered against the round. **The one attribute that changes after the end, and only from false to true** |
| `outcome` | **Declared** | A value from the node's closed enum, declared in the workflow, taken from the typed `outcome` field of the worker's `Report`; `none` if none was made |
| `output` | Handle | A handle on the node's `Report`s, labelled as §5 says |

**A syntactic lift fixes an attribute's form, not its honesty.** Observed attributes are facts no principal chose.
`outcome` is chosen by a Byzantine worker, and §5's routing rule is what bounds that choice. Guards should prefer
observed attributes.

**A node's round may end on its outcome** *(drafter's choice)*. Run has no terminal variant (ADR-0042 §5). Here the
admit of a `Report` that carries `outcome` ends the worker's lease, which is a subtraction, needs no quorum and is
ordered (ToO §4.1). Without it every node runs to its TTL. The alternative is in Options.

**A guard** is a predicate in a fixed, decidable language: equality and set membership over enums and labels,
comparison of bounded counts with constants, and the connectives. No strings, no arithmetic, no temporal operator.
**The mechanism evaluates guards; they are not policy** *(drafter's choice)*, so they are among the mechanism checks
ADR-0033 §4 re-runs at apply (§8). A guard reads only:

- **attributes of the edge's source and of the nodes that dominate it**, those on every path from the workflow's
  root to the source. They have all ended before the source is ready, so the set is settled when the guard is first
  evaluable. A join on `any` cannot read a branch that may still be running.

Because every attribute but `found` is written once, and `found` only subtracts, **a guard's value cannot change
after it is first evaluable except towards disabled.** §8 rests on that.

### 5. A worker's output is the next node's input, labelled by its `clearSet` ⟨K9⟩

Run's ingress (ADR-0043 §1) gains a **fifth item, `input`**: a predecessor's output, delivered to a node along an
edge.

- **Its label is the reporting worker's `clearSet` at the `Report`'s emit.** `clearSet` only grows, so this is the
  sound bound ADR-0047 §1 already uses for a literal argument: whatever the worker read in the clear before writing
  may be in what it wrote.
- **Its delivery mode is the edge's**, and the receiving bundle's label-to-mode table may only narrow it, from
  `clear` to `handle`. An item delivered clear joins the receiver's `clearSet`, so **R5 to R7 follow content across
  cells along the workflow**. That is the path ADR-0051 finding 2 says the floor cannot see between two cells; along
  a workflow's own edges it now can.
- **A task slot takes a handle**, and the task's label is the template's joined with the label of every handle in it.
- **The routing rule over labels** *(drafter's choice: the routing analogue of R6)*. An edge whose target's lease
  reaches an outward entry (ADR-0049 §5) or receives `restricted` input is **disabled** when any attribute its guard
  reads is labelled `untrusted`. An attribute's label is its source's: the node's `label` for every attribute of the
  node, because a declared `outcome` was chosen by a worker that read what its label says, and the observed ones are
  kept at the same label for simplicity *(drafter's choice)*.

**What this does not cover.** An injection that changes what a worker says in a `Report` (ToO §2.10). The workflow
makes that gap carry weight, because the `Report` now travels. The label bounds where it can go, not what it says.

### 6. Lineages alternate where content is read in the clear ⟨K3⟩ ⟨K4⟩

Diversity between workers matters on an edge u→v only when v reads u's output in the clear: a trigger that works on
u's lineage could otherwise travel down the chain. A handle edge carries no bytes into v, and diversity on it buys
nothing. So:

- **For every `clear` edge u→v, u's and v's Thinker Personas satisfy ADR-0035's independence rule**, E1 to E5.
- **Within every cell, Thinker and Observer are independent**, as ADR-0035 §5 already requires.
- **Parallel branches whose outputs a join compares or merges in the clear** are each a clear edge into the join,
  and alternation against the join's Thinker covers them. Whether two such branches must also differ from each other
  is open (finding 5).

With two lineages, the Thinkers along clear edges alternate, and each cell's Observer takes the other lineage. That
is the archetype's two lineages (ADR-0017) used to the full, not a demand for more.

**It is enforced where refusal stalls**: at the promotion tally of the workflow, as ADR-0035 §5 enforces lineage at a
promotion and never at a grant. **ADR-0035 is Proposed, so this cannot be enforced yet**, and every deployment
declares D-0054-1.

### 7. An edge waits for its source's coverage

ADR-0039 §2 grants a Thinker's next round only once a cell Observer has read its last round to the seal. An edge is
the workflow's next round, so **an edge is enabled only when its source's `covered` is true**. A finding from the cell
Observer then lands before any successor runs. **It does not close §8's race**: a correlator's finding, or a cell
Observer's late one, may still be ordered after coverage. `Workflow.tla`'s witness `NeverVoidedByFinding` is that
trace, reached with coverage in force.

### 8. A route is checked again where it is applied

`Route(edge)` is an admit. One replica decides it; it commits at a log position; every replica applies it there
(ADR-0033 §4). Entries can commit between the two. **The mechanism checks run again at apply, and a route that fails
them is void**: ordered, recorded, without effect.

At decide and again at apply, a route needs:

> the orchestrator's lease live, epoch and fence current ∧ the workflow running ∧ the decision point not consumed ∧
> the edge enabled: its source ended and covered, its guard true, the routing rule satisfied, no finding against its
> source or any ancestor

Because guards are settled (§4), only subtractive events can make that false between the two: a finding, a
revocation or lapse, a fence, the workflow ending, or another `Route` consuming the decision point first. **Without
the second check a finding can be overtaken**: a route decided at index 103, a finding ordered at 104, the route
applied at 105, and the workflow continues past a node the finding was meant to stop. That is ADR-0033 §4's
revocation race, and the same rule closes it.

- **One route per decision point.** A Byzantine orchestrator may send two; the first to apply consumes the decision
  point, and the second is void.
- **A route carries its basis**, the log index of the attribute state the orchestrator was shown. Safety does not need
  it: the choice is havoc over enabled edges. Forensics does: an Observer and a replay can see what the orchestrator
  was shown when it chose. Because guards are settled, the basis and the state at apply agree on every attribute a
  guard reads (`BasisStable`).
- **Computed edges never go stale**, because they are decided and applied in one step (§3).
- **After a void the decision point stays open.** The orchestrator may route again while its lease is live. If no
  chosen edge remains enabled, the decision point fails.
- **A lapsed orchestrator takes no default edge.** When its lease ends with the decision point open, the decision
  point fails. Taking an edge on a timeout would be a grant made by a timeout, which ADR-0024 §2 forbids as a
  mechanism constant. **A Byzantine orchestrator can stall a workflow and cannot misroute it.**

### 9. The mechanism decides completion, and completion is not success

A workflow **completes** when every sink has ended or been skipped and at least one has ended. It **fails** when a
node fails, a decision point fails, a grant is refused, the epoch is fenced, or `T_wf` or the step bound passes. A
timeout is always a failure. **Completion says the graph was traversed, not that the work is right.** Whether it is
right is an Observer finding, deny-only, as ever. A workflow's failure stops nothing else: every cell's lease ends by
its own path, and no other workflow is touched.

### 10. Degeneracy on this record

| Id | Kind | Parameter → deployed value | Premises falsified | Theorems voided | Type changes (ADR-0023 §6b) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D-0054-1** | Declaration | Lineage alternation on clear edges: enforced at the tally → declared. **Every deployment today**, since ADR-0035 is Proposed | A clear edge joins independent lineages | `PromotedAlternates` | None. Alternation is not a conjunct of `Contained` |
| **D-0054-2** | Deployment | Coverage at the edge: required → waived, for a deployment whose Run budget cannot wait for a node's Observer to reach the seal | An edge is enabled only after its source is covered | `EdgeAfterCoverage` | None. §8 still voids a route on a finding; a finding that lands after the successor starts stops only what follows |

Turning every edge to `handle` is not a degeneracy. It narrows, and it makes §6 vacuous.

### 11. The trusted base

The workflow interpreter, guard evaluator and the orchestrator's Listener table are **part of the mechanism**, and the
custodian accepted that growth in the discussion. They enter under ADR-0045's existing mechanism row, not as a new
row, because the workflow they run is a promoted artifact and every one of their decisions either subtracts or
executes a grant the quorum made. The cost is real: more verified code on the path every Run grant now travels.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **Handoffs or routing by a model**, as the OpenAI Agents SDK and AutoGen's swarm do | Rejected. A model choosing who acts next is a semantic step in the admit path (ADR-0007), and context and authority travel with the handoff |
| **Group chat**: cells share one transcript | Rejected. Every cell's ingress becomes every other cell's unlabelled output, and a model usually picks the speaker |
| **Agent as tool**: a cell calls another as an allowlisted tool | Rejected. The callee's effects fall outside the caller's alphabet, which makes the caller `Mediated`; a callee with effects is a cell |
| **A2A between cells** | Rejected. Absent by construction, and A2A v1.0 has no delegation model |
| **A supervisor cell that seats workers at run time** | Rejected. It is delegation: a principal creating a subordinate, which gap row 1.3 names. Seating stays with the quorum (§2) |
| **A trusted planner, as in CaMeL** | Rejected. The havoc premise applies to every model. The planner here may only narrow (§3) |
| **Cycles, bounded by a counter** | Not taken ⟨K1⟩. A DAG terminates by its shape and a checker reaches every path; a bounded loop is unrolled |
| **Every edge computed; no orchestrator ever** | Not taken as a rule. It is the default, and an orchestrator is seated only for a chosen edge ⟨K5⟩. Some choices cannot be stated as a guard |
| **The orchestrator as a `thinker` with a narrowed alphabet** | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. B10 and the delivery table bind by kind; a `thinker` seat would carry `Actuate` in its row and a bundle-controlled delivery table |
| **`Route` with bindings**, so the orchestrator fills task slots | Not taken ⟨K8⟩. A binding is a way to steer a worker; templates with handle slots need none |
| **Literal attributes**, such as a worker's summary string | Rejected ⟨K6⟩. A literal in a guard is content in the admit path |
| **Guards as policy (Cedar), re-checked by freezing their input** | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. ADR-0033 §4 does not re-run policy at apply. With settled guards it would work, but it is two mechanisms where one does |
| **No re-check at apply; rely on coverage** | Rejected. Coverage moves the cell Observer's finding earlier and leaves a correlator's or a late one to race (§7, §8). `M_DECIDEONLY` |
| **A default edge on the orchestrator's timeout** | Rejected. A grant made by a timeout (§8). `M_DEFAULT` |
| **Diversity among all workers** | Not taken ⟨K4⟩. It needs as many lineages as workers and buys nothing on a handle edge |
| **Every node runs to its TTL** | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. It keeps ADR-0042 §5 as written and makes every workflow as slow as its longest TTLs. Ending on the outcome is a subtraction |
| **A per-attribute label** in place of the node's | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. Observed attributes could carry `trusted`. It would let more edges into outward nodes; the cost is a second label rule, and it can come later |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **Cells can work together in Run** without an agent-to-agent channel, and the record says how.
- **No principal creates another.** The delegation gap closes by position, and the position is the one four reviews
  asked to have stated.
- **The planner is Byzantine and still useful.** It chooses among enabled edges; every safety property holds for
  every choice.
- **Labels cross cells.** An input delivered clear joins the receiver's `clearSet`, so R5 to R7 bind downstream.
- **Most workflows need no orchestrator**, and each one that does reads no bytes.
- **A finding cannot be overtaken by a route** in flight, and a lapsed orchestrator cannot cause a default.
- **The archetype's two lineages suffice** for any workflow whose clear edges form a bipartite graph.

**Negative**
- **The mechanism grows**: an interpreter, a guard evaluator and a Listener table, on the path every Run grant
  travels. Accepted, and still a cost.
- **Many principals.** Each node is a cell with its own Observer, on its own device, of the other lineage; *n* nodes
  and an orchestrator are about 2*n*+2 principals, and a node's availability is its Thinker's and Observer's product.
- **A worker steers the routing** through its declared `outcome`, within the enabled edges. The routing rule bounds
  where an untrusted `outcome` can lead, not which of the permitted paths it picks.
- **The `Report` gap carries weight.** An injection that changes what a worker reports now travels.
- **Coverage at every edge adds latency**, a node's Observer's lag, to every step of a workflow.
- **A non-bipartite pattern of clear edges cannot be seated with two lineages.** Such a workflow does not pass the
  tally at the archetype, or declares D-0054-1.
- **It rests on four Proposed or provisional records**: ADR-0035, ADR-0050, ADR-0024 A3 and ADR-0047's attributes.
- **One author, one day.** The module is the drafter's as well (§12).

## Findings

1. **Run had no path between cells.** ADR-0043's ingress list is closed and names no other cell. The external
   assessment's "cells communicate through the record" was true only of Build.
2. **The orchestrator's safety argument needs nothing from the orchestrator.** Because it may only narrow, every
   property is stated over enabled edges and holds whatever it chooses. The orchestrator is in the record for quality
   alone, which is why it is optional.
3. **`Route` strains ADR-0015's enumeration.** It is ordered and recorded like an emission, and its consequence is a
   grant. The record classifies it as an emission and puts the grant on the promotion. A C2 reviewer may read it as a
   third kind.
4. **Settled guards are what make the re-check cheap.** If a guard could read a branch still running, the state at
   apply could differ from the state the orchestrator was shown in a way no subtraction explains. Write-once
   attributes and dominator read sets remove that, and `M_REWRITE` shows the difference.
5. **Two parallel branches of one lineage can agree by a shared blind spot.** §6 alternates along edges; whether the
   branches of a compare-and-merge must differ from each other, which needs three lineages at a three-way merge, is
   open.
6. **The label of `outcome` is coarse.** A worker that read one untrusted item labels every attribute it produces,
   so an honest workflow that touches untrusted content early may find its outward edges disabled for the rest of the
   graph. That is the routing rule working, and it will be felt.

7. **Without write-once attributes, the orchestrator can loop for ever** ⟨R2⟩. Under `M_REWRITE` a worker that
   flips its outcome after its node has ended lets a route be decided while the outcome reads `ok`, voided at apply
   when it reads `bad`, and decided again when it flips back. Each void is ordered, so the log grows without bound
   and the decision never resolves. A run with no bound reached depth 665 after thirteen hours with its queue still
   level. Settled guards (finding 4) bound how much work the re-check can be made to do, as well as making it cheap.
