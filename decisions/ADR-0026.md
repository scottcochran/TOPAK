# ADR-0026 — The rules floor bounds every anchor issuance

**Status:** Accepted (genesis) · 2026.09.23 ⟨B2⟩
**Status note:** Confirmation required: no. Bootstrap set — ADR-0029 §4's exception, since the
confirmation promotion runs under this record.
**Deployment position:** The archetype rules floor is specified below. Relaxing any entry is a degeneracy.
**Amends:** ADR-0010 (verified object; floor-change direction; A1 and A2 in ADR-0010 History)
**Pending:** A1 (R8, break-glass grant TTL), drafted 2026.09.24. Not in force; see History.
**Depends on:** ADR-0025 (issuances), ADR-0010 (verified object), ADR-0007 (gates)
**Depended on by:** ADR-0024, ADR-0027

## Context

Under ADR-0025, the anchor is a havoc issuer. Without a bound, havoc over rules is havoc over
authority: an issuance could set a promotion roster of one, and no theorem about promotion would
survive.

ADR-0010 solved the same problem one layer down. Bundles are unbounded parameters, admissible only
if they refine a verified floor. This record applies that move to rules.

**Why this is a separate record.** Superseding the rules floor (for example, deciding issuances are
unbounded) would leave ADR-0025's havoc model and axiom coherent. The two clauses fail each other's
supersession test, so they are separate decisions.

## Decision

### 1. The rules floor is part of the verified object

The mechanism rejects any issuance that violates it. Proofs quantify over all floor-conformant
issuance sequences.

### 2. Archetype contents

| Entry | Bounds |
|---|---|
| **R1** `rules.quorum = "all"` (unanimity over counted grant seats) | ADR-0024 |
| **R2** `rules.minGrant ≥ 2` | ADR-0024 |
| ~~**R3** `rules.recuse = TRUE` (author recusal)~~ | **Withdrawn 09.23 ⟨R1⟩.** Vacuous: recusal is enforced by the ADR-0022 `Vote` envelope and is a mechanism constant in ADR-0024 §6, so `recuse` is not an issued value and this entry bounds nothing (S6-3). ID reserved, not reused. |
| ~~**R4** Vote timeout ⇒ deny~~ | **Withdrawn 09.15.** Vacuous as a rules-floor entry; moved to ADR-0024 as a mechanism constant. ID reserved, not reused. |
| **R5** No key both in the anchor key set and on the promotion roster for the same artifact | ADR-0025 / ADR-0024 |
| **R6** Bound on floor-exception count (current count: 2) | ADR-0013 |
| **R7** No break-glass grant may authorize `Issue`, `Vote`, or promotion | ADR-0009 / ADR-0013 (discharges break-glass × issuance and break-glass × promotion) |
| **R9** `rules.quorum = "all" ∨ rules.quorum ≥ 2` ⟨R1⟩ | ADR-0024. Relaxing R1 alone yields a threshold *k*; without R9 that threshold may be 1, which is D-0024-2 (single signer) entering as though it were D-0024-1. R9 keeps the two degeneracies separately declared. **A conditional entry** ⟨R3⟩: it binds only once R1 is relaxed, and its independence is stated relative to a floor without R1 (§7, finding 1). R8 remains reserved for the break-glass TTL candidate. |

*Pending ⟨A1⟩, not applied:* R8 would enter this table as `0 < rules.breakGlass.ttl ≤ T_BG`. See History A1.

ADR-0024 decides *that* promotion is a quorum grant, and its mechanics. Issued rules carry two
quorum parameters: `quorum` — either `"all"` or a grant-vote threshold *k* — and `minGrant`. The
mechanism reads them, and R1, R2 and R9 bound them. A promotion degeneracy is a change to those
entries, not to ADR-0024. ⟨R1⟩

**Parametricity is load-bearing.** If the mechanism hard-codes these values, R1, R2 and R9 constrain
nothing and are vacuous (§6). The converse also holds, and is why R3 is withdrawn: a value the
mechanism fixes as a constant cannot be bounded by a floor entry. ⟨R1⟩

### 3. Gates run on the rules floor

ADR-0007's totality, non-vacuity, and discrimination gates apply here. A vacuous rules floor admits
a roster of none. The floor-fitness gate (MEASURE 2.13), when it exists, applies here too.

### 4. Changing the rules floor needs two keys

A rules-floor change is a mechanism release. It requires both:
- an anchor issuance (ADR-0025); and
- a promotion (ADR-0024), under the rules being replaced.

**Tightening and relaxing are both two-key.** A tightened rules floor constrains the anchor itself,
so the anchor must consent to it. This is where the rules floor differs from the policy floor.

### 5. Floor-change direction (policy floor)

- **Tightening the policy floor** is an ordinary promotion.
- **Relaxing the policy floor, or adding an exception,** is a rules change: an anchor issuance plus a
  promotion.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| No rules floor (unbounded havoc issuer) | Rejected. No promotion theorem survives, and degradation escapes declaration. |
| Rules floor inside ADR-0025 | Rejected by the decomposition test. |
| Rules-floor contents in ADR-0024 | Rejected. Couples promotion mechanics to archetype values, so every promotion degeneracy would supersede 0024. |
| Tighten-only rules-floor changes by promotion alone | Rejected. Promotion roster members would constrain the anchor without the anchor's consent. That inverts authority. |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **Promotion safety is universally quantified** over floor-conformant issuances, whatever the
  custody rung.
- **Degeneracy is well-typed.** A promotion degeneracy (D-0024-n) is a rules-floor change, which
  reopens proofs. An anchor custody degeneracy (D-0025-n) is a declaration, which does not.
- **Monotonicity reaches the authority layer.**

**Negative**
- **The verified object grows.** The rules floor must stay minimal, for the same reviewability
  argument as ADR-0010.
- **Global versus per-class floor** now applies to two floors.
- **Two-key changes are slow in both directions.** That is the right price, and it needs stating.

## 6. Non-vacuity check — run 2026.09.15

**Artifact:** `RulesFloorNV.tla` · digest `1468d8bab89bb076` · run with TLC 1.8.0 · raw results in `RulesFloorNV-results-2026.09.15.txt`.

**Model**
- Humans {h1, h2, h3, h4}; author h1; anchor key-holder h4.
- One artifact; one havoc anchor issuance, which fences in-flight votes.
- Actions: votes, timeouts, promotion, break-glass.

**Three mechanism variants** ⟨R2⟩
- `param` — the mechanism reads `quorum`, `minGrant`, `recuse`, and a hypothetical `timeoutGrant`
  from issued rules.
- `as0024` — the mechanism exactly as ADR-0024 revision 3's `PromotionEnabled` snippet.
- `rev4` — the mechanism exactly as ADR-0024 revision 4's snippet: reads `quorum`, `minGrant`, and
  `recuse`; only recorded votes count. Floor is R1–R3, R5, R7.

**Three checks per variant**
- **Satisfiability and independence** (ASSUME). Genesis satisfies all entries. Each entry excludes
  some rule set that all other entries admit.
- **Reachability.** Promotion must be reachable under the full floor, or every safety property holds
  vacuously.
- **Mutation.** Drop one entry and check the property that entry protects. A counterexample is the
  pass condition.

### Results

| Check | `param` | `as0024` | **`rev4`** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Satisfiability and independence (ASSUME) | pass | pass | pass |
| Full floor, all properties | holds | holds | **holds** |
| Reachability (`NeverPromoted` must fail) | counterexample ✓ | counterexample ✓ | **counterexample ✓** |
| Drop R1 → P1 every counted seat granted | counterexample ✓ | holds ✗ vacuous | **counterexample ✓** |
| Drop R2 → P2 at least two approvers | counterexample ✓ | holds ✗ vacuous | **counterexample ✓** |
| Drop R3 → P3 author not counted | counterexample ✓ | holds ✗ vacuous | **counterexample ✓** |
| R4 → P4 no timeout counted as grant | counterexample ✓ *(invented `timeoutGrant` only)* | holds ✗ vacuous | **holds with an empty floor.** A mechanism property, as intended |
| Drop R5 → P5 no anchor key-holder approves | counterexample ✓ | counterexample ✓ | **counterexample ✓** |
| Drop R7 → P7 break-glass never changes authority | counterexample ✓ | counterexample ✓ | **counterexample ✓** |

**Verdict for ADR-0024 revision 4 with floor R1–R3, R5, R7:** every entry is non-vacuous, promotion
is reachable, and all properties hold. The timeout property holds with no floor at all, which
confirms R4's withdrawal.

### Findings

1. **R1–R3 are vacuous against ADR-0024 as written.** Its snippet hard-codes unanimity, recusal, and
   a minimum of 2, so rules cannot express other values and the entries bound nothing. They are
   non-vacuous once the mechanism reads the values from issued rules, which is what the D-0024
   degeneracies already assume. **Fix ADR-0024, not this record.**
2. **R4 is vacuous in any design anyone would choose.** It is non-vacuous only if issued rules could
   make a timeout count as a grant, and no degeneracy wants that parameter. **Withdrawn.** "A timeout
   is never a grant" becomes an ADR-0024 mechanism constant.
3. **R2's counterexample is the vacuous-roster failure, demonstrated.** With R2 dropped, a havoc
   issuance sets an empty roster with `minGrant = 0`. Unanimity over the empty set is vacuously true,
   and the artifact promotes with **zero votes**. R1 alone does not protect against an empty roster;
   R2 is what does.
4. **R5 and R7 are non-vacuous in both variants.** They constrain roster membership and break-glass
   scope, which are genuinely issued.
5. **The smallest satisfying model has four humans** ⟨R2⟩, with one author and one anchor
   key-holder, leaving exactly two counted grant seats. This is a property of the *model*, not a
   genesis headcount: it declares custody (1, 0) for the anchor, which is not A-0025. The archetype
   figure is **five** (ADR-0029), derived as follows — A-0025 requires n ≥ 3 anchor key-holders, R2
   requires two counted grant seats, and R5 keeps the two sets disjoint, giving 3 + 2. The author
   need not be human; if the author holds a seat, recusal (ADR-0024 §6) raises the roster to three
   and the figure to six. ⟨R2⟩

**Scope of this check.** Small constants, a single artifact, and a single issuance. This is
non-vacuity and discrimination, not proof. It is a precursor to the `RulesFloor` and `Promotion`
bootstrap modules (ADR-0029 §5), not a substitute for them.

**Superseded in part, 2026.09.23 ⟨R1⟩.** The results above are the record of the 09.15 run and are
left verbatim. They no longer describe the current mechanism: the `rev4` variant they check reads
`recuse` from issued rules and expresses the non-unanimous case as `Granted # {}`, both of which
S6-2 and S6-3 changed. Three consequences for reading this section: the R3 column tests an entry
that is now withdrawn; no column covers R9, which did not exist; and finding 5's headcount rests on
a model with one anchor key-holder, which is custody (1, 0) rather than A-0025 (S6-10, open). The
`rev5` rerun in Open obligations replaces this section when it is run.

## 7. Non-vacuity rerun — run 2026.09.23 ⟨R3⟩

**Artifact:** `spec/RulesFloorNV_rev5.tla` · digest `3034df144d75ec3a` · TLC2 2.19 · raw results in
`spec/RulesFloorNV-rev5-results-2026.09.23.txt`. This section supersedes §6 for the current
mechanism; §6 stays as the record of the 09.15 run.

**What is different from §6.** The mechanism is ADR-0024 revision 5 — `quorum` is `"all"` or a
threshold *k*, `recuse` is not issued — and **the ADR-0022 `Vote` envelope is modeled**, which §6
never did. That absence is what let §6 report R3 as non-vacuous when the envelope had already
refused the author's vote (S6-3).

**Model.** Five humans; author h1; anchor key-holder h4; one havoc issuance, which fences in-flight
votes; recusal enforced at both sites. The floor is R1, R2, R5, R7, R9.

### Results

| Check | Active floor | Invariant | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Satisfiability, independence (ASSUME) | — | — | pass |
| Full floor | R1 R2 R5 R7 R9 | `Safe` | **holds** |
| Reachability | R1 R2 R5 R7 R9 | `NeverPromoted` | counterexample ✓ |
| Drop R1 | R2 R5 R7 R9 | `P1_Unanimity` | counterexample ✓ |
| Drop R1 **and** R9 | R2 R5 R7 | `P9_NoSoloGrant` | counterexample ✓ |
| Drop R2 | R1 R5 R7 R9 | `P2_TwoSeatsCounted` | counterexample ✓ |
| Recusal removed from the mechanism | R1 R2 R5 R7 R9 | `P3_AuthorNotCounted` | counterexample ✓ |
| Drop R5 | R1 R2 R7 R9 | `P5_AnchorNotApprover` | counterexample ✓ |
| Drop R7 | R1 R2 R5 R9 | `P7_BGNoAuthority` | counterexample ✓ |

### Findings

1. **R9 is a conditional entry, and does not pass the independence test as §6 states it.** Every
   rule set R9 excludes — `quorum` of 0 or 1 — is also excluded by R1, which admits only `"all"`.
   R9 binds only a deployment that has already relaxed R1, which is to say a promotion degeneracy.
   The module therefore states R9's independence relative to a floor without R1, as a separate
   ASSUME. This is a property of the entry, not a defect: R9 exists so that relaxing R1 yields
   D-0024-1 (k-of-n) and not D-0024-2 (single signer) wearing a threshold, and the `dropR1R9` row
   is what demonstrates it. An entry of this kind should be labeled as bounding degeneracies rather
   than the archetype.
2. **Withdrawing R3 is safe.** With recusal removed from both the counted seats (ADR-0024 §6) and
   the `Vote` envelope (ADR-0022 §1, §7), the author self-grants and P3 fails. The property is
   protected by the mechanism, so no floor entry is needed to protect it — which is the test R4's
   withdrawal set in §6, applied to R3.
3. **The 09.15 model was too small to check R1 after S6-2.** At four humans, R5 keeps the anchor
   key-holder off the roster and recusal removes the author, leaving exactly two counted seats, and
   a threshold of *k* ≥ 2 over two seats **is** unanimity — so dropping R1 changed nothing and the
   mutation held vacuously. The rerun uses five, which is independently the archetype figure §6
   finding 5 derives. A threshold mechanism needs a model with at least three counted seats before
   any threshold property means anything.
4. **R2's empty-roster result survives the change to thresholds.** With R2 dropped, a havoc issuance
   still sets a roster small enough to promote on fewer than two counted seats.

**Verdict for ADR-0024 revision 5 with floor R1, R2, R5, R7, R9:** every entry is non-vacuous —
R9 conditionally, per finding 1 — promotion is reachable, and all six properties hold. The open
obligation in §Open obligations is discharged by this run.

**Scope.** Small constants, a single artifact, a single issuance, no veto seats. As with §6 this is
non-vacuity and discrimination, not proof, and it remains a precursor to the `RulesFloor` and
`Promotion` bootstrap modules (ADR-0029 §5).

**A1 note (pending).** R8 closes the break-glass gap in the monotonicity claim (ToO rev 6 §10): today a
break-glass grant, once approved, is not ended by any clause. Four conditions must hold before A1 is ratified.

1. **R8 is vacuous until the mechanism reads the TTL.** This is §6 finding 1 again: an entry bounds only a value
   the mechanism takes from issued rules. No record says a break-glass grant expires, so A1 needs a companion
   mechanism clause, most naturally an amendment to ADR-0013: *a break-glass grant ends at the earliest of its issued
   TTL, the next epoch increment, and a targeted revocation that ends its seat's lease* (ADR-0031 R3; revised
   2026.09.25). The epoch and revocation halves are subtractions, need no clock, and are what the reference already
   does (I9, I5). **That companion touches a privileged path, so it carries C1 and is confirmation-required**,
   whereas A1 itself falls under this record's bootstrap-set exception, as R7 did.
2. **`T_BG` has no archetype value.** It is a floor constant, like R2's 2. It should be derived from `D`, the human
   response budget (ADR-0032 §4), not chosen as a round number. Relaxing it is a degeneracy on this record.
3. **The clock.** ADR-0032 §2 puts a break-glass TTL in the trusted-time row, because a slow clock extends the
   grant. One alternative to decide deliberately, not by default: the mechanism's replicas each read their own
   monotonic clock, and the first to see expiry proposes it as an ordered entry (ADR-0033 §4, I15). Extending the
   grant would then require every replica's clock to run slow. Whether a replica's clock counts as "outside the
   principal" in ADR-0032 §2's sense is a reading of that record, and it is not settled here.
4. **Non-vacuity.** A new dated artifact (the `RulesFloorNV*` files are frozen) with R8 in the floor, a
   reachability witness that break-glass is invoked and then expires, and a mutation dropping R8 that must break
   `P8_BreakGlassEnds`. `RulesFloorBase.tla` carries R8 as reserved and changes with it.

**R3 note.** §6 is not struck. It records what was checked on 09.15 and why R4 was withdrawn, and
§7 says where it no longer describes the mechanism. Two findings in §7 came from the run rather
than from the drafting: R9's conditional independence, and the four-human model being too small to
check R1 once `quorum` became a threshold. The second is the reason §6's own finding 5 matters
beyond bookkeeping.
