# ADR-0037 — The LTRO decomposes into principals: Thinker and Observer are seats, Listener and Reader are stages, and a principal holds one seat

**Status:** Proposed · 2026.09.28
**Status note:** Confirmation required under **C3** (§3 reverses in effect ADR-0018's Reader scope correction: the
Reader stops being a cognitive seat, carries no Persona, and leaves the seat-distinctness predicate) and **C4** (it
resolves ADR-0022's follow-up on seat scoping for authors and ADR-0016's open item on Persona granularity). C1 and C2

## Context

The LTRO has been in the corpus since its first checkpoint. The 09.10 Theory of Operation said "Principals are agent
LTROs", with "three deterministic stages wrapping one nondeterministic agent". No record has decided it since. Its
seat semantics are spread across eight records: ADR-0008 (the Observer and the Reader's placement), ADR-0016 §1 and §5
(seat binding and seat distinctness), ADR-0017 (two lineages across seats), ADR-0018 (the Reader is a cognitive
seat), ADR-0022 (seat scoping, left open), ADR-0024 §5 (grant and veto seats), ADR-0031 §1 (the lease's `seat`) and
ADR-0035 §5 (seat pairs). The 09.16 field assessment (LP-1), the 09.16 critical review and the 09.26 commentary all
name the gap. The commentary calls the LTRO "the one decomposition *inside* a principal" that still has no record.

Leaving it undecided has left four questions open.

**One principal or four?** The narrative says an LTRO is one principal. The accepted text says otherwise in three
places:

- **The lease.** A principal holds at most one live lease, and each lease names one seat (ADR-0031 §1, §2). Read
  literally, a principal occupies one seat per round, so the four positions cannot all belong to one principal at
  once.
- **The evictability test.** "A principal is anything evictable by an authority other than itself" (ADR-0002 A2). A
  targeted revocation of the Observer's Persona ends that seat's lease and nothing else (ADR-0031 R3). The Observer is
  therefore evictable on its own, which makes its holder a principal by the ratified test.
- **The floor.** The draft floor's principal entity type is `Seat` (ADR-0030, schema S).

The reference agrees with the accepted text: it keys lease uniqueness by seat (`live_by_seat`) and uses the principal
string only to match revocations. Only the narrative treats an LTRO as one principal.

**Three seats, two lineages** (CR-10). ADR-0018 made the Reader a cognitive seat with its own Persona and applied
seat distinctness to it. ADR-0017 requires two lineages. The Reader needs the Thinker's logits, so it presumably
shares the Thinker's lineage, and its deny-only interpretation then comes from the family it is watching. Either the
archetype needs three lineages or the Reader's exemption has to be declared. LP-12 is the same tension, seen from the
white-box side.

**Seat scoping is open.** ADR-0022 left "whether all four LTRO seats in Build draw from the whole alphabet or a subset
each" as a follow-up and named the failure: "A Reader with `Draft` authority erases Build's internal separation of
duties." §7 settled it for voters. For authors it is still open. The lease already has the carrier for the answer:
`alphabet` is "the action variants this seat may express". The bound is what is missing.

**"Seat" means two things.** In ADR-0016, ADR-0018 and ADR-0031 it is an LTRO position. In ADR-0024 it is a slot on a
promotion roster, grant or veto, and the reference's `SeatDecl` is that kind: a key and a domain. The two meanings
meet for the Observer, which holds a veto seat, and nowhere else.

**What the Reader and the Listener actually do.** ADR-0018 says the Reader "produces *interpretation*". ADR-0019
gives logprobs exactly two admissible uses. **Decoding conformance** is a decidable predicate that may admit.
**Substitution detection** is a decidable distance, with any threshold on it deny-only. Neither use needs a model.
The Reader's white-box role is a check, and its interpretive role is the Observer's work done from inside the watched
fault domain. The Listener was never given a Persona by any record. ADR-0022 §1 makes "task delivery and corpus
content" environment steps, and ADR-0036 §3 realizes ingress into a guest as node-side `Read`. LP-3 proposed that the
Listener be a deterministic stage. The 09.10 design already made it one.

## Decision

### 1. A principal holds exactly one seat, and the LTRO is not a principal

A **seat** is a slot declared in the issued rules, held by one principal, with a **kind** (§2). **A principal holds
exactly one seat, fixed when the seat is declared.** The lease's `principal` and `seat` fields therefore determine
each other, and ADR-0031 §2's "one live lease per principal" and the reference's "one live lease per seat" become the
same rule.

**An LTRO is a cell**: the principals seated around one Thinker for a round. A cell holds no lease, speaks no
alphabet and cannot be evicted as a unit; it ends when its members' leases end. It names a binding, as a round does.
It is not an identity.

**Separation of duties inside an LTRO is therefore separation between principals**, the only kind the corpus has
machinery for: disjoint leases, disjoint alphabets (§4), a topic per seat (ADR-0021 §2) and attribution per lease.
Nothing new is added to the mechanism.

### 2. Two positions are seats and hold Personas; two are stages and hold none

| Position | What it is | Persona | Principal | Alphabet | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Listener** | Stage | None | No | None | The mechanism's ingress path |
| **Thinker** | Seat of kind `thinker` | Yes; the decoder ships in it (ADR-0018) | Yes | §4 | Inside the guest (ADR-0034 P5) |
| **Reader** | Stage | None | No | None; it produces a check result | May colocate with the Thinker (ADR-0008) |
| **Observer** | Seat of kind `observer` | Yes; distinct from the Thinker's by lineage (ADR-0016 §5) | Yes | §4 | Never colocated with the Thinker (ADR-0008) |

There is a third kind, **`grant`**, for the human grant seats of ADR-0024 §5. It is not an LTRO position; it is here
because every seat has a kind.

**The Listener** is the environment step that delivers the task and the admitted `Read` content to the Thinker. It
has no Persona and makes no choice. At the reference it is the node side of `Read` (ADR-0036 §3), so ingress to the
Thinker is exactly the admitted `Read`s, which ADR-0035 §1 records as `consumed`. It adds nothing to the trusted
base, because the mechanism already admits and records those reads. If source labels are adopted (the 09.16
review's CR-6), this is where they would be attached; this record does not decide that.

**ADR-0016 §1's seat-binding field** takes the values `thinker` and `observer`. No promoted Persona is bound to a
Listener or a Reader, so narrowing the field refuses no existing artifact.

### 3. The Reader is a check, split by what its result is used for

This applies ADR-0018's split to the Reader. Where a check's result may admit, the check is trusted, model-agnostic
and changes at release cadence. Where its result may only deny, the check is untrusted:

- **Decoding conformance** (ADR-0019, use 1) is decidable from the sampled token, the top-k distribution and the
  Persona's declared decoding parameters. **Where its result admits, it sits on the mechanism side of ADR-0018's
  split** and is owed to the TCB declaration record (G4 item 16). Where it only denies, it needs no trust.
- **Substitution detection** (ADR-0019, use 2) computes a decidable distance, and any threshold on it is semantic.
  Its result may only deny or quarantine, so it needs no trust, as ADR-0018's decoder needs none.
- **Interpretation**, the third job ADR-0018 gave the Reader, **is not kept.** A semantic reading of the Thinker's
  output is an Observer finding. A reading made inside the watched fault domain by the watched lineage is the
  finding CR-10 says is worth least, and this record does not keep a seat for it. A deployment that wants more
  detection adds Observer seats under ADR-0008.

**Each cell therefore has two cognitive seats, and ADR-0017's two lineages cover them.** CR-10 closes without a third
lineage and without an exemption. LP-12 closes with it: the white-box role needs no Persona, so it raises no
distinctness question.

**What this reverses.** ADR-0018's Reader scope correction has three bullets. The first (the Reader carries its own
Persona) is reversed. The second (distinctness applies to it) no longer has anything to apply to. The third (Reader
output is deny-only) survives for substitution detection, and ADR-0019 already lets conformance admit. ADR-0018's
statement that the Reader does not perform the typed lift is unchanged. The reversal is C3.

### 4. Alphabets are bounded by seat kind, and the floor states the bound

| Kind | Build | Run (provisional: G4 item 13) |
|---|---|---|
| `thinker` | `Read`, `Draft`, `Invoke`, `Emit`, `Submit` | `RunInvoke`, `RunEmit` |
| `observer` | `Read`, `Emit`; `Vote(deny)` when on the veto roster | `RunEmit` (findings) |
| `grant` | `Read`, `Emit`, `Vote` | None; only Build seats vote |

**Only a `thinker` seat holds a variant that writes, runs or offers**: `Draft`, `Invoke`, `Submit` and `RunInvoke`.
**A `thinker` seat never votes.** A Reader with `Draft` authority, the failure ADR-0022's follow-up names, cannot
arise, because the Reader is not a principal and holds no alphabet.

**The carrier already exists.** The lease's `alphabet` (ADR-0031 §1) is issued at grant, and a renewal may only
narrow it (ADR-0031 §4). What this record adds is the bound, **stated in the policy floor**, so that relaxing it is an
anchor issuance and a promotion, the path ADR-0022 A1 gave recusal:

- Schema S: `Seat` gains `kind: String`, set by the mechanism from the issued rules, like roster membership.
- A new forbid, **`B10-only-thinkers-author`**: `Draft`, `Invoke`, `Submit` and `RunInvoke` are refused when
  `principal.kind != "thinker"`.
- **`B6-vote-seated` is tightened**: a vote is permitted from the grant roster only for a `grant` seat, and from the
  veto roster only for an `observer` seat. A roster issued with the wrong kind of seat on it cannot vote. That is a
  subtraction, and it fails closed. The rules floor (ADR-0026) is not changed.

**The grant path checks the same bound.** It refuses an alphabet outside the kind's row, just as it already refuses
one outside the seat's domain. As with recusal (`mechanism/` I20), the rule is enforced twice: once at grant and once
by the floor at admit.

### 5. Seat distinctness covers one pair per cell

ADR-0016 §5's predicate now has exactly one pair to cover in each cell: (`thinker`, `observer`). Its enforcement is
ADR-0035 §5's, at the promotion tally and the deployment declaration and never at grant, and this record does not
change it. ADR-0035 §5 already names only that pair, so its text agrees with this record and disagrees with
ADR-0018's (finding 5).

**Persona granularity** (ADR-0016, Open). Two `thinker` seats on one Persona are two principals in two cells, and
this is permitted. Attribution is per lease, and a Persona is a property of a lease, not an identity. The correlation
that shared Personas create across cells is the correlator's concern (ADR-0023 §5), not a seat rule.

### 6. The Observer seat: what this record decides and what it leaves

- **It is a principal**, with its own lease, topic and Persona, and it can be evicted alone. Evicting it does not
  evict the Thinker. An empty Observer seat costs different things in the two domains. **In Build**, the promotion
  tally stalls (ADR-0035 §5). **In Run**, nothing stops, because the Observer is deny-only and silence is not a veto
  (the 09.16 review's CR-2). Whether admission should wait on the Observer is **ADR-0039**'s decision. This record
  makes the question expressible: "the cell's `observer` seat holds a live lease" is a lookup, a syntactic fact.
  ⟨R1⟩
- **Its reach into the Thinker is its read authorization on the Thinker's round topic** (ADR-0003's access control).
  The cell is realized by that authorization. No lease field is added, and ADR-0031's closed schema is unchanged.
- **Its only path to effect is a finding** that the mechanism turns into a deny (ADR-0007). The finding crosses into
  the control plane as a fact (ADR-0018, ADR-0004).
- **Placement and scoping are not decided here.** They are **ADR-0038**'s, which supersedes ADR-0008: one
  `observer` seat per cell plus a correlator, ADR-0008's colocation rule carried forward, and the veto roster
  re-homed. ADR-0038 uses the kind this record provides: its correlator is an `observer` seat authorized on many
  topics, with no new kind. ⟨R1⟩

### 7. A human holds one seat, like every principal

A human principal holds a `grant` seat (ADR-0024 §5) or a `thinker` seat (a human author in Build), and never both.
A person who both authors and approves holds two seats and is two principals. Recusal is per seat and does not see
through them (finding 2).

The 09.12 statement "Human principals are atomic" therefore stops being a contrast: every principal is atomic. What
the LTRO gives an agent by default is an Observer seat in its cell. Nothing prevents a deployment from pairing an
Observer seat with a human's `thinker` seat, and that Observer reads the same kind of topic. What a human lacks is
effect exclusivity, which the LTRO never supplied: "the four-way LTRO separation was never what was buying it"
(external assessment §I.6).

### 8. Degeneracy on this record

| Id | Kind | Parameter → deployed value | Premises falsified | Theorems voided | Type changes (ADR-0023 §6b) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D-0037-1** | Declaration | Observer seats per cell: 1 or more → 0 | Every cell has a leased `observer` seat | **No safety theorem**, because the Observer is never in the admit path (ADR-0007). The cell loses its detection claims (the `L` term of ADR-0032 §4's budget) ⟨R1⟩. In Build its artifacts cannot promote (ADR-0035 §5) | No participant changes type |

ADR-0008's shared Observer group is a scoping degeneracy, declared as ADR-0038 D-0038-1, not here. **In Run,
D-0037-1 is usable only together with ADR-0039 D-0039-1**: under coupling, a cell with no Observer makes no admits.
The composition note is ADR-0039 §7's. ⟨R1⟩

### 9. Ratification condition

- **The floor.** Schema S gains `Seat.kind`. `B6` is tightened and `B10` added as §4 states. The floor gates are rerun
  (`floor/run-gates.py`), with results in a dated file under `floor/`. Generated tests show: an `observer`'s
  `Draft` is denied; a `thinker`'s `Vote` is denied; an `observer` on the veto roster may vote deny and not grant;
  and a `thinker` seat issued onto the veto roster cannot vote.
- **A TLC check**, as a module beside `Lease.tla`, with two properties and a mutation that must fail for each:
  - **`OnlyThinkersAuthor`**: no non-`thinker` seat has an admitted authoring variant. Mutation `M_KIND`: the grant
    path ignores the kind bound.
  - **`OneSeatPerPrincipal`**: no principal holds live leases on two seats. Mutation `M_SEAT`: the one-live-lease
    rule is keyed by seat rather than by principal. **`M_SEAT` is the reference's current behavior** (finding 1),
    so the check shows why the reference has to change.
- **The reference** (with an ADR-0033 addendum). `SeatDecl` gains `kind`. `apply_grant` refuses an alphabet outside
  the kind's bound, and refuses a principal that holds a live lease on another seat. A test covers each refusal, and
  the existing suite passes unchanged.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **One principal with four seats**, and a lease per seat | Rejected. ADR-0031 §2 would have to read "per (principal, seat)", which is C3 against an accepted record. It would also leave a principal whose parts are evictable separately, and the evictability test makes each of those parts a principal anyway |
| **One principal, one lease, and `seat` a set** | Rejected. The lease's fields are closed, and a single lease carrying both the Thinker's and the Observer's alphabets carries their union, the whole alphabet. That is the lack of separation the LTRO exists to prevent |
| Keep the Reader as a cognitive seat and require **three lineages** | Rejected. It adds a third unit of the most expensive line item (ADR-0017) for a seat whose independence nothing relies on |
| Keep the Reader as a cognitive seat and **declare it exempt from distinctness** | Not taken. It is admissible, and being deny-only makes it harmless. But it spends a Persona, a promotion and a seat on a function whose admissible logprob uses are decidable, and whose interpretation comes from the lineage it watches |
| Make the Listener a Persona-bearing seat | Rejected. Ingress is an environment step (ADR-0022 §1). A model that decides what the Thinker sees is a second Thinker without an alphabet |
| A `cell` field on the lease, binding each Observer to its Thinker | Not taken. It is a schema change and a mechanism release, and the topic read authorization already expresses the binding. Revisit if CR-2's coupling is adopted |
| Decide Observer-liveness coupling (CR-2) here | Not taken. It is a separate decision, with its own availability and throughput costs: ADR-0039 ⟨R1⟩ |
| Write the ADR-0008 supersession here | Not taken. It can be superseded on its own, and it must also place the correlator and name the veto-roster holder: ADR-0038 ⟨R1⟩ |
| **Split §3 (the Reader) into its own record** | Left to the custodian. §3 passes the decomposition test on its own. It is kept here so that CR-10 closes in the same record that counts the seats |
| Rename one of the two senses of "seat" | Rejected. Every accepted record uses the word. Giving seats a `kind` separates the two senses without editing any of them |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **The LTRO has a record**, and its seat semantics are in one place. LP-1 closes.
- **Separation of duties inside an agent uses existing machinery**: leases, alphabets, topics and the floor. No new
  mechanism.
- **CR-10 and LP-12 close**: two cognitive seats per cell, and two lineages to cover them.
- **ADR-0022's seat-scoping follow-up closes for authors**, as floor text that the gates can check.
- **The human asymmetry is stated precisely.** It is a matter of containment class, not of how many seats a
  principal holds.
- **A future delegation record** (LP-8, CR-3) has principals to attribute each link of a chain to.

**Negative**
- **The record is C3 against ADR-0018.** It is author-ratified until confirmed.
- **The colocated semantic check is gone.** If it had value, that value was low detection latency from inside the
  watched fault domain, and `L` is not measured anywhere in the corpus.
- **An agent is now at least two principals per round**: two grants, two leases and two topics. Grant traffic on the
  consensus log roughly doubles. ADR-0023 §5's per-principal Observers already implied this, and the record makes it
  explicit.
- **One seat per principal makes it matter who stands behind a seat** (finding 2), and no record binds seats to
  persons.
- **Narrative edits are owed**: ToO §2.4, §5.1 and §6, and the glossary's LTRO, Seat, Reader, Listener and Principal
  entries.
- **One author again.** The record was drafted without an options discussion, which is thinner provenance than
  ADR-0034 to ADR-0036 have.

## Findings

1. **The reference enforces ADR-0031's one-live-lease rule per seat, not per principal.** `apply_grant` checks
   `live_by_seat`. The test `one_live_lease_per_principal` passes only because its helper derives the principal from
   the seat (`p-{seat}`). A grant that names a principal who already holds a live lease on another seat is admitted.
   §1 makes the two readings equal, but until the reference checks it they are not (§9).
2. **Recusal is per seat.** Under one seat per principal, a person who holds a `thinker` seat and a `grant` seat
   authors and approves as two principals, and B7 cannot see it. ADR-0026 R5 compares seat keys with anchor keys, but
   nothing compares the persons behind seats. Routed to the seat-registration record (`mechanism/` I8) and to
   ADR-0024's seat-diversity obligation.
3. **ADR-0031 §1 does not say what `persona` holds on a human's lease.** This record makes human seats ordinary
   seats, and so it inherits the question without answering it.
4. **Conformance relies on the guest's own report.** With inference inside the guest (ADR-0034 P5), the logprobs
   come from the occupant's side of the cage. An admitting conformance check (§3) therefore trusts what the guest
   reports, unless the rung attests the inference itself. Routed to ADR-0019.
5. **The corpus had already moved.** ADR-0035 §5 checks only (Thinker, Observer) seat pairs, and its declaration
   check has never covered the Reader. §5 writes down where the later record already stood, as ADR-0023 R3 did for
   composition.
