## Context

**The corpus is written for the people who judge it, and not yet for the people who would act under it.** It has a
Reviewer's Guide and an Adopter's Guide, both drafts. It has a Theory of Operation, a glossary and an architecture
description whose stakeholder table names eight readers. It has no document for an operator, a key-holder, a grant
seat, a break-glass roster or the person seated by one, an erasure roster, or an auditor. The Theory of Operation's
assumption A5 is that "A trained, authorized human is present" for an emergency seat, for operation, for quarantine
release and for the detection interval. No record says what the training is, and nothing exists to train from.

**The custodian asked for a design** ⟨K1⟩ ⟨K2⟩: documentation for operators, anchors, those at the gate and the
rest, listed exhaustively across Build, Run, governance and the outside; read at three levels of disclosure ⟨K3⟩;
and mapped back to the records, the formal specifications and the policy ⟨K4⟩.

**Three things in the corpus shape the answer.**
- **A document that restates a guarantee can misstate it.** The corpus's rule for every layer that explains is that
  it does not decide: "Where the narrative and the current checkpoint disagree, the checkpoint wins"
  (`narrative/README.md`), and "Where a diagram and a record disagree, the record wins" (`diagrams/README.md`). The
  Adopter's Guide holds itself to two rules that make this checkable in part: "Every **must** cites the record
  clause it comes from", and "Every **should** is advice the records do not contain".
- **A summary is where a caveat is lost.** A one-screen statement of what an operator may do is useful only if it
  cannot say more than the record does. Progressive disclosure, done carelessly, discloses the guarantee first and
  the limit three clicks later.
- **Some readers are inside the proofs and some are not.** The Theory of Operation's rule is "A principal is
  anything evictable by an authority other than itself. Whatever is not is at the gate." An operator is bounded by
  the admit path. A key-holder at archetype custody is bounded only by the rules floor. What a wrong document can
  lead each to do differs accordingly, and §11 uses this.

## Decision

### 1. User documentation is derived, and it decides nothing

- **D-1. A unit of documentation states what records, modules, policies and code already state, for a named
  reader.** Where a unit and a record disagree, the record wins, and the state of record is the latest checkpoint.
  The disagreement is a finding against the unit, "whichever layer is right" being the Reviewer's Guide's phrase
  for why it is still reported.
- **D-2. Documentation ranks last.** In the Reviewer's Guide's order of layers (state of record, decisions,
  narrative, evidence, derived) user documentation sits with the derived layer and below the narrative, which it
  may cite and never overrides.
- **D-3. Three kinds of sentence, and each says which it is.** These are the Adopter's Guide's rules, taken for
  every unit:
  - a **must** (or *cannot*, *never*) restates a clause and is bound to it (§5);
  - a **should** is advice the records do not contain, is marked *advice*, and binds nobody;
  - where the corpus supplies nothing and a deployment has to, the unit says "the deployment writes this" and names
    the open item.
- **D-4. A unit does not supply what no record decides.** Where a procedure is owed by a record and unwritten, the
  unit says that no record decides it and cites the open obligation. It does not invent the procedure. §3's last
  column lists where this bites today.
- **D-5. A unit carries the status of what it describes** (§5 B-6), at every level it speaks at.

### 4. Three levels of disclosure ⟨K3⟩

**Every unit is written at three levels, named MI, SI and DI.** The custodian named them by their letters and did
not expand them. The drafter reads them as *main*, *supporting* and *detailed* information *(drafter's reading)*.
This record uses the letters, so that another expansion changes this paragraph and nothing else.

**The pattern is C4's**: a reader starts at a view of the whole and moves to a closer one only when they need it.
C4 has four levels of diagram, and the architecture description already draws three of them. The levels here take
them as follows *(drafter's choice)*.

| Level | The reader's question | Holds | Diagram, at C4's level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MI** | What is this, and what may I do and not do? | The subject in one screen: what the reader's role is here, what the system guarantees them, what it does not, and what they must never do | 1, context: the reader and what they touch |
| **SI** | How does it work, and how do I do it? | The parts the subject passes through and the procedure: steps, preconditions, what is recorded, what cannot be undone | 2 and 3, containers and components; a sequence where order matters |
| **DI** | Exactly what, and on whose authority? | Every action, field, rule, bound and error, each bound to its sources (§5) | 4, code: the clause, the property, the policy rule, the function. A table, not a drawing |

- **LV-1. A unit has all three, and neither MI nor DI is empty.**
- **LV-2. A level may leave out, and it never adds.** Every statement of what the system does, requires or
  guarantees at MI is made at SI, and every one at SI is made at DI, where it is bound. MI is a selection from DI
  and has no claim of its own.
- **LV-3. A limit travels with the guarantee it limits.** Where a level states a guarantee, the same level states
  its status mark, what it assumes, and the residual a record declares for it. A reader who stops at MI has read
  less. They have not read something more favourable.
- **LV-4. Each level answers its own question without the next.** MI is not a teaser for SI.
- **LV-5. A diagram depicts and decides nothing**, as `diagrams/README.md` has it. Each carries a short text
  alternative and a long description (ADR-0071 §8 U-3).
- **LV-6. MI fits one screen** *(advice)*: about 150 words and one diagram.

### 5. Bindings ⟨K4⟩

**A binding ties a statement in a unit to the artifact that makes it true.** It has four parts.

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| **Node** | One of: a record's clause, by record, section and amendment tags; a module and a named property; a proof module or a Lean file; a floor stage or a policy in it; a Rego policy or rule; a crate or a function; a format's schema; a results file; a trusted-base row; a degeneracy; a glossary entry; a checkpoint item |
| **Pin** | The digest of the node's content when the unit was read against it: a clause's digest once ADR-0057 §7's clause pins exist, and the file's until then |
| **Relation** | *decides* (a record clause), *states* (a model), *enforces* (a policy), *implements* (code), *evidences* (a result), *assumes* (a row), *defines* (a glossary entry) |
| **Reader** | Who read the unit against the node, and whether that person drafted the unit |

- **B-1. At DI every statement is bound** ⟨K4⟩. Each statement of what the system does, requires or guarantees
  names the clause that *decides* it. Beside that it names what *states* it in a model, what *enforces* it in
  policy, what *implements* it and what *evidences* it. **Where one of those does not exist, the unit says so**: "no
  module states this", "not built". An absence is written and never left blank. This is ADR-0057 §2's rule for a
  clause's discharge, where "none yet" is "stated as a claim".
- **B-2. At SI every must, cannot and never is bound to its clause** ⟨K4⟩, and every step that is a control action
  names the action and the clause that gives its preconditions.
- **B-3. At MI a binding is owed wherever MI states a guarantee or a limit** ⟨K4⟩, and each unit's MI binds at
  least the record that defines its subject.
- **B-4. A should is bound to nothing and says *advice*** (§1 D-3).
- **B-5. A binding is a pinned citation and not an edge.** ADR-0057 §3 has it that "A citation is not an edge": a
  unit discharges nothing, contributes no premise, and moves no theorem's grade. A binding uses the specification
  graph's node ids where the graph has the node, so that the graph's checker can report stale bindings beside stale
  edges (ADR-0057 §8). The graph today has no node for a narrative file or a guide, and none is needed for this.
- **B-6. Every bound record's status is shown in the corpus's own words**, at each level where the statement is:

  | The record is | The unit says |
  |---|---|
  | Accepted (genesis), no confirmation marker | Nothing more |
  | Accepted (genesis) · confirmation required | "author-ratified" (ADR-0029 §3) |
  | Accepted (genesis) · provisional | "author-ratified, provisional" (ADR-0029 §3) |
  | Proposed | ⟨Proposed⟩: drafted and not in force |
  | Bound through a pending amendment | ⟨pending⟩: drafted and not ratified |

  **A statement's evidence is named in the corpus's words and only of what ran**: proved (TLAPS), checked (TLC, with
  the constants), proved in Lean, tested, built at the reference, not built.
- **B-7. A bound overlay record is marked as an overlay**, so that a reader of Ov-1 does not take it for the
  archetype.

### 6. A unit

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Id** | Stable, and never reused. The document's id and a short name: `Rn-1/revoke` |
| **Kind** | *concept*, *task*, *reference* or *troubleshooting* *(drafter's choice)* |
| **Readers, domain** | From §2 and §3 |
| **MI, SI, DI** | §4 |
| **Bindings** | §5, each attached to the statement it supports |
| **Language** | Declared, with any passage in another marked |

- **U-1. A task's steps are numbered, and each control action says four things**: what it needs (a lease, a peer),
  what the log records of it, whether it adds or denies, and whether it can be undone. For the six operator
  actions these are ADR-0050 §4's columns, bound and not paraphrased.
- **U-2. A unit is the smallest thing revised, released and withdrawn** (ADR-0071 §2, §3).
- **U-3. A unit holds no example taken from a round** (ADR-0071 §10 H-1).

### 7. Staleness

- **ST-1. A binding whose pin no longer matches its node is stale**, and so is the unit, at each level the binding
  supports.
- **ST-2. A stale unit is served and says it is stale**, naming the node that moved. It is not hidden, because the
  procedure it holds may be the only one, and it is not shown as current.
- **ST-3. Only a reading clears it.** A person reads the changed node, brings the unit into line or records why it
  already is, and re-pins. No program re-pins. This is the review ADR-0057 §3 asks of a stale edge.
- **ST-4. A cut view keeps the pins it was cut with** (ADR-0071 §4 V-2). Staleness is a fact about `latest`, and
  about a past view only as a note beside it.

### 8. A release, and what its gates can and cannot check

**A documentation release is a manifest over the unit files at a commit, cut with each checkpoint and whenever the
custodian cuts one between** *(drafter's choice: the narrative's rhythm, "revised against a checkpoint after it is
cut")*. It is a fifth kind of manifest, `documentation`, beside the four `manifests/README.md` lists, and
ADR-0071 §4 makes a view of it.

The gates are programs named by digest (ADR-0069 §6 G-1). Each is mechanical, as ADR-0007 requires of anything that
enables.

| Gate | Passes when | Enforces |
|---|---|---|
| **DG-1 Resolve** | Every binding's node exists at the commit, and the gate has computed which pins match. A release may contain a stale unit only marked as one | §7 |
| **DG-2 Bound** | Every DI statement row has a *decides* binding, and each of its other relations has a binding or a written absence. Every SI sentence with must, cannot or never has a binding | §5 B-1, B-2 |
| **DG-3 Marked** | Each bound record's status, read from its Status and Pending lines at the commit, appears at each level that binds it | §5 B-6; §4 LV-3 |
| **DG-4 Levelled** | Every unit has MI, SI and DI, and MI and DI are not empty | §4 LV-1 |
| **DG-5 Describable** | Every figure has both descriptions, every table its headers, every unit its language | ADR-0071 §8 U-3 |
| **DG-6 Covered** | The coverage report exists: for each reader of §2 and each document of §3, its units, or the word *unwritten*. The gate passes once the report is written, as ADR-0069 §3a's IG-7 does | §3 S-1 |
| **DG-7 Own text** | No unit includes a file from outside the documentation tree | §6 U-3 |

**What no gate checks: that a unit says what its clause says.** DG-2 finds a binding and cannot read it. Whether MI
left out a limit (LV-3), whether a step restates a precondition rightly, and whether a should is really advice are
a person's to judge. So:

- **RL-1. Each unit names who read it against its bindings.** A unit read only by its drafter says so, in the words
  ADR-0029 A2 uses of a record: not read by a reader other than the drafter.
- **RL-2. During genesis a release promotes nothing**, as ADR-0069 §10.3 says of the repository's other checks. It
  is cut by the custodian.
- **RL-3. After bootstrap, whether a release is promoted is open** *(drafter's recommendation: it is not a class
  under ADR-0024 §1, because the mechanism loads none of it; and a unit of Rn-3, Rn-8, Gv-2, Gv-3 or Gv-6 is read
  before release by a member of the roster or key set it instructs)*.

### 9. What exists today

*(Drafter's choice throughout.)* **No file is moved or changed by this record.**
- The two guides become Ex-1 and Ex-2 when they are next rewritten, as units. Until then they stand as they are,
  drafts that `manifests/EXCLUDE` leaves unmeasured.
- The Theory of Operation and the glossary stay the narrative. Units bind to them (Cx-1, Cx-2) and do not replace
  them. The architecture description stays an index.
- The READMEs' instructions for running things are the source of Cx-5.
- Units live in one tree in the repository, one file to a unit. The tree's name and the unit file's form are not
  fixed here (Open obligations).

### 10. A deployment's documentation

A deployment's units are an overlay on these: it keeps, replaces, adds or omits by unit id (ADR-0071 §10 H-3). Where
§3's last column says *the deployment writes this*, the overlay is where it is written. A deployment's replacement
of a unit is its own statement and carries its own reader (RL-1). It inherits no reading from the archetype's.

### 11. Degeneracy and the trusted base

**No degeneracy is declared, and no row is added.** The argument is §2's positions.

- **A reader inside the proofs can be misled only as far as the mechanism admits.** An operator who follows a wrong
  step still signs one of six actions, and each is admitted or refused on the lease, the peer and the floor
  (ADR-0050 §4). A grant seat who misreads a document casts a vote the tally counts as any other. The theorems
  already take these principals as `Mediated` and attributable, and not as correct.
- **A reader at the gate is already assumed to do anything the rules floor allows.** The anchor is "a havoc
  issuer" (ADR-0025's title), and a document cannot widen what havoc reaches.
- **So a wrong document subtracts, or it moves a person within a bound a theorem already assumes.** By ADR-0045
  §2's test it is not a trust root, and no theorem takes a premise from it.

**What this does not make harmless.** A wrong runbook can cost a revocation that should have been made, an
emergency seating that should not have been, or an issuance the floor permits and nobody wanted. Those are inside
the bounds and are still harms. The pattern bounds authority, and it does not make its readers right. A5 stays an
assumption of use. This record gives it something to point at and does not discharge it.

### 12. Ratification condition

- **The readers of §2 are checked against the corpus by someone who is not the drafter**, and every role a record
  names has a row. **Not met. The inventory was made by a delegated reader for this draft and was not re-read

- **At least one document in each of Build, Run, governance and external is written at three levels**, with one of
  them for a reader at the gate and one for a principal. **Not met: no unit is written.**
- **The gates DG-1 to DG-7 are programs in the repository**, and a release has been cut under them, with its

- **LV-2 and LV-3 have been tested by a person on the written units**: for each MI statement, its SI and DI

- **Bindings resolve against the specification graph's node ids** where the graph has the node (B-5). **Not met:
  ADR-0057 is Proposed, and its graph holds no documentation.**
- **A manifest of kind `documentation` is cut**, and `manifests/README.md` lists the kind. **Not met.**
- **The dependent entries of Annex A are drafted.** **Not met: none is drafted.**
- **A reader who is not the drafter** (ADR-0029 A2). **Not met.**
- **The custodian's reading of every drafter's choice, recommendation and reading**, the expansion of MI, SI and DI

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **Every role the corpus relies on has a named document**, and the gaps are a list a person can read (§3's last
  column), in place of the absence of the word "runbook".
- **A summary cannot promise more than its reference** (LV-2, LV-3), and a reader can walk from a sentence to the
  clause, the property, the policy and the code.
- **Where the corpus has not decided, the documentation says so** and points at the obligation, which is pressure on
  the record that owes it.
- **The status of a design not yet confirmed follows it onto every page**, as ADR-0029 §3 already required of any
  publication.
- **A5 has an object.** "Trained" can come to mean "has read these units at this view", once a deployment says so.

**Negative**
- **It is a great deal of writing**, and nearly none of it exists.
- **Three levels for every unit triples the surface that can go stale**, though one set of bindings serves all three.
- **The gates check form.** A unit can pass all seven and be wrong, and RL-1's reader is one more thing the corpus
  does not have.
- **Much of the set would describe Proposed or provisional records.** Operators, emergency seating and the whole
  overlay are in that state, so their documents will be marked from head to foot and will change.
- **Honest documents will read as unfinished.** Gv-2 will say that no record decides the ceremony.
- **The inventory dates.** A-2 makes each new role owe a row, and nothing but a reviewer enforces it until DG-6
  reads the records for roles, which it does not.
