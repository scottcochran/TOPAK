## Context

**Six records owe this record rows, and no two shape a row the same way.** ADR-0034 §6 tabulates the trusted base
per rung, as what enters and what leaves. ADR-0036 §5 lists components trusted at one rung, with a reason each.
ADR-0030 §3 hands over one residual with no table. ADR-0037 §3 hands over a fact about where conformance sits.
ADR-0042 and ADR-0043 hand over one component, the tool host, by what it runs and which side of the front door it is
on. ADR-0023 §6b requires every degeneracy declaration to say what enters or leaves the trusted base, and the field
points at nothing, because no enumeration exists. ADR-0031 §6 supplies the one slice that is mechanically
derivable, the per-round slice in the lease, and says the rest belongs here.

**The corpus already has one rule for what may be trusted without being checked**, and states it in four places.
Bundles can only refuse (ADR-0030 §1). Renewal can only subtract (ADR-0031 §4). The host at rung 3 can only
subtract, which ADR-0034 §5 calls the delegation test and meets by construction. The tally conditions ADR-0024 A2
added can only stall. Each is the same principle: a component whose failure can only take authority away needs no
proof, no quorum and no key, because its failure is a denial. This record states that principle once, for the
trusted base, and adds the one thing the four places did not need: a class for the components whose failure *can*
add authority and which the corpus trusts anyway, because it has fixed their identity by issuance.

**Authority has an exact meaning here.** ADR-0015's authority-change set is exactly promotion and anchor issuance,
and its qualifier is *change to the rules in force*. Everything else that looks like an increase, break-glass
included, happens within the rules in force. So "adds authority" in this record means one of three things: an admit
the rules in force would refuse, a promotion the tally would stall, or an issuance the rules floor would reject.
Nothing else counts, and nothing less does.

## Decision

### 1. A row has a fixed schema

Every entry in the trusted base is a **row**, identified `T-0045-n`, with these fields. The schema is fixed so that
rows are comparable across deployments and across archetype versions (§4), and so that ADR-0023 §6b has ids to cite.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Component** | What is trusted: a program, a device, a person, a store, a value |
| **Layer** | `mechanism-boundary`, `substrate`, `toolchain` or `people` *(drafter's choice: ADR-0031 §6's three, plus ToO §13's people)* |
| **Trusted for** | The premise the theorems take from it, in one sentence. This is the row's **premise node** in the theorem DAG (§3) |
| **Rungs** | The axis and the rung range at which the premise is assumed. `all` for rung-0 rows |
| **Class** | `delegated`, `trust root` or `excluded` (§2) |
| **Failure can produce** | The worst thing the component's failure yields, named as a subtraction (ADR-0031 §5) or as one of the three additions above |
| **Identity fixed by** | For a trust root: the issuance (which ADR-0025 §1 item) or the promotion (which artifact, by digest) that names it. For a delegated row: the deployment declaration. For an excluded row: nothing |
| **Residual** | What remains when the component fails as named, stated on the face. Never empty; "none beyond the class" is a claim, stated as one |

Custody of a component, who holds it and under what loss behaviour, is not a field. It is a pointer to a custody
declaration (ADR-0025's annex, or the deployment's), and the pointer goes in *Identity fixed by*.

### 2. Three classes, and one refusal

**A component is a row only if its failure can only subtract, or its identity is issued.** In full:

- **Delegated.** The component's failure can produce only subtractions: a denial, a lapsed lease, an ended round, a
  revocation, an epoch fence, a stalled promotion, lost evidence. It can never produce an admit the rules in force
  would refuse, a promotion the tally would stall, or an issuance the rules floor would reject. A delegated
  component needs no proof, no key and no quorum. It may be commodity substrate. It is declared per deployment and
  its declaration reopens nothing (ADR-0023 §6a, a declaration degeneracy when it varies). This is ADR-0034 §5's
  test, and every one of the four places in Context is an instance of it.
- **Trust root.** The component's failure can produce an addition, and the corpus trusts it anyway. Admissible only
  if **both** hold: its identity is fixed by an anchor issuance (a key carried under ADR-0025 §1, item (c) or (f), or
  a roster under (a) or (b)) or by a promotion (an artifact named by digest), so that replacing it is itself an
  authority change and consensus-ordered; **and** it entered by a record of its own, which is ADR-0023's
  non-increasing-TCB rule applied. A trust root's residual is what its holder can do while the identity stands.
  ADR-0034 §2's verifier is the model: it decides what counts as Attested, its key is issued, and ADR-0034 is its
  record.
- **Excluded.** A component the deployment relies on in practice and trusts for nothing. The row exists so that
  every theorem premised on it is computed void rather than silently assumed (§3, rule 1). A native tool under
  D-0034-3, a Run tool's declared channel under G19, and the host at rung 0 are excluded rows. An excluded row is
  the honest form of the rung-0 profile's "declared by the operator and verified by nobody" (ADR-0036 R1).
- **Refused.** A component whose failure can add authority and whose identity is fixed by neither issuance nor
  promotion is not a row. It is either inside the verified object, where it is checked and not trusted, or the
  design is changed until its failure can only subtract. §6 applies this to the candidates the prioritization
  named, and refuses one.

*(drafter's choice: three classes rather than two. The excluded class is what makes rule 1 of §3 computable; without
it, a component nobody declared and a component declared untrusted are the same absence.)*

### 3. Rows are premise nodes

Each row's *Trusted for* sentence is a premise node in the theorem-to-premise DAG the 09.27 analysis describes, and
which ADR-0023 §2 already assumes when it computes an inventory as theorems minus those whose premises are lost.
Three rules follow, the first two from that analysis:

1. **An undeclared row is false.** A deployment that has not declared a row sits below every theorem whose premises
   include it. Declaration debt (ToO §7.8) becomes a computed fact.
2. **Type changes are rows.** "The key-holders are at the gate" and "the hypervisor is outside the trusted base for
   guest integrity" are rows, and a degeneracy under ADR-0023 §4a is one that changes a row's class or rung range.
   §6b's field is a list of row ids with the change stated.
3. **Membership per rung is the *Rungs* column.** ADR-0034 §6's "enters" and "leaves" columns are the same
   information read across rungs; Annex A flattens them to one row per component and loses nothing (finding 4).
   The per-round slice of ADR-0031 §6 is derived from the lease and is not repeated here.

The binding table that names, for every theorem, the rows it cites, **exists as `spec/theorem-premise-binding.md`**
⟨R6⟩: one row per checked property, with its row premises, its parameter and gap premises, and the inventory computed
for the reference in its last column. Its versioning belongs to the archetype-evolution record. Until that record
exists, a row's *Trusted for* sentence is still cited in prose by the record that owns the theorem, and the table is
read beside it.

### 4. Changing the table has a direction

- **Removing a row**, a component leaving the trusted base or a trust root becoming delegated, is a tightening: an
  ordinary promotion at deployment level, an archetype version at archetype level.
- **Adding a row**, or moving a row from delegated to trust root, is a relaxation: its own record, and where the row
  names a key, an issuance. This is ADR-0023's carried scope, "moving a property into *assumed* requires its own
  ADR", made checkable. ADR-0034 and ADR-0044 are the records that added T-0045-17 and T-0045-27.
- **A deployment instantiates rows; it does not add them.** Its declaration names which hypervisor, which verifier,
  which store, under the same ids. A deployment that trusts something Annex A does not is a degeneracy under
  ADR-0023 §4a, declared with a D-row that cites the T-row it changes.
- **Annex A carries the archetype version it belongs to**, and the non-increasing-TCB rule is checked row set
  against row set across versions.

### 5. The verified object is the specification, and what runs it is a row

The admit path, the linked floor evaluator, `Authentic`, the tally, the rules floor and the log are what the theorems
are *about*. **As specified**, they are checked, by TLA+ properties with mutations at small constants, by the gates
over the floor, and by the reference's tests, and are not trusted. What the checks *assume* is the rows. The
boundary is drawn where ADR-0030 §2 drew it for the floor: a component is inside the verified object when a
property has been stated over it and a mutation of it fails a check; it is a row when the property takes it as a
premise. The Cedar engine sits on that boundary, and Annex A says how (T-0045-3).

**What runs is not the specification** ⟨R1⟩. ADR-0033's title says the reference is evidence and not the verified
object, and the 09.25 review grades the guarantee delivered today *Empirical*: no refinement from the TLA+ modules
to the Rust exists, TLC checks the modules at small constants, and the build that turns the Rust into a release is
asserted, not reproduced. Each of those is a premise the checks take and do not discharge, each fails the
subtract-only test, since a divergence between code and spec can admit what the spec refuses, and each has its
identity fixed by the release's digest, a promoted artifact. They are therefore trust-root rows, T-0045-28 to
T-0045-30, for every deployment, until a refinement chain exists. **The gap is a debt, not a residual the archetype
keeps** ⟨R2⟩: Moon & Varshney show the chain is buildable, so its absence has an owner, ADR-0033, the record that
made the reference evidence and not the verified object, and the rows leave the trusted base when that record's
addendum cites the chain. The first draft of this section left them out as
"checked", which was the claim of proof the 09.25 review's CR-25 warned against, and the 09.30 comparison with Moon
& Varshney caught (finding 7).

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **ADR-0023 §6b has something to point at**, and "what enters or leaves the trusted base" becomes a list of ids.
- **The delegation test is written once**, and the four places that used it become instances.
- **Eight open gaps become rows or refusals**, each with its residual on the face, instead of build items.
- **Non-increasing TCB is checkable**: row set against row set, under one schema, across versions.

- **One candidate the prioritization delegated is refused**, the solver, and its disposition costs ADR-0030 an

- **One author**, and the author is the assistant drafting at the custodian's request, which is a step further from
  review than the records this one collects from.

## Findings

1. **The solver fails both admissible classes.** ADR-0030's Consequences already said "the solver is trusted" and "a
   solver bug could classify a relaxation as a tightening", and its §5 routes a tightening to an ordinary promotion
   with no issuance. Together those make the solver a component whose failure adds authority and whose identity no
   issuance fixes. Routed to ADR-0030: either the routing check produces a proof certificate a small checker
   verifies inside the verified object, or floor changes become two-key in both directions as ADR-0026 §4 already
   makes rules-floor changes, at which point a wrong "implies" can only block. The 09.28 prioritization's "write the
   row; do not try to verify cvc5" is not available under §2. *Answered by ADR-0030 A1 (Batch 24, 2026.10.01), the
   first way: a proof certificate, checked by a pinned checker at the gate ⟨B24⟩.*
2. **`T_persona` is measured on a local clock.** ADR-0031 A2 bounds the *declared* `ttl`, and the mechanism can
   refuse a longer one. Expiry itself runs on the local monotonic clock with an asymmetric margin (ADR-0032 §2), so a
   slow clock extends the re-attestation interval past `T_persona`, which ADR-0032 §2 classes as preserving
   authority and assigns to trusted time. A2 is honest as a bound on the value; the interval it bounds is
   `T_persona` plus drift. Routed to ADR-0032 and ADR-0031: either the row says so as a residual, or `T_persona`
   joins the break-glass TTL in waiting for a time-source row.
3. **Gate evidence has no signing key.** ADR-0024 §3 binds every vote to a gate-evidence digest, and `mechanism/`
   I18 records that digest from the operator channel with nothing signing it. The gate runner is excluded until
   ADR-0025 §1 (f) carries its key, and a forged "gates passed" is today an operator's assertion at the promotion
   gate.
4. **ADR-0034 §6's two columns are one column.** "Enters at rung *r*" and "leaves compared with rung 1" are the rung
   range of a row read from two ends. Annex A's flattening lost no information; ADR-0036 §5 has no rung column
   because it is one rung, and Annex B instantiates it as such.
5. **Two rows have no theorem yet.** T-0045-21 (inference attestation) and T-0045-26 (conformance where it admits)
   are premises ADR-0037 §3 R3 named before any property cites them. The DAG allows a premise node with no theorem
   above it; the row says so.
6. **ToO §13 already states four rows in prose.** A3 is T-0045-9, A6 is the refused time source, A9 is T-0045-1, A2
   is T-0045-23. The assumptions of use are the people-and-substrate rows read as a checklist, and the ToO's next
   revision can cite the ids.
7. **The first draft of §5 claimed proof where the corpus claims evidence** ⟨R1⟩. It called the admit path, the
   tally and the log "checked, not trusted", which is true of the specification and false of any code that runs it.
   The 09.30 comparison with Moon & Varshney (`derived/`, the TCB comparison) found it: their short trusted list names
   the Dafny verifier and compiler precisely because a checked refinement reaches their deployed code, and this
   corpus has no such chain. Three rows were missing by the record's own rule, and the rule applied to a foreign
   system had sorted its prover, its first-use digests and its allowing guardrails the same way it sorts ours, which
   is the first evidence that §2 is a rule and not a description.
