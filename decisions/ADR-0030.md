# ADR-0030 — Bundles cannot add authority by construction, and the policy floor runs on a verified evaluator

**Status:** Accepted (genesis) · 2026.09.25 · confirmation required (ADR-0029 C1) ⟨B6⟩ ⟨B17⟩
**Status note:** Ratified at R4, in Batch 6 with ADR-0022 A1. Confirmation-required under C1: the floor exceptions
of ADR-0013, and the forensic classes of ADR-0028, are re-expressed as entries in the floor language (§5).
**What is not ratified:** the genesis floor text and schema S (`floor/`), which stay **DRAFT**. This record
ratifies the language, the composition, the input discipline, the gates and the routing, not a floor.
**Debts carried on the face** (ADR-0029 §2), from Open obligations: a record for **Run's alphabet**, without which
the floor's Run half and break-glass's envelope are provisional; the **ToO revision** of §1, §2.4, §3.1 and §11.3;
**solver trust** for routing's "implies" results; the **engine-divergence residual**, owed to the TCB declaration
record; **decoder–alphabet compatibility** under a change to S (ADR-0018); and ~~**bundle signing** (§2), which the
reference does not implement~~ **bundle signing**, discharged in the record by ADR-0044 (Batch 17, 2026.09.30) and
still unimplemented in the reference ⟨B17⟩.
**Deployment position:** Archetype on a new axis, *floor verification*. One degeneracy is declared (§8).
**Depends on:** ADR-0010 (verified object, `bundle ⊑ floor`), ADR-0012 (resident floor), ADR-0007 (gates),
ADR-0018 (validator in the mechanism), ADR-0023 (declaration schema)
**Relates to:** ADR-0013 (exceptions as floor entries), ADR-0028 (forensic classes as floor denials), ADR-0024 §4
(enabling conjunct), ADR-0026 (rules floor, out of scope, §7), ADR-0031 (lease fields as request context), ADR-0003
(wrapper-Rego masking, out of scope, §7)
**Retires:** the claim in ToO §2.4 that refinement is decided by "Rego predicates checked by an SMT solver"
(critical review §2.7)

---

## Context

ADR-0010 made the bundle a parameter admissible only if it refines the floor, and said refinement is decided at
promotion. The ToO says how: "refinement is decided over variants with symbolic parameters (Rego predicates checked
by an SMT solver)." **That tool does not exist, and cannot be built cheaply.** Rego has no mechanized semantics; its
meaning is whatever the Go evaluator does in a given release. A restricted Rego subset — a capabilities file, Regal
rules, signed bundles — makes policies *safe* (no network built-ins, no nondeterminism, bounded cost). It does not
make them *analyzable*: `data` is unbounded JSON, the decision shape is open, and implicit iteration pushes the
questions that matter into quantifiers. Close all three and the result is a smaller Cedar with no proofs behind it.
So the corpus carries an **assumed** property presented as **modeled**, on the path every grant takes.

The critical review (§2.7) named the gap and recommended stating the requirement and naming Cedar as the reference
instantiation. The 09.17 sessions went further and found that the gap does not need to be closed. It can be removed.

**Two observations carry the record.**

1. **Refinement can be a property of composition rather than of the bundle.** If the mechanism admits only what the
   floor permits *and* the bundle does not deny, then no bundle can add authority, whatever its text says.
   `bundle ⊑ floor` stops being a cross-language check at promotion and becomes a mechanism constant. This is R4's
   move (a timeout never grants) applied to refinement.
2. **Once no check is needed between the tiers, the floor alone needs a verified evaluator**, and a language exists
   with one: Cedar's semantics are mechanized in Lean, its symbolic compiler to SMT is proven sound and complete
   against that model, and its engines are differentially tested or conformance-tested against the same model. What
   Cedar buys the floor in particular is the **non-vacuity gate** that §3.1 and §11.3 say is missing. It would be
   the corpus's first gate with a proof behind it rather than a reachability witness.

**The sessions arrived here from the language side**, without the ToO in view, and re-derived the policy floor: an
upper bound on what the system can do, subtract-only composition beneath it, no business rules in it, and the rule
deciding which decisions may consult an unverified tier held inside the verified one. That is independent
confirmation of ADR-0010's shape, and it is why this record changes the floor's **language** and leaves its
**role** alone.

## Decision

### 1. Composition is monotone, and it is a mechanism constant

For every admit decision,

> **admit ⟺ floor_permit ∧ ¬bundle_deny**

evaluated by the mechanism over one frozen input (§4). Fail-closed on both sides:

- **`bundle_deny` holds** when the bundle denies, errors, returns undefined, is absent, or cannot be reached. A
  bundle is never asked whether to grant, only whether to refuse.
- **A bundle decision counts only if it comes from exactly the bundle revision the lease names** (ADR-0031 §1
  `bundle`) ⟨R4⟩. Where the tier runs two engines, a sidecar and a local engine:

  | The sidecar… | Result |
  |---|---|
  | returns `true` from the lease's revision | Allow |
  | returns anything else from the lease's revision | **Refuse. Authoritative:** no fallback |
  | is unreachable, errors, times out, or serves another revision | The local engine decides **iff** it holds the lease's revision |
  | — and the local engine holds no such revision | Refuse: the bundle "cannot be reached" |

  Without the binding, a sidecar outage would silently lift every narrowing pushed after the local copy was built,
  which is §9 finding 3 arriving by another route. Every admit records which engine decided. Bundle identity is a
  content revision, a digest over the bundle's sources, until signing (§2) binds it to a signature. Folded from
  ADR-0033 §5.
- **A floor deny names its reason** ⟨R4⟩: the forbids that fired, or **no permit matched** when none did. Cedar
  reports determining policies only for forbids, so a deny outside every envelope carries no reason from the
  engine, and the mechanism must supply one. Without it, most denials answer "why?" with nothing, and the Observer
  and ADR-0020's replay read a record that cannot tell *outside every envelope* from *refused by a named rule*
  (ADR-0033 §7, finding 10).
- **`floor_permit` holds** only when the floor engine returns Allow **with no evaluation errors**. Cedar skips a
  policy that errors, so a `forbid` that errors does not fire. The mechanism therefore treats any floor error
  diagnostic as a deny, whatever decision the engine returns.
- **A floor deny short-circuits.** The bundle is consulted only on a floor permit.

**Consequences for ADR-0010's relation.** `bundle ⊑ floor` now holds of the composed decision for every bundle, at
every age, so it is no longer something a bundle can fail. A bundle whose text permits more than the floor is inert
there, not inadmissible. ADR-0010's decision text stays true: refinement is still discharged at promotion, now by
construction. ADR-0024 §4's enabling conjunct is discharged the same way. Under the amendment test neither record
changes (the same disposition as S6-16).

**What is given up.** A bundle cannot establish that something is allowed. `always_allows` over the composed
decision is not provable. Over-denial by a bundle is a modeled, attributed local event (`deny=all` is F2 under
ADR-0028), so the pattern loses nothing it was claiming.

### 2. The O letter names a role, and the floor tier has a stated requirement

The O is *policy as code* as a role. Substitution is by role obligation, so the obligation is written out.

| Tier | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **Floor** | An evaluator whose semantics are mechanized; an implementation proven against, differentially tested against, or conformance-tested against that semantics; a **decidable** comparison of two policy sets (implies, equivalent, always-allows, always-denies, never-errors) over a declared schema; resident and linked into the mechanism (ADR-0012); minimal |
| **Bundle** | Signed; evaluated per request over syntactically lifted input; the three ADR-0007 gates. Any per-request engine discharges it, because the mechanism does the composition |

**Reference instantiation: Cedar for the floor, OPA/Rego for the bundle tier.** A deployment that already runs OPA
keeps it on the bundle tier without degeneracy. A floor written in the mechanism's own verification language (a
policy inside the verified artifact, Moon & Varshney's shape) also meets the floor obligation and is an admissible
archetype instantiation. Each floor change then requires a re-proof.

### 3. The floor is linked into the mechanism, not served

The floor engine is a library inside the mechanism release, and the floor text and schema ship in that release. **No
process, no socket, no fetch.** ADR-0012's residency argument settles this. It also gives the isolation property the
sessions had to build a guard service to get:

- **The floor's inputs are written only by the typed lift and the issued rules.** No bundle has a write path to
  any value the floor reads. If a bundle could inject state, the floor's analyses would describe entity stores that
  the running system does not have.
- **Nothing the floor evaluates depends on bundle output.** The dependency is one-directional, so the floor can be
  analyzed alone.

**Engine choice goes by the length of the assurance chain, not by language.** The Rust reference engine is
differentially tested against the Lean model; `cedar-go` is checked against a conformance corpus generated from the
formal specification. If the mechanism can link the Rust engine, that is the shorter chain. The engine and its
version are pinned in the mechanism release. **The residual** — engine divergence on inputs outside the conformance
corpus — is stated in the TCB declaration.

### 4. The schema is the typed lift's type, and it is enforced at request time

The analyses quantify over **all requests and all entity stores satisfying schema S**. A proof about a schema the
running system does not enforce proves nothing about the running system. So:

- **S is enforced at the decision point on every request**, by the model-agnostic validator of ADR-0018. An input
  that does not conform is a deny. Build-time use of S is not sufficient.
- **The entity slice the lift supplies is complete** for every entity the floor can reference. An incomplete slice
  makes attribute access error, and §1 turns that into a deny rather than into a skipped `forbid`.
- **The input is resolved once and frozen.** Floor and bundle evaluate the same input. The bundle tier may not
  re-fetch attributes after the floor has decided, or the floor's decision covers a request that no longer exists.
  **A bundle's own `data` is not a re-fetch** ⟨R1⟩**:** tables, allowlists and thresholds shipped inside the signed bundle
  are part of the bundle, and a rule may use them freely. What is barred is resolving facts *about the request*
  anew. A rule that needs one gets it through the lift, which then supplies it to both tiers.
- **S changes only with a mechanism release**, because it ships with the floor. A floor analysis is evidence only
  for the schema version it names.

The PARC fit is direct:

| Cedar | TOPAK |
|---|---|
| Principal | Seat identity (ADR-0016, ADR-0031 `seat`) |
| Action | Build's six variants (ADR-0022) or Run's typed alphabet — finite in variants, as §2.4 already requires |
| Resource | The action's syntactic target: ref, workspace target, tool name and digest, topic, artifact digest |
| Context | Epoch, `fence`, `rulesEpoch`, round, and the lease fields the floor needs (ADR-0031) |

**Epoch fencing stays in the mechanism**, ahead of the floor, as ADR-0022 §2 already requires. The floor never
decides whether an epoch is current.

### 5. The gates on the floor become decidable checks

ADR-0007's gates run on the floor (ADR-0010 tangential 2). With a verified evaluator each becomes a query with a
proof-backed answer, and a counterexample request when it fails:

| Gate | Check over S, per action |
|---|---|
| **Totality** | Validation against S, and **never-errors** over the whole floor |
| **Non-vacuity** | **Not always-allows**: the floor refuses some conforming request. A floor that permits everything admits every bundle and discharges every property over nothing |
| **Discrimination** | Non-vacuity **and not always-denies**: the floor both admits and refuses. An action class the floor always denies is declared as such, never left implicit |

**Floor revisions are classified mechanically.** Between the deployed floor F and a candidate F′:

| `F′ ⇒ F` | `F ⇒ F′` | Classification | Authority (ToO §3.2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| holds | holds | Equivalent | Ordinary promotion |
| holds | fails | Tightening | Ordinary promotion |
| fails | holds | Relaxation | Anchor issuance **and** promotion |
| fails | fails | **Incomparable: routed as a relaxation** | Anchor issuance **and** promotion |

This removes a reviewer's assertion from a spot where Build principals, who are Byzantine by premise, choose the
authority path for their own change. A Build author cannot relabel a relaxation as a tightening, because the
classification is computed.

**Exceptions and forensic classes are floor entries in the floor language.** The break-glass and erasure entries
(ADR-0013) and the six forensic classes, each a denial on the erasure envelope (ADR-0028), are written as Cedar
policies inside the floor. Composition applies to them uniformly, so **a bundle can narrow a floor exception,
including break-glass**. That is ADR-0028 §2's rule ("bundles can only narrow erasure further") carried across both
exceptions. Its cost is liveness: a bad bundle can disable break-glass. It cannot widen either exception.

**The analysis results are gate evidence.** The schema version, the checks run, the classification, and the
analyzer, solver and engine versions go into the gate-evidence digest that votes already bind to (ADR-0024 §3). No
new primitive is needed.

### 6. No transpiler, in either direction

Neither Rego → Cedar nor Cedar → Rego is part of the archetype. A transpiler is a Build artifact whose output
becomes the enforced floor. Build is where the Byzantine threat lives, and a silent mistranslation is exactly the
failure this record exists to rule out: the proven text is not the enforced text. Running the floor natively removes
that Build step from the TCB. Cedar's analyses compare Cedar with Cedar, so no translation is needed anywhere.

### 7. What this record does not decide

- **The rules floor.** ADR-0026's entries bound issuances, not admits. Some would read naturally as Cedar forbids
  over an issuance-as-request (R1, R5, R7). R6 bounds the floor's own exception count, a property of the policy set
  rather than of any request, so Cedar cannot state it, and every entry depends on a roster the mechanism reads. The
  rules floor stays mechanism-checked. Moving any entry is a separate record.
- **Global versus per-class floors** (ToO §3.4). Cedar scopes policies per action, so either answer can be stated.
  This record does not choose.
- **Sequence and flow properties** (CR-6). Cedar decides one request at a time, as Rego does, so this record is
  neutral there. Provenance labels as lifted fields would be Cedar attributes, so the two fixes compose.
- **Wrapper-Rego masking** (ADR-0003). Masking transforms a payload and makes no admit decision, so the
  composition does not reach it.
- **The bundle tier's language.** Cedar for both tiers is admissible and trades expressiveness (no aggregation, no
  regex) for one language. OPA is the reference because deployments already run it.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Keep a Rego floor with refinement decided by SMT (the current ToO text) | Rejected. It needs a mechanized Rego semantics and an analyzer proven sound against it, and both would have to be revalidated at every OPA release |
| A restricted Rego subset as the floor | Retained only as **D-0030-1** (§8). It delivers safety and exhaustive testing over a bounded schema, not proof |
| Refinement *decided* at promotion, bundle against floor | Rejected. It is a cross-language check nobody has, and §1 makes it unnecessary |
| Rego → Cedar transpiler, with differential testing | Rejected (§6). The translator enters the TCB, and "is this policy in the subset?" has to be decided by hand |
| Cedar → Rego transpiler, keeping OPA as the only runtime | Rejected (§6). Better than the reverse, but it becomes unnecessary once the floor runs natively, and it keeps the translator in the TCB |
| The floor as a separate guard service in front of the bundle engine | Rejected (§3). A process and a socket are a fetch path ADR-0012 forbids, a bypass surface during restarts, and a second availability dependency |
| An unreachable bundle is a deny, with no engine fallback (as first drafted) ⟨R4⟩ | Admissible and simpler. Not taken: a fallback bound to the lease's revision loses no narrowing and keeps admits flowing through a sidecar restart (ADR-0033 §5) |
| A local bundle fallback not bound to the lease's revision ⟨R4⟩ | Rejected. It lifts every narrowing pushed after the local copy was built |
| Recusal kept out of the floor, as a mechanism constant only ⟨R4⟩ | Rejected (§10 finding 3). The floor's analysed `Vote` envelope would permit author votes, wider than what is enforced, and recusal would sit outside the gates |
| Permit-override composition (the bundle may grant within its own scope) | Rejected. A bundle able to add authority is ADR-0010's unconstrained parameter. It is not a degeneracy on this axis: it removes the floor |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **An assumed property becomes a mechanism claim.** Refinement no longer rests on a tool that does not exist. This
  is ADR-0015's move for effect exclusivity, applied to refinement.
- **Floor non-vacuity becomes proven**, closing the silent failure §3.1 ranks as having the widest blast radius. It
  is the first gate in the corpus backed by a proof rather than a witness.
- **Floor-change routing is computed, not asserted** (§5). Incomparable goes the expensive way, by default.
- **Floor tightening never invalidates a promoted bundle**, and the bundle gate reduces to the three ADR-0007 gates
  plus signing.
- **Stale bundles stay monotone-safe** under partition, now with no refinement premise at all.
- **The article can answer "why OPA?"** by stating a requirement, with a shipped precedent for the choice (Amazon
  Bedrock AgentCore's policy layer uses Cedar for tool-call authorization on analyzability grounds) and a deep
  lineage for the shape (mandatory access control over discretionary, stacked seccomp filters, service control
  policies, Simplex, shielding).

**Negative**
- **Two policy languages and two engines.** Floor authors write Cedar. That is the cheaper side of the trade only if
  the floor stays small, which ADR-0010 already requires for other reasons.
- **The TCB changes shape.** Entering: a pinned Cedar engine, the floor text, the schema. In the Build toolchain the
  analyzer is sound by proof, **but an "implies" answer is an UNSAT result from the solver, and the solver is
  trusted.** A solver bug could classify a relaxation as a tightening. Leaving: the unstated assumption that a
  Rego-to-SMT path exists.
- **The floor must fit PARC.** No aggregation, no regex, no computed data. Policies that need them belong in the
  bundle tier, where they can only subtract, or in the Observer as detections.
- **A bundle can disable break-glass** (§5). That is liveness, not safety, but it is a new way for a bad bundle to
  hurt during an incident.
- **This changes the verified object's language**, so every analysis, witness and non-vacuity result about the
  floor has to be redone in the new form once a floor text exists.

## 8. Declared axis: floor verification

A new axis under ADR-0023 §5. Entries follow ADR-0024's degeneracy table. The §6 schema fields are below it.

| Record | Rule | Premise falsified | Sacrificed | Gained |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A-0030** | Floor on a verified evaluator (§2); monotone composition (§1) | — | — | — |
| **D-0030-1** | Floor in Rego, with tests over a bounded schema and `RulesFloorNV`-style witnesses | The floor's evaluator is faithful to a mechanized semantics | Floor non-vacuity, never-errors and revision classification as **proven** properties; they become **measured** | One engine and one language, where the deployment already runs OPA |

**D-0030-1, declaration schema** (ADR-0023 §6)

- **Kind: mechanism degeneracy.** The floor's evaluator is inside the verified object.
- **Theorems voided:** none of the containment theorems. **`bundle ⊑ floor` survives**, because §1's composition
  is a mechanism constant and does not depend on the floor's language. The DCKLP session's rung table lists it as
  lost; with composition in the mechanism it is not. What is lost is the ability to prove that the floor text
  *means* what its reviewers read it to mean.
- **Type changes:** none. No participant changes type.
- **Mitigation:** exhaustive evaluation over a bounded schema; a capabilities file removing network, time and
  randomness built-ins; Regal rules; a reachability witness for each gate.
- **Composition note:** required above one parameter. With floor residency (ADR-0012) degraded as well, a remote
  Rego floor loses both proven gates and local enforcement under partition. No further delta is known. This is a
  claim, not a check.

## 9. Property check — run 2026.09.24 ⟨R2⟩

**Artifacts:** `spec/FloorComposition.tla` · digest `3d30a058d8f8d9d1` and `spec/FloorRouting.tla` · digest
`9d4c0f5e03defc1e` · TLC2 2.19 · raw results in `spec/FloorComposition-results-2026.09.24.txt` · driver
`spec/floor-run.sh`.

**Model, composition.** Two seats, two targets, three actions. The floor is a four-policy Cedar-shaped set — permit
`Read`; permit `Draft` and `Emit` to the resource's owner; forbid `Emit` to a sealed resource — evaluated with
Cedar's rules, including **a policy that errors is skipped**. The bundle is havoc: at every decision it may return
allow, deny, error, undefined, absent or unreachable, which covers every bundle at once. The lift writes the
floor's store and a ghost copy. The slice handed to the engine may drop an attribute — the fault §4 forbids,
modeled so that §1 is checked without §4's help. `EnvelopeHolds` states what the floor's analysis would prove, against
what the lift actually wrote.

**Model, routing.** A floor is its meaning, the set of requests it permits, over a three-request universe; a
proposal is any subset, since the author is Byzantine; implies is inclusion. This checks the routing, not the
analyzer — it assumes the implies answers are right, which is the solver-trust residual stated in Consequences.

| Module | Check | Mut | Invariant | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Composition | Full mechanism | none | `Safe` (all five) | **holds** |
| Composition | Reachability — something is admitted | none | `NeverAdmitted` | counterexample ✓ |
| Composition | Reachability — **a bundle refuses what the floor permits** | none | `NeverNarrowed` | counterexample ✓ |
| Composition | Reachability — the floor errors | none | `NeverFloorError` | counterexample ✓ |
| Composition | Permit-override composition | `M_OVERRIDE` | `AdmitImpliesFloorPermit`, `EnvelopeHolds` | counterexample ✓ both |
| Composition | Engine decision taken, errors ignored | `M_ERR` | `FloorErrorIsDeny`, `EnvelopeHolds` | counterexample ✓ both |
| Composition | Bundle error, undefined, absent or unreachable is not a deny | `M_FAILOPEN` | `BundleRefusalHonored` | counterexample ✓ |
| Composition | The same | `M_FAILOPEN` | `EnvelopeHolds` | **holds** (finding 3) |
| Composition | Bundle tier can write a floor input | `M_WRITE` | `BundleCannotWriteFloorInputs`, `EnvelopeHolds` | counterexample ✓ both |
| Routing | Full mechanism | none | `Safe` (both) | **holds** |
| Routing | Reachability — an incomparable proposal, a tightening, an issued relaxation | none | three witnesses | counterexample ✓ each |
| Routing | Only a strict superset counts as a relaxation | `M_ROUTE` | `NoWideningWithoutIssuance` | counterexample ✓ |
| Routing | Non-vacuity gate off | `M_GATE` | `FloorNeverVacuous` | counterexample ✓ |

### Findings

1. **The `M_ERR` counterexample is the hazard §1 names, in three steps.** A target is sealed; its owner emits to it
   through a slice missing `sealed`; the forbid errors and is skipped, the owner permit fires, and the emit is
   admitted to a sealed target. An engine used as Cedar documents it — decision taken, diagnostics logged — admits it.
2. **§1 makes §4's slice-completeness a liveness requirement, not a safety one.** Under the full mechanism the slice
   may drop any attribute and `EnvelopeHolds` still holds: an incomplete slice costs denies, never a breach. §4's
   requirement stands, because a mechanism that denies on every incomplete slice is unusable, but a lift defect
   cannot widen authority.
3. **Bundle fail-closed protects the bundle, not the floor.** With `M_FAILOPEN` the envelope still holds; what fails is
   `BundleRefusalHonored`. So the §1 rule that an erroring or unreachable bundle counts as a deny is not what keeps
   the floor sound — composition does that. What it protects is **every narrowing an operator has put in the
   bundle**: without it, a bundle outage during an incident silently lifts an incident `deny` and the system runs at
   the floor. That is the case for keeping it, and the reason it is a mechanism constant rather than a bundle
   convention.
4. **The narrowing witness is reachable**, so `AdmitImpliesFloorPermit` is not holding because the two tiers never
   disagree.

**Scope.** Small constants, one floor text, havoc bundles, and a routing model that trusts its implies answers. It is
a discrimination test of the composition and routing rules, not a check of Cedar, SymCC or any real floor. A floor in
Cedar and its schema are still required before §5's gates run on anything.

## 10. Gate run on the draft genesis floor — 2026.09.24 ⟨R3⟩

**Artifacts:** `floor/genesis-floor.cedar` · digest `9b6adeee0c2ae308` (14 policies) and `floor/genesis.cedarschema` ·
digest `77d9b566d76134c3`, both DRAFT · `cedar-policy-cli` 4.13.0 built with `analyze` · cvc5 1.4.0 · results in
`floor/genesis-gates-results-2026.09.24.txt` · driver `floor/run-gates.py` · 38 behavioral cases in
`floor/genesis-tests.json`, all passing.

**What the floor contains.** Six Build envelopes from ADR-0022 §1; three carve-outs (author recusal, grant only from
a grant seat, voters do not author); two Run envelopes, **provisional** because no record defines Run's alphabet;
the two exceptions of ADR-0013 (break-glass over `RunInvoke` only, erasure under a quorum); and the six forensic
classes of ADR-0028 as one forbid on the erasure envelope. Not in it: epoch fencing, the round bound, the rules floor,
and `Invoke` hermeticity, each for the reason given in the file header.

| Gate (§5) | Check, per action signature | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Totality | `never-errors`, each policy on each action it covers (16) | **16 verified** |
| Non-vacuity | `always-allows` refuted (9 signatures) | **9 refuted** — each with a counterexample request |
| Discrimination | `always-denies` refuted (9) | **9 refuted** |
| Every entry matters | floor minus one policy not `equivalent` to the floor (14) | **14 differ** — no dead entry |
| Routing | a tightening, a relaxation (F1 made erasable), an incomparable revision | **classified correctly**; the last two route to anchor issuance + promotion |
| Negative controls | a permit-all on an uncovered action; a policy that can overflow; a duplicated forbid | **3 caught** |

### Findings

1. **The gates are cheap.** The whole suite, including the controls, runs in seconds. Running them at every
   promotion that touches the floor costs nothing worth weighing.
2. **Non-vacuity is per action, and forbids carry it.** The first negative control failed as written: a permit-all
   added to the floor is *not* vacuous on `Vote`, because recusal and the grant-seat forbid still refuse requests.
   It is vacuous on `Read`, which no forbid covers. A single floor-wide non-vacuity answer would hide which action
   classes a floor actually bounds. §5's "per action" is the right granularity, and this is why.
3. **Recusal in the floor changes its authority path.** ToO §2.4 and ADR-0022 §7 say relaxing recusal is "a mechanism
   release, not an issuance." Stated as a floor forbid (B7), relaxing it becomes a floor relaxation: an anchor
   issuance *and* a promotion (§5's routing classifies it so). That is stricter, so it is monotone-safe, but it
   contradicts the corpus's stated path. ~~**Open:** keep recusal in the floor and amend that sentence, or keep it in
   mechanism code outside the floor and drop B7.~~ **Resolved 2026.09.25** ⟨R4⟩: recusal stays in the floor as B7,
   and ADR-0022 §7's sentence is amended to name the floor path (ADR-0022 A1, ratified with this record in Batch 6). **The principle is general:**
   every ADR-0022 envelope and carve-out stated in the floor — B8 (grant only from a grant seat) and the
   voters-do-not-author carve-out included — is floor text, so relaxing it is a floor relaxation routed by §5, and
   tightening it an ordinary promotion. Recusal is the only one whose path an accepted record had stated otherwise.
4. **Run's alphabet has no record.** ADR-0022 asserts one exists; ADR-0015 gives only its two effect classes. The
   floor cannot bound Run properly until a record names the variants, and break-glass's envelope is stated over a
   provisional action. This is a gap in the corpus, not in the floor.

**Scope.** A draft floor, checked against a draft schema. The analyses are proofs over S — every request and every
entity store S admits — with the solver trusted for each "verified" answer. They say the text is well-formed,
non-vacuous and minimal. They do not say it is the *right* floor; that is floor fitness, still unowned (ToO §12).
