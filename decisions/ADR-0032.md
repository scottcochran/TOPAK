# ADR-0032 — The clock model: clocks buy liveness, and intervals are trusted by failure direction

**Status:** Accepted (genesis) · 2026.09.23 ⟨B5⟩
**Status note:** Confirmation required: no. None of C1–C4 applies — no proof obligation over privileged
paths, no exhaustiveness or confinement claim, no reversal of an accepted record. The `W < L < D` demotion
(§4) touches ADR-0021, which is Proposed, so it is an in-place revision there rather than a reversal.
**Deployment position:** Archetype. The clauses hold on every rung; the budgets in §4 are declared per deployment.
**Depends on:** ADR-0007 (placement), ADR-0022 (epoch and lease on every action), ADR-0031 (lease schema)
**Relates to:** ADR-0003 (round topics), ADR-0005 (causal frame), ADR-0009 (break-glass), ADR-0024 (vote timeout),
ADR-0026 (R8 candidate), ADR-0016 (Persona expiry)
**Discharges:** ADR-0031 §7; ADR-0024's clock-model open obligation; boundary register §9.1
**Corrects:** the `L` / `D` letter drift (§5)

---

## Context

Leases expire, epochs fence, votes time out, and `W < L < D` is quoted in the article material — and **no record
states a clock assumption anywhere.** Bounded drift, monotonic versus wall-clock, NTP step behavior, and what a
backwards jump does to a fenced epoch are all unaddressed. The boundary register called this "the standard hole in
this class of design," cheap to close and embarrassing to be caught on.

The 09.17 sessions opened it and it split four ways (N17-1). The most useful result was the first: **most of what
looked like a clock problem is not one.**

---

## Decision

### 1. Clocks buy liveness, never safety

**Epoch fencing carries safety without reading a clock.** An action under a stale epoch is rejected by the
mechanism because the epoch does not match, not because a timer expired. An expired lease that its holder still
believes in is therefore **not a containment violation**: the fence rejects it whatever the holder's clock says.

It follows that **clock misbehavior costs availability or detection lag, and never containment.** A clock that runs
fast evicts early and subtracts authority. A clock that runs slow delays an eviction the fence would still catch.
Neither admits an action the envelope forbids.

This is the clause that makes the rest of this record small. Everything below is about liveness budgets and
trusted intervals, not about whether the containment theorems hold.

### 2. Intervals are trusted by failure direction

| If the interval's failure… | Then it may use | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Subtracts** authority | a **local monotonic clock**, read with an asymmetric margin | lease expiry, passive e-stop, vote timeout |
| **Adds or preserves** authority | **trusted time**: a signed, attested time source outside the principal | break-glass TTL, Persona expiry, re-attestation intervals |

**The margin is asymmetric and fails closed.** An interval that subtracts is treated as elapsed early rather than
late, so clock error removes authority rather than extending it. This is the direction ADR-0031 §7 already
declared for lease expiry, generalized.

This is the monotonicity principle on the temporal axis — its seventh appearance, after grants and denials, floor
refinement, authority direction, deployment variation, the forensic floor, and lease renewal.

**The consequence for R8.** A break-glass grant TTL preserves authority until it fires, so by this rule it needs
trusted time. That is a real cost and it is the reason R8 has stayed a candidate entry rather than a floor entry;
the cost is worth naming rather than discovering at implementation.

### 3. No safety decision reads a clock supplied by a principal

Time enters the mechanism the way everything else does under ADR-0007: from the mechanism side of the boundary. A
principal may report a time; nothing admits on it. A timestamp inside a payload is cognitive content, not a
control-plane fact.

### 4. `W + L + D < T_irrev` is the property; the ordering is not

For each effect class, the deployment declares **`T_irrev`**, the time from emission until the effect becomes
irreversible. The safety budget is:

> **`W` + `L` + `D` < `T_irrev`**

| Term | Meaning | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| **`W`** | Effect propagation window, about a second at the archetype | **Guaranteed by the mechanism** |
| **`L`** | Observer detection latency | **Emergent property of a component** — and **unmeasured** |
| **`D`** | Human response budget | **Organizational commitment** |

**The three terms are of three different kinds**, which is why the earlier framing — "specification invariant or
deployment obligation?" — was a false dichotomy. `W` is proven, `L` is measured, `D` is promised. A statement that
mixes them is not one kind of claim and cannot be discharged in one way.

**The ordering `W < L < D` is demoted to a descriptive observation** about relative magnitudes. It was never a
safety property: nothing is made safe by detection being slower than propagation. Citations that use the chain as
a guarantee are to be corrected to the sum.

**When the budget cannot be met.** If `T_irrev` is smaller than the sum — in the limit, an effect that is
irreversible on emission — no budget satisfies it, and the conclusion is not a tighter clock. **The effect must not
be reachable from the alphabet at all.** This is where the timing argument rejoins ADR-0007: the fix for an effect
too fast to stop is to make it inexpressible, not to detect it faster.

**`L` is the weak term.** It is unmeasured, and the register notes it structurally cannot acquire a guarantee,
because detection is an emergent property of a component nobody verified. Until `L` is measured, the budget is a
form with one unknown in it.

### 5. `L` and `D` mean what they have meant since 2026.09.12

The letters drifted. **Before 09.12**, `L` was lease duration and `D` was detection lag. **From 09.12 onward**,
`L` is Observer detection latency and `D` is the human response budget. **The 09.12-onward meanings stand.**

Any citation of `W`, `L`, or `D` that predates 09.12 carries the older meanings and must be read accordingly or
corrected. The glossary records the drift; this record rules it.

### 6. What this record does not decide

- **A drift bound.** No numeric bound on clock drift is stated here, because §1 makes safety independent of it and
  §2 routes authority-preserving intervals to trusted time. A deployment that wants a liveness guarantee, rather
  than a liveness expectation, declares a drift bound as a degeneracy parameter under ADR-0023.
- **Wall-clock behavior.** Monotonic clocks are required for the intervals in §2's first row. What a wall-clock
  backwards jump does is out of scope for the same reason: it cannot reach containment.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| A global synchronized-clock assumption | Rejected. It would put a timing assumption into the containment argument, which §1 shows is unnecessary — and it is the assumption that fails quietly in production |
| One clock treatment for all intervals | Rejected. It either overpays for lease expiry or underpays for break-glass TTL. The failure direction is the discriminator |
| Keep `W < L < D` as the safety property | Rejected (§4). It mixes a proof, a measurement, and a promise, and the ordering carries no safety content |
| Derive timing from ADR-0005's vector clock | Rejected. Causality orders events and bounds no interval, so `W + L + D` cannot be discharged from it. The 09.15 session had already moved the causal frame into the Paxos log |
| A UPPAAL timed automaton for the lease module | **Deferred**, not rejected. It is the right tool if the liveness budget ever needs proving rather than declaring (open obligations) |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **The containment argument stops depending on time.** This was already true and is now stated, which is the
  difference between a design that survives a clock fault and one that is assumed to.
- **Two clock costs replace one vague requirement.** Cheap local monotonic clocks cover the common path; trusted
  time is required only where authority is preserved.
- **The timing claim becomes checkable.** `W + L + D < T_irrev` is a budget a deployment can be held to, per
  effect class.
- **A headline claim gets more honest.** The corpus stops quoting an ordering as though it were a guarantee.

**Negative**
- **Trusted time is a new dependency** wherever §2's second row applies, and it is the expensive row. R8 inherits
  this.
- **`L` is unmeasured**, so the budget cannot be evaluated today for any deployment. Naming the gap does not close
  it.
- **`T_irrev` must be declared per effect class**, and nothing in the corpus enumerates effect classes that way
  yet.
- **Existing citations of `W < L < D` are now wrong** wherever they claim safety from the ordering. The derived
  documents need regenerating.

---

## Open obligations

- **Measure `L`**, or declare it as an assumption of use with a stated value per deployment.
- **Enumerate effect classes and their `T_irrev`**, which is the input the budget needs and the corpus lacks.
- **R8**, the break-glass grant TTL, now with its trusted-time cost named (ADR-0026 candidate entry).
- **Persona expiry / re-attestation interval** (ADR-0016; boundary register §8), an authority-preserving interval
  and therefore trusted-time.
- **TLA+ property.** `NoAdmitOnPrincipalTime` — no admission depends on a principal-supplied timestamp — with a
  mutation that must fail. A timed-automaton treatment of the lease module is the deferred option above.
- **Correct the derived documents** where `W < L < D` is quoted as a guarantee.
