# ADR-0056 — A call's user scope is downstream: the mechanism binds the subject from the grant, the policy decides whether a call may use it, the downstream system decides what it permits, and the tool decides nothing

**Status:** Proposed · 2026.11.06
**Status note:** Confirmation required under **C1**. A subject-bound credential is a new route by which an admitted
call carries authority that is not the deployment's own (§3, §5). **C2** applies to §5: the tool host's premise
widens, and the claim that a tool sees exactly one credential is an exhaustiveness claim on the channel. **Every
clause the mechanism applies subtracts** except one: binding a subject at grant, whose authority is the subject's
provider's (§2).
**Pending:** none.
**Deployment position:** Archetype. Three degeneracies (§9).
**Depends on:** ADR-0007 (no semantic lift), ADR-0030 (`bundle ⊑ floor`), ADR-0031 (the lease; its closed field set;
A3, the task travels beside the lease), ADR-0040 §3 and ADR-0049 (the entry, its channel, its load checks), ADR-0041
(the Run floor), ADR-0045 (the trusted base), ADR-0050 §3 and §5 (a provider's assertion naming the fence; the task
and its label carried by the grant)
**Relates to:** ADR-0003 and ADR-0028 (erasure and the forensic floor), ADR-0034 §3 (appraisal at grant with a nonce
from the log), ADR-0047 (labels), ADR-0048 (the tool list), ADR-0054 and ADR-0055 (a workflow's node grants;
Proposed)
**Closes:** nothing until ratified. On ratification: on-behalf-of attribution for an end user, which ADR-0037 lists
as not owed, in the one form this record decides (one subject per round, evidenced at grant). Delegation between
principals stays as ADR-0054 leaves it.
**Carries a debt against:** ADR-0049 §1, §4, §6 (the credential part of the channel; W11 to W13) · ADR-0050 §5 (a
grant may carry a subject) · ADR-0031 A3 (the subject travels beside the lease) · ADR-0041 (`R10`, `R11`; the
`RunCtx` attributes) · ADR-0042 §6 and ADR-0045 T-0045-25 (the tool host injects the credential) · ADR-0045 (two
rows, §8) · ADR-0025 §1 (f) and A2 (the issued list of subject providers and their keys) · ADR-0048 §2 (the tool
list shows the credential mode) · ADR-0054 §2 (Proposed: a workflow activation carries one subject)

---

## Context

**A deployment that acts for its users needs their scope downstream, and the corpus has no record of whose scope a
call uses.** A Run tool that reads "the current user's documents" reaches a system that authorizes by user. Today the
entry declares where the tool connects (ADR-0049 §5) and nothing about the credential it presents there. A deployment
has two choices, and both are bad. It can give the tool one service identity that can reach every user's data, which
is LLM06's example 5. Or it can let the tool pick a user's credential from something it was given, usually an input
the Thinker wrote.

**The second is the worse failure, and the admit path cannot see it.** If the user a call acts for is a literal input,
an injected Thinker names another user, and the downstream system honours it. ADR-0047's F2 refuses a literal
*destination* after untrusted reads. A subject is not a destination, and nothing marks it.

**Where the scope is enforced is not the problem.** The membrane decides what crosses. What the downstream system does
with a call it receives is its own authorization, as LLM06's mitigation 7 asks ("implement authorization in
downstream systems rather than relying on the LLM"). A per-user credential can only make an admitted call do *less*.
It weakens no theorem, and none needs to rest on it ⟨U1⟩. Carrying the user's scope in the lease would be worse. Every
lease field is authority the mechanism grants and can check (ADR-0031 §1), and a user's rights in a third-party system
are neither.

**The problem is the binding: who decides which user a call acts for.** A wrong binding *adds* authority: another
user's. By ADR-0045 §2's test, the component that decides it must be checked, or be a trust root with an issued
identity. A model is neither, and a tool's behaviour is a claim (ADR-0049 §4). So the binding must be a syntactic
lift from something on the record ⟨U2⟩.

**The corpus already has the shape.** ADR-0050 §3 binds an operator to a provider's signed assertion that names the
fence, checked at the lease boundary against an issued key. ADR-0050 §5 carries the task and its label with the
grant, beside the lease. This record carries a subject the same way.

## Decision

### 1. Four questions, four owners

| Question | Decided by | How | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Which user** does this round act for? | **The mechanism** | A syntactic lift from a provider-signed assertion carried with the grant (§2). Fixed for the round | Checked at grant |
| **May** this call use that user's credential? | **The policy** | The floor's minimum (§4), and the bundle may narrow on the subject's attributes. It never widens (ADR-0030) | Checked at admit |
| **What** may the credential do downstream? | **The downstream system** | Its own authorization, on the subject the credential names | Outside the pattern, by position ⟨U1⟩ |
| **Which credential** does the tool present? | **Nobody: the tool host injects the one credential for the round's subject** (§5) | The tool is handed exactly one credential and has no means to obtain another | The tool host's premise (T-0045-25, widened) |

**The tool decides nothing** *(drafter's choice; the custodian asked)*. There are three reasons:

- **A tool that chooses must hold every subject's credential.** That is the generic high-privileged identity of
  LLM06's example 5, moved from the network to the tool's memory.
- **A tool's behaviour is a claim.** The digest pins its code, not what it does with its inputs (ADR-0049 §2, §4).
  A choice made inside it is made past the membrane, off the record.
- **A tool's choice is steered by its inputs**, and its inputs are the Thinker's. Letting the tool choose is letting
  the model choose, one step removed.

### 2. The subject is carried by the grant, evidenced by its provider, and fixed for the round

- **A subject is not a principal** *(drafter's choice)*. It holds no seat, no lease and no alphabet, and it is not
  promoted. It is the party whose downstream authority a round's calls may use. ADR-0037's one-seat-per-principal
  rule is untouched.
- **A grant may carry a subject**, beside the lease and not in it, as the task and its label are (ADR-0050 §5;
  ADR-0031 A3). §1's lease field set stays closed. A grant carries at most one subject.
- **The evidence is a subject assertion**: signed by a provider whose key is on the issued rules' list of subject
  providers (ADR-0025 §1 (f)). It names the subject, the provider and **the fence of the grant it is presented
  for**. It is checked at grant, as an operator's sign-on assertion is (ADR-0050 §3). An assertion that does not
  verify, or names another fence, refuses the grant. No wall clock is read: freshness is the fence.
- **Whoever submits the grant cannot name a subject without that subject's assertion.** So an operator cannot act
  as an arbitrary user, and a subject is attributed to its provider's signature rather than to the operator's word.
- **The seating bounds it** *(drafter's choice)*. A seating names whether its rounds may carry a subject and from
  which providers, as it names the labels a task may carry (ADR-0050 §5). A grant outside that bound is refused. A
  quorum decides that a seat acts for users. One operator cannot.
- **One subject per round, fixed at grant** *(drafter's choice)*. A subject cannot be added, changed or dropped
  inside a round, as the tool list cannot (ADR-0048 §2). A cell that serves two users serves them in two rounds.
  This keeps each user's content in that user's round topic, so ADR-0003's unit of erasure stays per principal per
  round.
- **The subject's authority ends with the round.** The lease's `ttl` bounds it (ADR-0031 A2). Nothing is refreshed
  inside a round. A user who is not present at grant cannot be acted for (finding 2).

### 3. The entry declares its credential, and the declaration is claimed

**The channel part of the entry gains `credential`** (ADR-0049 §1; dependent amendment):

| Field | Values | Checked or claimed |
|---|---|---|
| `channel.credential.mode` | `none` · `service` · `subject` | **Checked**: the tool host injects by it (§5), and the floor reads it (§4) |
| `channel.credential.providers` | For `subject`: the subject providers whose assertions this entry accepts | **Checked**: against the issued list at load (W12) and the round's subject at admit (`R11`) |
| `channel.credential.scope` | Text: what the credential permits downstream | **Claimed**. What the downstream system enforces is its own |

- **The mode is required.** There is no default *(drafter's choice)*, by the totality rule ADR-0047 §1 and ADR-0049
  W4 apply. A Run entry without it does not load.
- **`service` is permitted and is not the default.** A service credential is the deployment's own identity, which is
  right for a tool that reaches nothing per-user. Whether a `service` entry reaches per-user data is a claim nothing
  checks (finding 3).
- **A Build entry has no channel, so it has no credential.** ADR-0049 W5 already refuses one.

**Load checks**, added to ADR-0049 §6:

| | The bundle is refused when |
|---|---|
| **W11** | A Run entry's channel has no `credential.mode`, or a mode outside the three |
| **W12** | A `subject` entry names no provider, or a provider the issued rules do not list, or has empty `scope` text |
| **W13** | A `none` or `service` entry carries `providers` |

### 4. The floor carries a minimum, and the bundle may only narrow

The lift adds to `RunCtx`, from the grant: `subject.present`, `subject.provider`, and `subject.attributes`, the claims
the provider signed, as a set of names. It adds to the tool: `credentialMode` and `subjectProviders`. Each is a lift
from the record, mechanical and total (ADR-0007).

| Id | Kind | Action | Holds when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R10-subject-entry-needs-subject` | forbid | `Actuate` | the tool's `credentialMode` is `subject` and `subject.present` is false |
| `R11-subject-provider-accepted` | forbid | `Actuate` | the tool's `credentialMode` is `subject` and `subject.provider` is not in the tool's `subjectProviders` |

- **The ids take the next free numbers** after the `workflow` stage's `R8` and `R9` *(drafter's choice)*.
- **`R11` duplicates what the tool host would fail anyway**, and is kept so the refusal is an admit decision on the
  record, not a failed call past the membrane *(drafter's choice)*.
- **The bundle may narrow on `subject.attributes`**, for example to allow an entry only for subjects in a named
  group. By `bundle ⊑ floor` such a rule can only deny more. A provider's claim can therefore subtract authority, and
  never add it.
- **No floor entry looks for a subject in the inputs.** Telling whether a free-text input names a user is a
  judgment. §6 closes the path downstream instead.

### 5. The tool host injects the one credential, and the Thinker never sees it

- **At each `Actuate` on a `subject` entry, the tool host obtains the credential for the round's subject and
  presents it on the tool's one channel.** The tool receives exactly that credential and no means to obtain another.
  For `service`, the host injects the entry's service credential. For `none`, nothing.
- **The preferred form is exchange, not storage** *(drafter's choice)*. The host exchanges the round's subject
  assertion for a downstream credential scoped to that subject, so no standing credential for any user exists at
  rest in the deployment. A store of per-user credentials that the host selects from is D-0056-2.
- **Neither the assertion nor the credential is ingress.** The Listener never delivers them. They are not in the
  round payload a Thinker reads, and they are not in any record's payload. **The tool list shows each entry's
  `credential.mode` and `scope`** (ADR-0048 §2; dependent amendment), so a Thinker knows a call acts as its user. It
  does not show the subject. The task may name the user, and that is content.
- **This widens T-0045-25's premise** (§8). The tool host already runs only the allowlisted binary and binds its
  socket (ADR-0034 A2). It is now also trusted to present only the round's subject's credential.

### 6. The downstream system takes the subject from the credential

**An assumption of use, stated as one** (ToO §13; dependent amendment): *a downstream system that a `subject` entry
reaches authorizes on the subject its credential names, and never on a subject named in the request.* This is what
closes the free-text path that §4 leaves open. A Thinker may write any user's name into an input. The downstream
system acts for the user in the token. A deployment whose downstream system reads the subject from a request
parameter declares D-0056-3.

### 7. What reaches the record

- **The grant record carries the subject's provider and a digest of the subject's identifier** *(drafter's choice)*.
  This is an authority fact, so it is in the forensic floor and survives erasure (ADR-0028). The assertion's bytes
  are in the round's payload, inside the unit of erasure (ADR-0003).
- **Every `Actuate` record on a `subject` entry is attributable to the subject** through its round's grant. The admit
  record gains no field.
- **An Observer and the correlator read the subject's digest**, as they read every grant fact, so a finding can name
  the user a round acted for.

### 8. The trusted base

| Row | Component | Class | Failure can produce | Identity fixed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T-0045-38** | A subject provider | trust root | A round that acts for a subject who did not ask, within what the seating and floor allow | Issued (ADR-0025 §1 (f)), on the list of subject providers |
| **T-0045-25** (widened) | The tool host | trust root | As before, and a call presented with another subject's credential | Promotion: part of the release |
| **T-0045-39** | A downstream system's authorization, and any token service that mints for it | **excluded** | Whatever the credential permits, done for the subject it names | Nothing. No theorem rests on it ⟨U1⟩ |

- **T-0045-39 is excluded, and that is the position, not a gap.** Every theorem stays about the membrane. The row
  exists so that a reader computing a deployment's guarantees sees that per-user scope is the downstream system's.
- **A provider is a trust root for the subjects it vouches for, and for no others**, as an operator's provider is
  (T-0045-35). One provider may serve both.

### 9. Degeneracy on this record

| Id | Degeneracy | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **D-0056-1** | **Declared subject**: the subject is named by the operator's signed grant, with no provider assertion | Attribution moves from the subject's provider to the operator. A havoc operator can act for any subject the seating admits. §2's bound still holds |
| **D-0056-2** | **Stored credentials**: the tool host selects a per-user credential from a store rather than exchanging the round's assertion | The host's failure reaches every stored subject, not only the round's. The store becomes part of T-0045-25 |
| **D-0056-3** | **Request-named subject downstream**: the downstream system reads the subject from the request (§6) | A Thinker's input names the user. The confused deputy is open, and only the bundle and the Observer stand against it |

### 10. What this record does not cover

- **How a user's request becomes a task.** Today a Run task arrives with a seating or an operator's `Regrant`
  (ADR-0050 §5), so a round for a user needs an operator to act. A deployment serving many users needs a task path
  without one human per round. That is a separate record (finding 1).
- **Long-running or offline action for a user**, such as refresh tokens or a scheduled job for an absent user (§2;
  finding 2).
- **Delegation between principals**, which ADR-0054 decides by seating every cell.
- **A subject's consent**, beyond the fact that its provider signed an assertion naming the grant's fence.

### 11. Ratification condition

- **ADR-0050 is confirmed first, or this record is ratified provisionally with it**, since it stands on §3 and §5 of
  that record.
- **The dependent entries are drafted**: ADR-0049 (§3's fields; W11 to W13), ADR-0050 §5, ADR-0031 A3, ADR-0041
  (`R10`, `R11`, the `RunCtx` attributes), ADR-0042 §6, ADR-0045 (T-0045-38, T-0045-39, T-0045-25's premise),
  ADR-0025 A2 (the provider list), ADR-0048 §2, ToO §13 (§6's assumption of use), and ADR-0054 §2 if it is ratified
  first.
- **A module states the properties and its mutations break them**, by the method of ADR-0046. At least:
  `SubjectFixedForRound`, `SubjectEvidenced` (no grant carries a subject without a verified assertion naming its
  fence), `InjectedIsRoundSubject` (every credential presented is the round's subject's), and `SubjectEntryNeedsSubject`.
  Mutations: `M_INPUT_SUBJECT` (the host takes the subject from an input), `M_RESUBJECT` (a subject changes inside a
  round), `M_STALE_ASSERTION` (an assertion naming an earlier fence is accepted) and `M_NO_R10`.
- **The floor**: a stage `subjects` in `floor/proposed/`, built on the stage in force when this is ratified, with
  `R10` and `R11`, validated, tested and gated, each entry shown to fire and none shown vacuous.
- **The reference**, with an ADR-0033 addendum: one `subject` entry over a stub downstream that authorizes by token.
  **Behaviours, each seen:** a grant with a valid assertion binds; one naming another fence is refused; a `subject`
  call in a subjectless round is refused by `R10`; a Thinker input naming another user reaches the stub and is
  served for the token's subject, not the input's; the Thinker's tool list shows the mode and not the subject.
- **A reader who is not the drafter** (ADR-0029 A2).

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **The subject in the lease**, as a thirteenth field | Rejected ⟨U1⟩. Every lease field is authority the mechanism grants (ADR-0031 §1). A subject's authority is its provider's, and it travels beside the lease as the task does |
| **The subject as a Thinker input**, checked by policy | Rejected ⟨U2⟩. Whether the named user is the right one is a judgment about content (ADR-0007) |
| **The tool chooses the credential** | Rejected (§1). It needs every credential, its choice is a claim, and its inputs are the model's |
| **A subject input marked as a `destination`**, so F2 and F3 apply | Not taken. It stretches what a destination is, and it still lets a Thinker that has read only trusted content name any user |
| **A seat per user** | Rejected. A user is not promoted and holds no Persona, and a quorum per user does not scale. One entry or seating per tenant remains possible and is syntactic, so it needs no degeneracy |
| **The operator declares the subject** | Not taken at the archetype. It is D-0056-1 |
| **Several subjects per round** | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. It mixes two users' content in one round topic and one `clearSet`, and needs a per-call subject, which is a choice someone must make |
| **A floor forbid on `service` entries in a subject round** | Not taken. A `service` entry that reaches nothing per-user is legitimate, and the floor cannot tell which kind it is (finding 3). The bundle may forbid it |
| **No record: leave per-user scope to deployments** | Rejected. The scope can be left to deployments, and §6 does. The binding cannot, because its failure adds authority |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **LLM06 mitigation 5 and example 5 have an answer** of the pattern's shape. The membrane decides who and whether.
  The downstream system decides what.
- **The confused deputy is closed at three points**: at grant (no subject without its assertion), at the host (one
  credential per round) and downstream (§6). Each fails toward refusal.
- **A provider's claims can only narrow** a round's authority, by `bundle ⊑ floor`.
- **No standing per-user credential is needed** at the archetype (§5).
- **The lease is unchanged.**

**Negative**
- **Two new trust-base facts**: one root, T-0045-38, and one widened premise on T-0045-25. T-0045-39 is a row that
  admits the pattern does not reach the scope.
- **`scope` is a claim**, like `purpose`. A reviewer at promotion is told what the credential permits and cannot check
  it.
- **The subject must be present at grant**, which excludes offline action at the archetype.
- **Every Run entry changes form** (W11): a bundle and policy change together, as ADR-0049's was.
- **One author, one day.**

---

## Findings

1. **The task path is the larger gap.** A round for a user needs a grant that carries the user's assertion. Today
   grants come from a quorum or an operator, and ADR-0050 §2's "the rate problem does not arise" assumed one operator
   acting per round. A deployment serving users at volume reopens that rate problem. This record binds a subject however the
   task arrives. It does not say how a user's request arrives.
2. **Freshness by fence asks the user's session to be live at grant.** That is the right failure direction: an absent
   user's authority lapses. But it excludes a class of deployment (scheduled or long-running work for a user) that
   would need a degeneracy or a record of its own.
3. **`service` is where LLM06's example 5 survives.** An entry declared `service` that in fact reaches per-user data
   is the generic high-privileged identity, and nothing checks the declaration. The `scope` text and promotion are the
   only defence, as for `purpose`.
4. **Results from a `subject` call are that user's data**, and nothing labels them so. Within a round one subject
   holds. Across rounds, a workflow edge (ADR-0054) could carry one user's data into another's round unless the
   activation is per subject. ADR-0054's pending amendment should say so.
5. **§6 is an assumption, and it carries the weight for free text.** The floor cannot find a user named in an input.
   The design moves that path to a system TOPAK does not verify, which is the position ⟨U1⟩, and is recorded as one.

## Open obligations

- **The form of the subject assertion**, and whether an existing token format (an OIDC ID token with a nonce bound to
  the fence; a token-exchange request) carries it without change.
- **The size and form of `subject.attributes`**, and which provider claims may be lifted.
- **A task path for end users** (finding 1): a record of its own.
- **Whether a digest of a personal identifier in the forensic floor** meets a deployment's data obligations (§7).
- **The label of a `subject` call's result** (finding 4): whether ADR-0047's vocabulary gains a per-subject
  sensitivity name, or the bundle declares one.
- **ADR-0054's activation** carrying one subject, if ADR-0054 is ratified first.
