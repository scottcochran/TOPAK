## Context

ADR-0024 makes promotion a quorum of principals. That moves the gate up one level: the authority
defining who votes cannot itself be voted in without regress.

Three treatments were considered:

- **(A) Infallible.** The anchor is an oracle.
- **(B) Havoc.** Any authentic issuance, bounded by a verified floor.
- **(C) Characterized.** B, plus a model of how members decide.

Under B, safety is proven for every issuance the anchor could make, so composition never enters the
proof. C adds governance content, not proof content. A costs P1 at the top of the authority chain
and adds nothing the proof needs.

"Infallible" bundles three properties that want different treatment: intent, integrity, and custody.

**Prior art**
- Trust anchor (RFC 5280, RFC 6024)
- Root role (The Update Framework, TUF)
- Configuration master (Vertical Paxos)

## Decision

### 1. The trust anchor is the sole issuer of rules

**Rules** are the authority-defining set:

- **(a)** The promotion roster and its parameters (ADR-0024).
- **(b)** The break-glass and erasure quorum rosters.
- **(c)** The anchor's own key set.
- **(d)** Any change that relaxes the policy floor or adds a floor exception.
- **(e)** Any change to the rules floor, in either direction (ADR-0026 §4). ⟨R1⟩
- **(f)** The keys the mechanism accepts a signature from in place of its own check: the verifier's
  appraisal key at rung 3 (ADR-0034 §2), and the bundle-signing key (ADR-0044 §1). Each decides what
  the mechanism will admit on the strength of a signature, so replacing one adds authority, and is
  an issuance. ⟨A1⟩

Tightening the **policy** floor is not a rules change. It is an ordinary promotion. ⟨R1⟩ The rules
floor is the exception: tightening it constrains the anchor itself, so it needs two keys like any
other rules change (ADR-0026 §4), which is why it is listed as (e) above.

### 2. Infallibility is split three ways

| Property | Treatment | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent** | **ASSUMED**, the sole axiom | *An authentic issuance at the current anchor epoch is what the anchor intended.* |
| **Integrity** | **MODELED** under havoc | `Issue` is an environment action. It is constrained only by `Authentic`, epoch increment, and the rules floor (ADR-0026). Safety is proven for every issuance sequence satisfying those constraints. |
| **Custody** | **DECLARED** per rung (ADR-0023) | The pair (capture threshold, loss tolerance), plus the composition and eviction rules that make the pair true. Not in the model. |

`Authentic(i)` is opaque in the specification. The custody declaration instantiates it: which
keys, and how many signatures. Changing custody never reopens a proof.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **(A) Infallible anchor** | Rejected. Key theft becomes infallible too. The rules floor loses its justification. Silent degradation escapes declaration. P1 is abandoned at the top of the authority chain. Residual risk and GOVERN 2.3 accountability become unstatable. |
| **(C) Composition in the model** | Rejected. No proof content under havoc, and every custody change would couple to re-verification. |
| **Verified anchor** | Rejected. Relocates the axiom without removing it. |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **The authority regress ends at a one-sentence axiom.** With ADR-0026, it is also bounded by a
  verified floor, mirroring how the policy regress ends.
- **Custody evolution is declaration-tier.** No proofs are reopened.
- **Residual risk becomes numeric.** The capture threshold feeds MANAGE 1.4 directly.
- **The keys that vouch for artifacts are issued, not configured** ⟨A1⟩. The rules schema gains
  `verifier_keys` and `bundle_keys` (`mechanism/` I8's `Rules`), each a set, each possibly empty. A
  deployment with no rung-3 lease has no verifier key; one whose bundle tier is unused has no bundle
  key. An empty set admits nothing that needs the key: no rung-3 grant, no bundle load. The rules
  floor is unchanged: R5 compares seat keys with anchor keys, and an (f) key is not a seat and casts
  no vote. Whether an (f) key may also be an anchor key is a custody question for the deployment
  declaration, as A-0025 leaves seat keys.

**Negative**
- **No safety theorem covers anchor intent.** A coalition at the capture threshold can issue any
  rules-floor-conformant roster. This is the headline residual.
- **Freeze-on-loss makes evolution fragile.** The archetype custody declaration freezes on loss, so
  operators will be pressured toward degenerate custody rungs. That is correct if declared.

## Open obligations

- **Genesis and key-ceremony procedure.** Includes the genesis eviction or recovery rule for
  degenerate rungs.
- **Re-genesis migration.** What a successor system may inherit, and under whose authority.
  **Includes the forensic classes** ⟨R1⟩: ADR-0028 §3 sets the archetype legibility horizon to the
  lifetime of the rules-epoch chain, and ADR-0027 §4 starts a new chain at re-genesis, so on the
  present wording the authority record lapses exactly when it matters most — re-genesis follows
  anchor loss or capture. The migration rule must state that a successor inherits read access to its
  predecessor's forensic classes, and it is required before ADR-0028's confirmation promotion
  (S6-14).
- **A named owner and organizational role** for every custody declaration (GOVERN 2.3).
- **A custody row for the ADR corpus itself.** Currently (1, 0), from solo authorship.

## Scope carried into open records

- **ADR-0013**
  - **Single grant-authorization model:** every **privileged** grant — promotion, break-glass,
    erasure — is a quorum on an anchor-issued roster. ⟨R1⟩ Ordinary Run admits are grants too
    (ADR-0015) and are decided against a bundle, not a roster; the unqualified sentence claimed
    otherwise (S6-13).
  - **Non-composition:** break-glass × erasure (NC-1) and erasure × forensic floor (NC-2).
    Break-glass × promotion and break-glass × issuance are discharged by ADR-0026 R7.
  - **Forensic floor:** issuances are non-erasable, now ADR-0028 class F4.
  - **Exception-count bound:** becomes a rules-floor parameter (ADR-0026).
- **ADR-0023**
  - **Archetype dominance rule:** the archetype moves on a custody axis only to Pareto-dominating
    pairs.
  - **Non-increasing TCB across archetype versions:** moving any property into *assumed* requires
    its own ADR.

## Annex — Custody declarations

These are declaration records under ADR-0023. Each is separately supersedable by construction and is
not a decision clause of this ADR.

### A-0025 — Archetype custody: strict unanimity

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Pair | **(n, 0)** |
| `Authentic` | Signed by every key in the anchor key set at the current epoch |
| Composition | Unanimous quorum of `Mediated` key-holders, n ≥ 3 |
| Key-holder type | **At the gate** under the restated test (ADR-0002 Amendment 2). `Mediated` applies as an attribution predicate only. |
| Loss behavior | Any permanent loss freezes the rules. The system can run and subtract, but cannot change. |
| **Resignation** ⟨R1⟩ | A permanent loss, and therefore a freeze. Resignation is not eviction (ADR-0002 A2), and unanimity leaves no rule by which the remaining n−1 may seat a replacement. A key-holder who wishes to leave without freezing the rules must first move the deployment to D-0025-2. |
| Exit | **Re-genesis.** A successor system, not a continuation. |

### D-0025-1 — TUF-style threshold with recovery

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Pair | **(k, n−k)**, k < n−1 |
| Premise falsified | Rule changes carry unanimous consent |
| `Authentic` | k of n signatures; a genesis-declared recovery rule lets k rotate any key |
| Key-holder type | **Principals.** The gate moves to the genesis threshold-and-recovery rule. |
| Gained | Liveness under up to n−k losses |
| Sacrificed | A k-coalition can issue rules over n−k objections |
| **Resignation** ⟨R1⟩ | Absorbed by the recovery rule: k rotate the resigning key to a replacement. No freeze while k remain; a resignation that drops the set below k is a loss like any other. |
| Prior art | TUF root role and root-key rotation; RFC 6024 |

### D-0025-2 — n−1 eviction with replacement

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Pair | **(n−1, 1)** |
| Premise falsified | No key-holder is removed without their own consent |
| Eviction rule | Unanimity of the remaining n−1 evicts one key-holder, atomically with seating a replacement |
| Key-holder type | **Principals.** The gate moves to the genesis-declared eviction rule. |
| Gained | A compromised key-holder's permanent veto ends. Freeze requires a second loss within one replacement window. |
| **Resignation** ⟨R1⟩ | The eviction rule run with the departing key-holder's consent: the remaining n−1 seat a replacement atomically, as for an eviction. It consumes the epoch's one eviction, so a resignation and a compromise cannot be handled in the same anchor epoch. |
| Preconditions for the pair | One eviction per anchor epoch; n ≥ 3; the replacement countersigns |
| Optional mitigation | A time-locked notice window in which an outside quorum may deny (subtractive, so admissible) |
