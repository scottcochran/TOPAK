## Context

**One row of the trusted base asks one store to do two opposite things.** T-0045-7 is "The archive and plaintext
store", and its identity is fixed by "The deployment declaration, with object lock or equivalent". ADR-0004 keeps
plaintext in "a separately governed store built for deletion". A lock exists so that nothing can be removed, and
the governed store exists so that something can. On the common object-store interface the conflict is exact: a
bucket with Object Lock must keep versions, and a bucket that keeps versions keeps what a delete removed.

**The corpus has asked for integrity without saying of what.** ADR-0015 ends the audit chain "at a named axiom":
"Effect → admit → bundle ⊑ floor → rules epoch → issuance → ADR-0025's intent axiom". A recovery finding in
ADR-0003 recalls that an early design had "a Merkle root over round envelopes", and that none of it appears in the
record. ADR-0006 wants lawful erasure told apart from rewriting. An archive that held the facts as ordinary rows
could offer none of this. Whoever administered it could change a row, and the only check would be a copy elsewhere.

**The archive must not become an authority by being read.** T-0045-7 is delegated because its failure "loses
evidence, which subtracts legibility and never authority". That holds only while nothing decides on what the
archive returns. ADR-0028 already notes that "Stop attribution is a feedback path, not merely evidence". ADR-0035
(Proposed) computes lineage "from the consensus log", and ADR-0069's IG-4 (Proposed) reruns an extractor "on the
cited log positions". Once the log is compacted (ADR-0074 §4), those positions are in the archive.

**No record says who reads the archive or how.** ADR-0003 A3 has it "read under its own authority (ADR-0004)", and
ADR-0004 defines none. ADR-0028 §3 declares an audit authority, "the anchor plus a declared external auditor role",
and lists the definition of that role as open. The Adopter's Guide says it plainly: "No record supplies the
archive, its horizon or its read path".

**The custodian asked that the archive be informed by the documentation's design** ⟨K12⟩. That design is a
single-table store behind a fixed interface, two generated read interfaces, and every past view equal to the
newest. Each of those fits an archive better than it fits a documentation site, and §4 and §7 reuse them.

## Decision

### 1. Two stores and an index

| Part | Holds (ADR-0074 §2) | Its duty | Its failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The locked store** | A facts, as segments; C the corpus; D evidence; E the journal | Keep, unchanged, for each line's *at least* (ADR-0075 §2) | Evidence lost or damaged |
| **The governed store** | B round units | Keep until erasure or period, and then no longer | A unit lost early. A unit not deleted |
| **The index** | A projection of both: fields of facts, unit headers, cut manifests | Answer queries | Unavailable, or wrong and caught by a proof (§7 RD-7) |

- **P-1. The two stores share nothing**: no bucket, no volume, no credential and no administrator's role. The
  feeder of facts holds no right to delete anywhere. The only right to delete in the governed store is the
  executor's, which acts on an F6 entry or the end of a period (ADR-0075 §3 ER-1, §5).
- **P-2. The index is not the record.** It can be dropped and rebuilt from the two stores, and the rebuild is
  checked against the roots (§5), as ADR-0072 §1 P-2 checks its own.
- **P-3. Each part's interface is fixed and its product is not**: the S3 interface with Object Lock for the locked
  store, the S3 interface without it for the governed store, and the DynamoDB interface for the index. ⟨K5⟩

### 2. The locked store

- **LS-1. Every object is addressed by its digest and written once.** A segment, a file of a cut, an evidence
  object and a journal segment are each stored under the SHA-256 of their bytes.
- **LS-2. Every object is under a lock no credential can shorten or lift**: the interface's *compliance* mode, or
  what a deployment declares as its equivalent. The lock's term is extended before it ends, for as long as the
  object's line runs, and is never shortened. **A lock that lapses on an object still under its line is a finding.**
- **LS-3. Every object is held in every fault domain the deployment declares.** The archetype's number is at least
  three, under at least two administrations, with one of them sharing no site with the mechanism's replicas
  *(drafter's choice)*. A segment is *held* only when all of them have it (ADR-0074 §4 A-1).
- **LS-4. Bytes are kept as they were written.** A segment holds the log's entries in the serialization the log
  used. The archive does not re-encode, compress lossily or migrate a format in place. A segment names the cut of
  the corpus whose formats it is written in, and that cut is in holding C, so the means of reading a segment is
  kept with it.
- **LS-5. The reference's product is Ceph's object gateway** *(drafter's recommendation)*. It is LGPL, implements
  the interface with Object Lock, and is the most widely deployed of the open-source stores that do. Options lists
  the others.

### 3. The governed store

- **GS-1. A unit is one container, in one store, for the whole of its time.** ADR-0003 0003.7 as scoped by A3 has
  "one store per topic for the whole of its retention: a sealed topic is never moved between storage classes, and
  it is dropped whole". The unit keeps that rule in the archive *(drafter's choice)*: no tiering, no move to a
  cheaper class.
- **GS-2. The store keeps no version and holds no lock.** A deleted object is gone from every read at once. This
  is the reason the governed store cannot be a bucket of the locked store.
- **GS-3. A unit is *dropped* when no read in any fault domain returns it, and *purged* when its bytes are gone
  from the media.** A store reclaims space later than it deletes. The deployment declares the purge interval, and
  ADR-0075 §3 ER-2's *erased* mark is ordered on the drop. A per-unit key destroyed at the drop covers the interval
  (ADR-0075 §3 ER-3). What happens to media at the end of their life is the deployment's sanitization practice.
- **GS-4. Nothing derived from a unit is stored outside it** (ADR-0075 §3 ER-5). The store has no search service, no
  thumbnailing, no analytics, and no replication to a system outside §1.
- **GS-5. A unit is held in the fault domains the deployment declares for payload**, each of which receives every
  drop. There may be fewer than the locked store's. Losing a unit early costs legibility, and every extra copy is
  one more place an erasure has to reach.
- **GS-6. The reference's product is the same gateway under a separate deployment** *(drafter's recommendation)*,
  with versioning off, no lock, and its own credentials (P-1).

### 4. The index ⟨K5⟩ ⟨K12⟩

- **IX-1. The index is one table behind the DynamoDB interface, under ADR-0072 §5's contract**: a composite primary
  key, every access pattern a key condition and none a scan, secondary indexes for the other readings, immutability
  by conditional write. Annex A gives the keys.
- **IX-2. The index holds fields of facts and of headers, and no attribute derived from a body** (ADR-0075 §3
  ER-5). A unit's item says that the unit exists, where, its digest and its state. It does not say what is in it.
- **IX-3. The index is rebuilt from the stores, and every answer about a fact can be proved without it** (§7 RD-7).
  An engine that loses or corrupts the index costs readers their answers until a rebuild. It cannot change what
  the record says.
- **IX-4. The reference's engine is ADR-0072 §6's**, ExtendDB on PostgreSQL *(drafter's recommendation)*, with that
  section's caveat in full: it is new, it declares itself for development and testing, and nothing here has run it.
  IX-3 is why that is tolerable for an archive's index, as P-2 of ADR-0072 was for a documentation store. **It
  would not be tolerable for either store of §2 or §3, and it is not proposed for them.**

### 5. Integrity

- **IN-1. The facts of a chain form one Merkle tree, in log order**, in the form RFC 9162 gives a transparency log
  *(drafter's choice)*. A leaf is the hash of one entry's bytes. The tree after a segment's last entry has a root,
  and that root covers every entry of the chain so far.
- **IN-2. The log orders each root.** The *held* mark of ADR-0074 §4 names the segment's range and the root after
  it, and a replica proposes it only for a root it recomputed. So the roots are facts, and an auditor has them from
  the log and not from the archive.
- **IN-3. Two proofs follow, and the archive serves both.** *Inclusion*: this entry is a leaf of the tree with this
  root. *Consistency*: the tree at a later mark extends the tree at an earlier one, with nothing before changed.
  With them the audit chain of ADR-0015 can be walked from an effect to the genesis issuance without trusting the
  store the entries came from.
- **IN-4. An erasure touches no leaf** (ADR-0075 §4 AB-4). No body is in the tree. A deployment that keeps fewer
  entries (D-0075-1) keeps their leaf hashes, and every proof still holds.
- **IN-5. A unit is checked against the log as far as the log reaches.** Every emission in a unit carries the log
  index and sequence number of its admit (ADR-0005 §2), and the round "seals at the final sequence number"
  (ADR-0042 §5). So a captured unit with an emission missing, added or out of place is detectable from the facts.
  For Build, the container is checked against the sealed topic-set digest the votes bound (ADR-0021 §4). **What the
  log does not reach is a body's bytes where the log holds no digest of them**, and the denials, which the log does
  not order.
- **IN-6. The journal has a tree of its own, and each mark carries its root.** The mechanism cannot recompute it and
  does not vouch for it. Ordering it fixes what the archive said and when, so that the archive cannot later say
  something else about what it ingested, dropped or showed.
- **IN-7. Fixity is audited on a declared cycle.** Every object in the locked store is rehashed in every fault
  domain. A mismatch is repaired from another domain and entered in the journal, and one that cannot be repaired is
  a finding. The same audit reads the governed store for every unit an *erased* or *disposed* mark names, and a
  container found there is a finding (ADR-0074 §4 A-7). Each audit writes a dated results file, which is never
  edited.
- **IN-8. No key of the archive's is trusted for anything.** The archive signs nothing a decision or an auditor
  relies on. The roots are the log's. A deployment may add an outside witness or a timestamp authority (RFC 3161).
  That is an addition, with the standing ADR-0069 §9 C-2 gives a carrier's check: it may refuse and it never
  admits.

### 6. Nothing that decides reads the archive without a proof

- **AR-1. The admit path does not read the archive.** An admit is decided on the replicated state and the rules in
  force. This is true of the reference today, and this clause keeps it true.
- **AR-2. A gate, a tally or a tool that needs an entry the log no longer holds takes it from the archive only with
  an inclusion proof against a mark in the log.** An entry that comes without one is treated as absent, and the
  gate fails. Lineage (ADR-0035 §1, Proposed) and the intake gate that reruns an extractor (ADR-0069 §3a IG-4,
  Proposed) are the first two readers this binds.
- **AR-3. Absence only subtracts.** A gate that cannot obtain a proved entry does not pass. No rule lets a missing
  record count as a clean one.
- **AR-4. Nothing decides on a unit.** No admit, gate or tally reads holding B. A unit is evidence for people and
  for the Observer's successors in review, and "never admits anything", as ADR-0037 §3 says of the raw emission in
  it.

**So an archive that fails in any way, lying included, produces no admit, promotion or issuance the mechanism
would otherwise refuse.** That is ADR-0045 §2's test for a delegated row, and §11 asks for it as a property.

### 7. Reading ⟨K6⟩ ⟨K10⟩ ⟨K12⟩

- **RD-1. Every read is by an identified reader, under a reader set.** A reader set is declared per holding, and
  for holding A per forensic class, in each chain.

  | Holding | Readers |
  |---|---|
  | **A** | The declared audit authority, always: "the anchor plus a declared external auditor role" (ADR-0028 §3). Others as the deployment declares. A class may be removed from a reader's set, and never from the audit authority's (ADR-0028 §4) |
  | **B** | Whoever the round topic's ACL named (ADR-0003 0003.3), carried over to the unit *(drafter's choice)*, and the "Separate read-only principals for audit" of ADR-0003 |
  | **C** | Anyone, for the archetype's corpus, which is published. A deployment's own cuts as it declares |
  | **D** | As the fact that cites the object |
  | **E** | The audit authority |

- **RD-2. Reader sets are policy, in a promoted bundle** *(drafter's choice)*. The read service evaluates them and
  holds no list of its own. ADR-0028 §4's proviso, that the audit authority stays a reader, belongs in the floor.
  The genesis draft floor has an erasure entry and a forensic-classes entry and none about readers.
- **RD-3. The interfaces are ADR-0072 §7's**: GraphQL and REST, generated from one model, equal, and read-only.
  That section's A-1 to A-4, A-6 and A-7 apply as written. ⟨K6⟩
- **RD-4. The operations are these.**

  | Operation | Returns |
  |---|---|
  | Facts of a chain by log index range; by principal and round; by forensic class; by rules epoch | Entries, each with its proof (RD-7) |
  | Facts that cite a digest | Every vote on an artifact, every admit under a bundle revision, every grant that cites an appraisal |
  | The marks of a chain; a consistency proof between two | §5 IN-3 |
  | A unit's header and state: present, erased, disposed, not captured, held | For any reader of A |
  | A unit's records | For a reader of B only |
  | Erasures ordered and not complete; headers with no explanation | The two lists ADR-0075 §3 ER-2 and §4 AB-1 define |
  | A cut; a path's digests across cuts; an object by digest | ADR-0074 §5 C-1, C-2 |
  | The journal: ingests, fixity results, drops, reads | For a reader of E |
  | An export (§8) | A package |

- **RD-5. Every past state is read as the newest is** ⟨K10⟩. A superseded bundle revision, the rules of an earlier
  epoch, a closed chain and an old cut are reached by the same operations as their successors, and no operation
  is defined only for what is in force.
- **RD-6. Every read but a read of C is entered in the journal**: the reader, the set it read under, what was
  asked for, and the purpose the reader gave, which nothing checks. This is the accounting ADR-0028 lists as a
  requirement its floor "does not express".
- **RD-7. An answer about a fact carries its inclusion proof and names its mark.** A reader verifies it against a
  root the log ordered. The read service and the index are then conveniences. The reader does not have to trust
  either.
- **RD-8. A read writes nothing but its journal entry.**
- **RD-9. How to read the archive is a document**, ADR-0071's Ex-3, written at three levels like any other. Its
  bindings point at objects in holdings C and D by digest.

### 8. Export

- **EX-1. Any range a reader may read can be exported as one self-describing package**: the objects, a manifest of
  their SHA-256 digests, the marks that cover them, and the proofs. The reference's form is a BagIt bag (RFC 8493)
  *(drafter's choice)*.
- **EX-2. A package verifies with no access to the archive**: the digests against the manifest, the entries
  against the roots, and the roots against a copy of the log's marks that the reader obtains separately.
- **EX-3. A package of units goes only to a reader of B**, is a copy that ADR-0075 §3 ER-4 does not reach, and is
  entered in the journal as such. What the recipient then owes an erasure is the deployment's to bind them to.
- **EX-4. A successor chain's audit authority receives a closed chain's facts as a package**, once ADR-0025's
  migration rule says it may (ADR-0075 §6 RG-3).

### 9. The trusted base

**T-0045-7 stays delegated, and Annex B proposes it become two rows** *(drafter's recommendation)*: T-0045-7 for the
locked store, and a new T-0045-40 for the governed store, whose worst failure is the opposite of the first's.

| Component | Class | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The locked store | delegated (T-0045-7) | Its failure loses or damages evidence. §6 keeps a damaged entry from deciding anything |
| The governed store | delegated (T-0045-40, proposed) | Its failure loses a unit early, or fails to delete one. Neither is an admit, a promotion or an issuance. **The second is a harm to a person and is the residual on the row's face** |
| The index, the read service, the feeders, the export | No row | Projections and copies. A read service that shows a class to a reader outside its set is a confidentiality failure. It is stated as a residual on T-0045-7 and adds no authority |
| The fixity audit | No row | It reports. It decides nothing |

## Options considered

**The stores.** Licences and features are as the provenance note says each was learned, and none was verified
against a running system.

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **Ceph object gateway** (LGPL): the interface with Object Lock, set at a bucket's creation | **Recommended for both stores**, as two deployments (§2 LS-5, §3 GS-6). Heavy to run |
| **SeaweedFS** (Apache-2.0): its wiki claims Object Lock, retention and legal hold | Not taken as the reference: the claim was read on a mirror of the wiki and not tested. The lighter candidate, and the first substitution to try |
| **MinIO** (AGPL) | Not taken. Its community edition is reported to be in maintenance mode, with no new features accepted |
| **Garage** (AGPL) | Not taken. Whether it supports Object Lock was not confirmed. A candidate for the governed store, which needs none |
| **Apache Ozone** (Apache-2.0) | Not taken. Built for analytic workloads; its lock support was not looked up |
| **A filesystem with immutable snapshots and holds** (ZFS, for example) | Not taken as the reference. A real equivalent under LS-2's "or what a deployment declares", with no interface another product could stand behind |
| **Write-once media**: tape or optical | Admissible for the locked store as a fault domain. Wrong for the governed store, which must delete |
| **A managed object service with Object Lock** | A substitution a deployment may declare, as ADR-0072 §6 allows for the index |
| **One store for everything**, with a prefix for units | Rejected (D-0076-4). The lock needs versions, and versions defeat the delete |
| **The transport with long retention as the archive** | Rejected by ADR-0005 §1 and ADR-0003 A3 |
| **A relational database as the archive** | Rejected for the stores. Rows can be changed by whoever administers it, and deletion leaves the bytes in its log and its backups. PostgreSQL does sit under the index, where neither matters |
| **Apache Accumulo for the index**, for its cell-level visibility labels | Not taken ⟨K5⟩. Its labels would put compartmentation (RD-1) in the store itself, which is attractive. It is not the DynamoDB interface, and reader sets are policy here (RD-2) |
| **The index's engine for the stores as well** | Rejected (IX-4). An engine that declares itself unfit for production may hold a projection and not a record |

**Integrity.**

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **A hash chain**, each segment naming the one before | Not taken. It shows that history was not rewritten only to someone who reads all of it. A tree gives a short proof for one entry |
| **A transparency-log service** (Trillian or its successor) as the tree | Open. IN-1 fixes the form and not the program. A pinned library may well be the implementation |
| **The archive signs its roots** | Rejected (IN-8). The key would be a trust root for whether evidence exists. The log already orders the roots |
| **A public ledger or an outside timestamp as the anchor** | Not taken as the anchor. Admissible as an addition (IN-8) |
| **A ledger database** | Not taken. The ones the drafter knows are a proprietary service since withdrawn, or under a source-available licence |
| **Bodies as leaves of the tree** | Rejected (IN-4). An erasure would then leave a hole in the tree, or a hash of low-entropy content that "can be confirmed by guessing", which is ADR-0047's reason for not using content digests in handles |
| **Trust the archive for old entries**, with no proof | Rejected (§6). It would make the archive a trust root with no issued identity, which ADR-0045 §2 refuses |

**Reading.**

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **Direct access to the stores for auditors** | Not taken. No reader sets, no journal, and for B no ACL |
| **Reader sets held by the archive** | Not taken (RD-2). They would be a list an administrator edits. In a bundle they are promoted, and a change to them is a vote in the log |
| **Journal only reads of B** | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. Who looked at the authority record is also something an audit asks |
| **A query language over the facts** | Not taken. IX-1 has every reading a key condition. An auditor who needs more takes an export and queries their own copy |
| **Only GraphQL, or only REST** | Not taken ⟨K6⟩ ⟨K12⟩ |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **The lock and the delete each have a store**, and the conflict in T-0045-7's one line is gone.
- **An auditor can check one entry without reading the whole record and without trusting the archive**, because
  the roots are in the log.
- **The archive can lie and nothing is admitted on its word.** Its class stays delegated for a reason that can be
  stated as a property.
- **A failed delete can be found**: the audit looks for what the marks say is gone.
- **Who read what is itself on the record.**
- **The documentation's design carries over nearly whole**: one table, two interfaces, every past state equal.
- **The index's engine is the least proven part and the least trusted one.**

**Negative**
- **Two object stores, an index, a tree and a read service** are a great deal to run for a deployment that has not
  yet run the mechanism.
- **The lock has to be extended for ever**, and a lapse is silent until someone looks.
- **Three fault domains under two administrations** is a cost many deployments will declare away (D-0076-2).
- **A body's bytes are checked against the log only where the log holds a digest of them** (IN-5).
- **An export of units is a copy no erasure reaches** (EX-3).
- **Reader sets as promoted policy make changing an auditor as slow as a promotion.**
- **Every recommendation of a product here rests on reading and on none of it having been run.**

## Annex A — The index's keys (reference design)

*(Drafter's choice throughout. This annex is the reference's design and not a requirement. §4 is the requirement.)*

One table. `c` is a chain id, `i` a log index zero-padded to sort, `s` a segment number, `d` a SHA-256 in hex.

| Item | `PK` | `SK` | Holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fact | `CHAIN#<c>#SEG#<s>` | `LOG#<i>` | Kind, class, epoch, principal, round, sequence number, the digests it cites, the leaf hash, the object key in the locked store |
| Segment head | `CHAIN#<c>` | `SEG#<s>` | Index range, root, the mark's log index, the corpus cut whose formats it uses |
| Mark | `CHAIN#<c>` | `MARK#<i>` | Kind (*held*, *erased*, *disposed*), range or unit, roots |
| Unit header | `CHAIN#<c>#PRIN#<p>` | `UNIT#<round>` | Fence, seal sequence number, container digest, state, schedule line, hold if any |
| Cut | `CORPUS` | `CUT#<date>#<name>` | Kind, commit, manifest digest, entry count |
| Cut entry | `CUT#<name>` | `PATH#<path>` | `d` |
| Object | `OBJ#<d>` | `HEAD` | Holding, size, lock term, fault domains, last fixity result |
| Journal entry | `JOURNAL#<c>` | `AT#<time>#<id>` | Kind (ingest, fixity, drop, read), subject, reader and reader set for a read |

| Index | Partition key | Sort key | On |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GSI1**, a principal's facts | `CHAIN#<c>#PRIN#<p>` | `ROUND#<r>#SEQ#<n>#LOG#<i>` | Facts |
| **GSI2**, a class's facts | `CHAIN#<c>#CLASS#<class>` | `LOG#<i>` | Facts |
| **GSI3**, what cites a digest | `DIGEST#<d>` | `CHAIN#<c>#LOG#<i>` | One item per digest a fact cites |
| **GSI4**, a path across cuts | `PATH#<path>` | `CUT#<date>#<name>` | Cut entries |
| **GSI5**, units by state | `CHAIN#<c>#STATE#<state>` | `PRIN#<p>#UNIT#<round>` | Unit headers |
| **LSI1**, a chain's facts by epoch | `CHAIN#<c>#SEG#<s>` | `EPOCH#<e>#LOG#<i>` | Facts |

| Operation (§7 RD-4) | Key condition |
|---|---|
| Facts by index range | `Query` `PK = CHAIN#<c>`, `SK between SEG#…` for the segments; then `PK = CHAIN#<c>#SEG#<s>`, `SK between LOG#<i1> and LOG#<i2>` |
| Facts by principal and round | `Query` GSI1, `SK begins_with ROUND#<r>#` |
| Facts by class | `Query` GSI2 |
| Facts by epoch | `Query` LSI1 within each segment the epoch spans |
| Facts that cite a digest | `Query` GSI3 `PK = DIGEST#<d>` |
| Marks; two roots for a consistency proof | `Query` `PK = CHAIN#<c>`, `SK begins_with MARK#` |
| A unit's header | `GetItem` `CHAIN#<c>#PRIN#<p>`, `UNIT#<round>` |
| Erasures not complete; unexplained headers | `Query` GSI5 on the states that mean them |
| A cut's files | `Query` `PK = CUT#<name>` |
| A path's digests across cuts | `Query` GSI4 `PK = PATH#<path>` |
| An object by digest | `GetItem` `OBJ#<d>`, `HEAD` |
| The journal; one reader's reads | `Query` `PK = JOURNAL#<c>`, by time. A reader's own history is a further index, not drawn here |

A unit's records are not in the table. They are read from the governed store by the unit's container key.
