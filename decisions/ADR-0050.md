# ADR-0050 — An operator is a principal: seated by promotion, renewing within its domain under a lease, and able to deny without one

**Status:** Proposed · 2026.10.04
**Status note:** Confirmation required under **C1** (it changes who may put a lease on the log, which is the path
every other authority travels on), **C3** (ADR-0031 treats a grant as an operator's act at any time; §2 gives first
leases to a quorum) and **C4** (it answers CR-34 and part of `mechanism/` G5). C2 does not apply: no confinement claim

## Context

Principals' actions are typed, lifted, floored and bundled. The requests that create their leases are not. At the
reference an operator is whoever can open a socket (`ops.rs`; G5).

| Request | Effect on authority | Signed | What stands behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Issue` | Adds | By anchors | The rules floor |
| `BreakGlassApprove` | Adds | By the roster | The replicated state machine |
| `Grant`, `Launch`, `Renew` | Adds: a lease, or more of one | No | Apply-time checks |
| `GatesPassed` | Adds: unlocks votes | No | Nothing (G8) |
| `RegisterProfile` | Adds: a profile a lease may name | No | That the profile was promoted |
| `Revoke`, `FenceEpoch`, `EndRound` | Subtracts | No | Nothing; the attribution is free text |

Three things follow.

- **Five requests add authority on a file's permission bits.** Every other addition in the corpus is consensus-ordered
  and made by a quorum or by a principal inside an envelope a quorum set.
- **A stop is unattributed.** ADR-0028's F2 asks that every stop be attributed, and the field that carries it is a
  string the caller types.
- **The task is not on the channel.** ADR-0043 §1 makes the task the first item of a Thinker's ingress and says it is
  the grant's. `Grant` and `Launch` carry no task, so at the reference the first item of the closed list arrives by a
  path no record names, and ADR-0047's label for it has nothing to attach to.

The corpus already has the pieces. A human `Mediated` principal holding a seat exists (ADR-0024 §5). A Persona is
attested at the lease boundary (ADR-0016 §6). Lineage independence is an exclusion rule (ADR-0035). Revocation is a
local deny (ADR-0016 §7; ADR-0031 §5). This record applies them to the one actor they were never applied to.

## Decision

### 1. An operator is a principal, holding a seat of kind `operator`

- **A fourth seat kind.** A seat of kind `operator` is declared in the issued rules with a domain, like every seat
  (ADR-0037 §2). Its alphabet is the control alphabet of §4 and nothing else: an operator does not author, vote or
  actuate.
- **Only a human holds one.** No model's Persona binds to an `operator` seat. This is a rule about what may be
  promoted to the seat. The mechanism cannot tell a person from a program holding the person's key, and §8 says so.
- **A person holds one seat** (ADR-0037 §7). Someone who operates and also votes holds two seats and is two principals.
- **An operator is `Mediated`.** A person cannot be confined, so no rung above 0 applies, as for a grant seat
  (ADR-0024 §5). What this record gives is attribution and authorization. It does not give containment, and a person
  with physical access to a host is outside it.
- **An operator is treated as havoc**, like every principal. Nothing below rests on an operator being honest. It rests
  on a quorum having seated the operator, on a peer being present, and on the envelope of §4.

### 2. First leases come from a quorum; an operator renews what a quorum granted

**A principal's first lease is granted by the mechanism when a quorum completes, never by an operator.** There are two
quorums and each has its case.

| First lease of | Comes from | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A seat on a roster the rules name (grant, veto, anchor) at genesis, and the first operators | An anchor issuance (ADR-0025; ADR-0029 §5) | Something must be seated before anything can be promoted |
| Every other principal, operators included | A promotion (ADR-0024) | "Promotion is a grant" |

- **What is promoted is a seating** *(drafter's choice)*: an artifact naming the principal, the seat, the Persona, the
  bundle revision, the confinement profile and rung, the alphabet, and the labels a task for it may carry (§5). When
  the tally completes, the mechanism grants the first lease at a fresh fence. No request from an operator is involved.
- **An operator makes successor grants and renewals.** A **successor grant** is a lease for the principal's next
  round in which every field is the previous lease's or narrower, and only `round` advances. A **renewal** keeps the
  round (ADR-0031 §4). Both are monotone: the operator can keep a quorum's grant going and can narrow it. It cannot
  widen it.
- **Anything else is a first lease.** A different Persona, bundle, confinement profile or seat, or an alphabet with a
  variant the last lease lacked, is not a narrowing (ADR-0031 §4 says none of these is ordered). It needs a seating
  and a quorum.
- **An emergency seating is one of these** ⟨R6⟩ (ADR-0052, Proposed 2026.10.04): prepared by promotion, and activated
  for one round by the break-glass roster's quorum and not by a tally. An operator's `Regrant` or `Renew` naming an
  emergency seat is refused; its topic is in the domain's operators' `W(p)`.
- **So the rate problem does not arise.** A quorum acts once per seating. Round by round, one operator acts.

### 3. An operator has a Persona and a lineage, and its sign-on is checked at the lease boundary

- **An operator's Persona record** binds a signing key, a role, a domain and a lineage. It has no weights, prompt or
  decoder. It is promoted and signed like any Persona (ADR-0016 §1) and carries a sequence number.
- **The lineage's root is the identity provider** that vouches for the person. The lineage is declared and not
  projected: enrolling a credential is not a Build round, so this is ADR-0035's declared lineage (D-0035-1), and its
  corpus rules have nothing to measure. Its root rule does apply: **two operators behind one provider do not have
  independent lineages.**
- **Authentication is delegated to single sign-on, and it is read only when a lease is granted or renewed**
  *(drafter's choice on where; the delegation is the custodian's)*. The Persona is attested at grant and renewal
  (ADR-0016 §6). For an operator the evidence is a sign-on assertion, signed by the provider, that names the
  Persona's key and the fence the mechanism asked it to name. An assertion that does not verify is a refused
  renewal, and the lease lapses.
- **Between boundaries a request is signed with the Persona's key.** The mechanism checks a signature it can check
  itself, and reads no token.
- **No wall clock is read.** The assertion is fresh because it names the current fence, not because of its expiry
  time, and the lease's own `ttl` is at most `T_persona` on the local monotonic clock, as for every lease (ADR-0031
  A2). The corpus's refusal of trusted time (ADR-0045 §6) stands.
- **The provider's key is issued.** It is a key the mechanism accepts a signature from in place of its own check,
  which is ADR-0025 §1 (f). ADR-0045 §1 admits a component whose failure can add only if its identity is issued; this
  is that route. A provider is a trust root for the operators it vouches for, and for no others.

### 4. The control alphabet: adds need a lease and a peer, denies need neither

| Variant | Kind | What it does | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Regrant` | Add | A successor grant to a principal in the operator's domain, with the round's task (§5) | A live lease; a live peer (§6); the target seated and not revoked; every field the last lease's or narrower |
| `Renew` | Add | A renewal of a live lease in the domain (ADR-0031 §4) | The same |
| `AttestGates` | Add | Records that the gates ran on an artifact, with the evidence's digest | A live lease in Build; a live peer |
| `EndRound` | Deny | Ends one principal's round | An operator Persona in force, and either a live lease or a fresh sign-on assertion ⟨R1⟩ |
| `Revoke` | Deny | A targeted revocation (ADR-0031 §5) | The same |
| `FenceEpoch` | Deny | The fleet fence | An operator Persona in force **and a live lease** ⟨R5⟩ |

- **A deny needs no lease** (the custodian's decision). An operator whose lease was fenced or has expired can still
  stop things, and that is the only thing it can do. Its Persona must be in force: promoted and not revoked.
- **Without a live lease, a deny carries a fresh sign-on assertion** ⟨R1⟩ (the custodian's decision).

  | The operator's state | It denies by |
  |---|---|
  | A live lease | Its key alone. The lease was attested at its last boundary (§3) |
  | Lease fenced or expired, Persona in force | Its key, and a sign-on assertion naming the key and the current fence |
  | Persona revoked, or the provider will not vouch | Nothing |

  **A lapsed operator stops its own domain and not the fleet** ⟨R5⟩ (the custodian's decision). `EndRound` and
  `Revoke` in its domain are open to it on a fresh assertion; `FenceEpoch` needs a live lease. The widest stop is
  therefore held only by people attested within their last lease.

  The assertion is the one §3 describes, read here instead of at a lease boundary. A key by itself therefore stops
  being a standing lever: a person removed from sign-on cannot fence the fleet with a key nobody revoked. A provider
  that is down silences the operators whose leases have lapsed, and not those who hold one.
- **A deny is local and attributed.** It needs no quorum and no peer. It is consensus-ordered, as every revocation is
  (ADR-0031 §5), and the signature on it is the attribution ADR-0028's F2 asks for.
- **An operator may not regrant or renew itself** *(drafter's choice)*. Its own successor lease is a peer's `Regrant`.
- **`Regrant` and `Renew` stay inside the domain** the operator's seat declares.
- **`AttestGates` is attributed and still an assertion.** Who runs the gates and how their evidence is authenticated
  is the gap assessment's row 1.7 and is not closed here.
- **`RegisterProfile` stops being a request** *(drafter's choice)*. A profile is promoted; the mechanism registers it
  when the tally completes. `Launch` is the mechanism's part of a first lease or a `Regrant` at rung 1 and above, and
  is not something an operator asks for separately (ADR-0036, pending).
- **`Issue` and `BreakGlassApprove` are not operator variants.** Anchors and the break-glass roster act by their own
  signatures, as now.
- **The floor states the envelope.** The control variants are actions in schema S, and the entries that permit and
  forbid them are floor entries with the same gates as any other. This record says when each holds; the entries are
  an amendment to the floor's records (Open obligations).

### 5. The task is carried by the grant, on the log by digest, with its label

- **A seating and a `Regrant` each carry the round's task** *(drafter's choice)*: its digest and its label on the
  log, its bytes in the round's payload. The Listener delivers the bytes that digest names, as for every other item
  (ADR-0042 §4). This gives ADR-0043 §1's first item a record.
- **The label is the operator's signed declaration.** ADR-0047 §1 requires it to be present. It is now attributed to
  a principal, where it was a field on an unauthenticated request.
- **The seating bounds it** *(drafter's choice)*. A seating names the labels a task for that principal may carry. A
  `Regrant` whose task label is outside them is refused. A quorum can therefore decide that a seat's tasks are always
  `untrusted`, and one operator cannot say otherwise.
- **The task is inside the round's unit of erasure.** Its digest on the log survives, as every admit's does.

### 6. Operators observe each other

- **Every control action is written to the operator's round topic**, and every other operator in the domain is in
  its `W(p)` (ADR-0038 §2). An operator reads what its peers did.
- **A finding is a deny.** An operator that objects to a peer's action revokes it or ends its round, locally and
  under its own signature (§4). No operator can undo a peer's deny.
- **An add needs a live peer.** `Regrant`, `Renew` and `AttestGates` are refused when no other operator in the domain
  holds a live lease. This is the shape of R3 (ADR-0039 §2) applied to operators: it couples an add to a peer's
  presence. It does not couple it to the peer having read anything *(drafter's choice)*; there is no watermark here.
- **At the archetype the peer's lineage is independent**, which means a different identity provider (§3). Peers
  behind one provider are a declared degeneracy (§9).
- **The archetype has at least three operators in a domain** ⟨R5⟩ (the custodian's decision). The peer rule is kept
  as written, with no exemption for regranting a peer, and three is what lets a lapse be recovered without a
  quorum. Two is a declared degeneracy (D-0050-3).
- **Two operators can keep each other seated by renewal, and cannot recover a lapse** ⟨R2⟩. If one of two lets its
  lease lapse, the other has no live peer, so it cannot regrant the first, or anything else. Only a quorum can seat
  the lapsed one again. With three, the two that remain are each other's peers and can regrant the third.
  `spec/Operators.tla` shows it: an operator's next round granted by a peer is unreachable with two operators and
  reached with three.
- **When no operator in a domain holds a live lease, nothing in it can be regranted.** Leases run out, rounds end,
  and the domain stalls until a quorum seats an operator again. That is the corpus's posture: stall, not proceed.

### 7. How it changes

| What changes | By whom |
|---|---|
| Who is an operator; a seating; the labels a seat's tasks may carry | A promotion |
| The first operators; the identity provider's key; the `operator` seats themselves | An anchor issuance |
| A principal's next round, and its task | One operator with a live lease and a live peer |
| A stop | Any operator whose Persona is in force: by key under a live lease, by key and a fresh sign-on without one |
| The control variants and their floor entries | A mechanism release and a promotion, as for any floor change |

### 8. What this record does not cover

- **That an operator is a person.** The seat is for humans by rule. A key can be handed to a program.
- **A person outside the mechanism.** Physical access, the host's root account and the replicas' own traffic
  (`mechanism/` G5, mutual authentication, unbuilt) are untouched.
- **A provider that is compromised.** It can vouch for anyone it serves. Independence between providers bounds this
  to one operator of a pair; a single provider bounds nothing.
- **Gate evidence** (row 1.7), beyond attributing who asserted it.
- **Break-glass and issuance**, which keep their own rosters.
- **What a peer must have read** before an add proceeds (§6).

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **Signed requests under an issued operator roster**, with no seat or lease | Not taken, by the custodian's decision. It authenticates the channel and leaves the operator outside the type discipline every other actor is under |
| **Declare the operator a trust root and stop** | Rejected. It is the present position written down, and CR-34 is the finding against it |
| **A quorum on every grant** | Not taken. A lease is one round, and a quorum of people cannot keep up with Run. First leases take the quorum; successors do not (§2) |
| **A grant envelope approved by an operator quorum**, as a new object | Superseded by the seating. A promotion already is a quorum's grant; a second kind of quorum is not needed |
| **A model may hold an operator seat** | Rejected, by the custodian's decision. An operator's adds would then be a model's, and its alphabet would need a Thinker's whole apparatus |
| **Operators are observed by an `observer` seat**, as Thinkers are | Not taken, by the custodian's decision: operators observe each other. The peer is a principal with the same alphabet and its own lineage, and its finding is a deny it can make itself |
| **A deny needs a live lease**: every privilege goes when the lease does | Rejected, by the custodian's decision. It is the more uniform rule, since an Observer's finding and a veto vote both need a lease. It puts the identity provider on the path of every stop: a provider outage, a fleet fence or the last operator's expiry would leave nobody able to stop anything |
| **A deny by key alone, with no lease** | Rejected at R1, by the custodian's decision. It was the first draft. A key nobody revoked would stay a fleet-wide stop for ever, and removing a person from sign-on would not remove it |
| **A lapsed operator may fence the fleet** | Rejected at R5, by the custodian's decision. It was the rule from R1 to R4. The widest stop is limited to people attested within their last lease |
| **Exempt the regrant of a peer from the peer rule** | Rejected at R5, by the custodian's decision. Two operators would heal themselves, and one would act alone exactly when its only watcher is gone. The archetype has three |
| **The identity provider's assertion on every request** | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. It puts the provider on the hot path and its expiry clock in the admit path. A key signs requests; the provider is read at the lease boundary |
| **Self-renewal** | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. It would let one operator keep itself seated with no peer looking |
| **The task off the log** | Not taken *(drafter's choice)*. The first item of ingress would stay unrecorded, and ADR-0047's label would have nothing to name |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **No lease is on the log without a quorum behind it**, directly or through a monotone chain from one.
- **The operator is under the same discipline as everyone else**: a seat, a Persona, a lineage, a lease, a floor.
- **A stop is attributed**, by a signature and not by a typed string.
- **A stop is available to any seated operator who can still sign on**, lease or no lease, and to no one who cannot.
- **The task has a record**, and its label has an author and a bound a quorum set.
- **Single sign-on is used without trusting a clock**, and enters the trusted base by issuance.
- **ADR-0035's independence rule does the work for operators** that it does for Personas, with no new rule.

**Negative**
- **Changing a principal's Persona or bundle takes a seating and a quorum.** An incident deny bundle already needs a
  promotion; reaching each principal now rides that tally and cannot be an operator's quick regrant. This is slower
  than the reference, on purpose.
- **Three operators are needed in every domain at the archetype** ⟨R5⟩, and two identity providers. Two operators
  can do everything but recover a lapse (§6; D-0050-3).
- **A person must re-attest every `T_persona`**, which is an hour at the placeholder value. That number was chosen for
  models.
- **A fleet fence leaves nobody able to regrant.** Recovery is a quorum's work. It is the safe direction and it is
  the slow one, at the moment a deployment most wants to be fast.
- **Any one operator can stop a domain, and any one with a live lease the fleet** ⟨R5⟩. That is the corpus's
  direction of safety, and it is a lever for denial of service held by every seated operator who can sign on.
- **A lapsed operator's stop depends on its identity provider.** If the provider is down, only operators holding a
  live lease can stop anything, and their leases run out within `T_persona`.
- **Human-only is unenforceable.** It is a promotion rule and a declared premise.
- **Many accepted records move.** The debt line is long because the grant path is under everything.
- **One author, two days.** The module and its proof are the drafter's as well (§10).

## Findings

1. **The task was never on the operator channel.** ADR-0043 §1 calls it the grant's, and no grant carries it. Row 1.6
   was written as a gap in typing; part of it is a missing item.
2. **The operator requests are typed and two of them are signed.** CR-34's "untyped" is accurate about the envelope
   and the author, and not about the wire. The gap is five adding requests with no author and no stated envelope.
3. **Renewal across rounds had no name.** ADR-0031 has a renewal, which keeps the round, and a grant, which is
   everything else. The successor grant is the case between them: a new round and nothing wider. Naming it is what
   lets a quorum's grant be continued without a quorum.
4. **An identity provider is a lineage root.** Treating it as one turns "one provider collapses a quorum" from a
   caution into an instance of a rule the corpus already has.
5. **"Deny without a lease" is the exception in the corpus, not the rule.** ADR-0016 §7 calls Persona revocation
   "local, no consensus, no floor exception", which says a deny needs no quorum. It does not say a deny needs no
   credential: an Observer's finding and a veto vote both need a live lease. The first draft of this record read the
   precedent too widely. R1's rule keeps the stop and asks for a credential that is fresh, by the lease or by the
   assertion.
6. **The peer rule is weaker than Observer coupling.** It reads presence and not progress, which is exactly what
   ADR-0039 replaced for Thinkers ("liveness is progress, not presence"). It is taken here because people do not
   emit heartbeats with watermarks. Whether they should is open.
7. **The peer rule makes two operators brittle.** The module found it as an unreachable witness and not as a
   violation: nothing unsafe happens, and a pair that loses one member to an expired lease is stuck until a quorum
   acts. The rule could exempt the regrant of an operator, so that one may bring its peer back alone. That would let
   a single operator act alone at the moment its only watcher is gone, which is what the rule is for. It is left as
   written, and the number is stated.
