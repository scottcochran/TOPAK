## Context

**The corpus says where the record is kept and nothing about the place.** ADR-0005 §1 gives the system of record
as "The archive (ADR-0004), with its governed plaintext store and the forensic horizon". ADR-0004: "Log the hash and a
pointer for cognitive payloads; keep plaintext in a separately governed store built for deletion." ADR-0003 A3:
"The archive is a separate store fed by that ETL and is not a tier of the topic: it is read under its own authority
(ADR-0004) and neither extends a topic's life nor survives its erasure." ADR-0045's T-0045-7 trusts it to retain
"what was written, for the declared horizon, under the declared audit authority". ADR-0005 §5 then says "The
archive does not exist at the reference".

**No record says what the ETL takes, when, or on whose word**, or what the "own authority" is. Two accepted records
carry debts against a store that was never specified: ADR-0034 for attestation evidence, and, Proposed, ADR-0035 for
lineage.

**The records give two answers to the question of which store is the record, and both are right about something.** ADR-0005
makes the archive the system of record. ADR-0024 §8 says of a vote that "Neither the topic nor the emission is the
record; the log is." The first is about keeping and the second about authorship. §1 puts them together: the log
says what happened, and the archive is where what it said is kept.

**The corpus keeps itself in a repository on a carrier, and that is all.** A checkpoint is "one cut, never revised"
and a manifest measures it. ADR-0029 §5 puts "the corpus manifest at bootstrap" in the genesis bundle, and T-0045-11
trusts that "The records the mechanism pins are the records that were ratified". The manifest is a list of
digests. The bytes it measures are wherever the carrier has them, and ADR-0069 §9 C-5 (Proposed) says of a carrier
that its identifiers "locate and do not identify". No record says the corpus is kept anywhere. The custodian asked
that every revision and checkpoint of the documentation stay reachable ⟨K10⟩ and that the archive be informed by
that design ⟨K12⟩. The documentation service is a projection (ADR-0072 §1), and a projection needs something to be
a projection of.

## Decision

### 1. The archive keeps and never originates ⟨K11⟩

- **K-1. Every item in the archive is a copy**: of an entry the log ordered, of a round topic the mechanism sealed,
  of an object a fact cites by digest, or of a file a manifest measured. Each carries what says which: a log index,
  a round's identity and seal, a digest, a manifest line.
- **K-2. The log says what happened, and the archive is where that is kept.** A fact is in the archive because it
  was in the log. Where an archived copy and the log disagree, the log is right and the copy is a finding. When the
  log no longer holds an entry (§4), the archived copy stands on the mark that covers it and on nothing else.
- **K-3. No program in the archive writes to a source.** The feeders of §3 read the log, the transport and the
  repository. They hold no credential that could write to any of them.
- **K-4. The one exception is the archive's account of itself**: what it ingested, checked, dropped and showed to
  whom (holding E). That is a record about the archive. It is not a fact about any principal's action, and nothing
  reads it to decide anything.

This is what keeps T-0045-7's class. A store that only copies can lose or corrupt a copy, which is "Evidence lost
within the horizon: legibility subtracted, authority untouched". ADR-0076 §6 closes the remaining route, a program
that decides something by reading the archive.

### 2. Five holdings ⟨K12⟩

| | Holding | Source | Unit of ingest | Erasable | Named by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Facts**: every entry of the consensus log, whatever its kind. Admits (F1), stop attributions (F2), votes (F3), issuances (F4), break-glass invocations (F5), erasure events (F6), and every other entry: lease and epoch transitions, seals, and the marks of §4 | The log | A **segment**: a contiguous range of log indices | No | ADR-0015; ADR-0028 §1; ADR-0024 §8 |
| **B** | **Round units**: a sealed round topic with its `after` companion, whole. Envelopes, denials and bodies together: raw emissions, `Actuate` records, the task, the tool list, review commentary | The transport, after the seal | A **unit**: one principal's round in one rules-epoch chain | Yes, whole | ADR-0003 0003.1, 0003.2, A1, A2; ADR-0037 §3; ADR-0042 §3 |
| **C** | **The corpus**: records, checkpoints, manifests, modules and results, the floor, the narrative and its revisions, derived documents, documentation releases (ADR-0071 §8), and the reference trees | The repository, at a manifest | A **cut**: the files one manifest measures | No | ADR-0029 §5; T-0045-11; the checkpoints' convention G-C1 |
| **D** | **Evidence**: an object a fact cites by digest that is neither payload nor corpus. Attestation evidence, gate-evidence files, promoted artifacts and their signatures, the genesis bundle, issued rules, the review attestation; lineage records and corpus-root manifests if ADR-0035 and ADR-0069 are ratified | Where the citing fact's author put it | An **object**, by digest | No, unless it embeds payload, when it is in B | ADR-0034 §3; ADR-0024 §3; ADR-0029 §5, A2.2 |
| **E** | **The archive's journal**: ingests, marks received, fixity results, drops, and reads | The archive | An entry | No | §1 K-4; ADR-0076 §5, §7 |

- **H-1. The list is exhaustive.** A thing a record says is retained, kept or archived belongs to one holding. A
  record that adds such a thing names its holding, or adds one here.
- **H-2. A holding has one source and one feeder** (§3).
- **H-3. Payload is only ever in B.** Holdings A, C, D and E hold no cognitive payload (ADR-0004). An object that
  would be evidence and carries payload is put in the unit it came from, so that an erasure reaches it.
- **H-4. A denial is in B.** A denial is a control-plane fact, and it is not in the log: ADR-0005 §2 has it written
  "by the deciding replica, unordered", to the round topic or its `after` companion. It is kept and dropped with
  its round, as ADR-0003 A2 says of the companion.
- **H-5. Three things are not holdings.**
  - The correlator's store, whose fold state ADR-0051 §6 (Proposed) calls "a cache and never a source".
  - A replica's security log of attempts with no session, which ADR-0003's A2 note leaves outside that record and no
    other record places.
  - The live content store behind a corpus root (ADR-0069 §10.1, Proposed), which is Build's working store. The
    archive keeps the root's promoted manifests, in D.
- **H-6. The corpus is a holding** *(drafter's choice; no record says the corpus is archived)*. A deployment's
  archive holds the cuts its genesis bundle and its promotions cite. During genesis there is no deployment, and §7
  says what holding C is then.

### 3. Each holding is fed by a program that only reads

**A feeder is a program in the repository, named by digest, which reads one source and writes one holding.** The
corpus's phrase for it is ADR-0003's: "Archive is its own ETL per use case."

- **F-1. Facts.** The feeder reads the log in index order and writes segments. A segment is complete: it has every
  entry in its range, in order, with no gap between it and the segment before.
- **F-2. Round units.** The feeder reads a topic after its seal (ADR-0003 0003.2; ADR-0021 §3 for Build's topic
  set; ADR-0042 §5 for Run) and writes the unit as one container. It reads the `after` companion until the mechanism
  marks where it stopped, and writes that as the unit's second member.
- **F-3. A unit's header is a fact, and its container is payload.** The header is the chain, principal, round and
  fence, the seal's sequence number, and the container's digest. It is written to the index (ADR-0076 §4). The
  header says a round existed and what its sealed bytes hashed to, and survives the unit. This is ADR-0004's
  hash and pointer.
- **F-4. Capture does not touch the topic.** A topic is dropped on its own schedule whether or not it was captured
  (ADR-0003 A3: the archive "neither extends a topic's life"). A topic that expires uncaptured is lost to the
  archive, and its header says *not captured*.
- **F-5. Which units are captured is declared per use case** (ADR-0003 0003.4). **At the archetype every unit is
  captured** *(drafter's choice: ADR-0028 has it that "The archetype log remains the maximum projection", and
  ADR-0037 §3 retains raw emission at the archetype)*. A deployment that captures fewer declares it (§9).
- **F-6. The corpus.** The feeder is given a manifest and a commit. It refuses a tree that does not verify against
  the manifest, and writes each file under its digest with the manifest as the cut's index. A file unchanged
  between two cuts is stored once.
- **F-7. Evidence.** When a segment is ingested, each digest its facts cite is looked for. An object found is
  written under its digest. **A digest with no object is recorded as *missing*** and is never passed over in
  silence.
- **F-8. Ingest is idempotent and addressed by content.** Running a feeder twice writes nothing the second time.
- **F-9. A feeder judges nothing.** It does not filter, redact, classify or summarize. Masking is the write path's
  (ADR-0003 0003.6), before the seal. What was sealed is what is kept.

### 4. A segment is held before a mark says so, and the log is not discarded above the mark

- **A-1. A segment is *held* when its bytes are in the locked store in every fault domain the deployment declares**
  (ADR-0076 §2), and each copy hashes to the segment's root (ADR-0076 §5).
- **A-2. The mechanism orders an *archive mark* for a held segment** *(drafter's choice)*: an entry in the log that
  names the segment's range and its root. It is not an admit, a promotion or an issuance, and it enables nothing.
  ADR-0063 (Proposed, overlay) orders a watermark in the same way, when the intent boundary "has archived a segment
  and run its checks", and says the archetype would owe "a record that goes up in segments with a receipt". The
  mark is that receipt.
- **A-3. A replica discards no log entry above the newest mark** *(drafter's choice)*. ADR-0045 §6 leaves log
  compaction "Inside the verified object" and no record decides when it may run. Without this rule a compacted
  prefix could exist nowhere, and "system of record" would name a store that had not yet received the record.
- **A-4. A mark is checked, not believed.** A replica proposes a mark only for a root it has recomputed from its own
  log over that range. A wrong root from the archive is refused, and no mark is ordered.
- **A-5. An unmarked log grows.** If the archive is down, nothing is marked, and replicas keep their entries. That
  costs storage and, at the limit, availability. It never costs an entry.
- **A-6. The marks themselves are facts**, in holding A, in the next segment.
- **A-7. A mark has a kind.** *Held* is this section's. *Erased* and *disposed* are ADR-0075's (§3 ER-2, §5 DP-2),
  and record that a unit's container is gone. **A-4 does not reach those two.** A replica can recompute a root and
  cannot see that bytes are absent from a store, so an *erased* or *disposed* mark records the archive's report.
  A false one says a unit is gone when it is not, which is T-0045-7's residual, "An erasure that fails to delete",
  now with a mark that claims otherwise. ADR-0076 §5's audit is what looks for it.

### 5. What the archive keeps for the documentation ⟨K10⟩ ⟨K12⟩

- **C-1. Every cut is kept whole and verifies as cut.** A manifest is checked "against the tree it measured, not
  against today's" (`manifests/README.md`), and the archive holds that tree, so the check needs no carrier.
- **C-2. Every revision of a file is reachable**: by its digest, by its path in a cut, and across cuts as the
  sequence of digests one path has had.
- **C-3. A cut is never preferred for being newer.** The archive has no "current" cut. Which checkpoint is the
  state of record is the corpus's statement (`checkpoints/README.md`), kept in the cut that makes it.
- **C-4. The documentation service reads holding C and nothing else** (ADR-0072 §1 P-1). Its store can be rebuilt
  from C. C is rebuilt from nothing, which is the difference between a projection and a record.
- **C-5. What a manifest excludes is not kept by it.** `manifests/EXCLUDE` leaves the two guides, the diagrams and
  the READMEs unmeasured. A documentation release (ADR-0071 §8) measures the unit files. What no manifest measures
  is in no cut, and holding C says nothing of it.

### 7. During genesis

*(Drafter's recommendation.)* There is no log, no round and no deployment, so holdings A, B and D are empty at
every site but the reference's tests. **Holding C can exist now**: an archive of the corpus's cuts, fed from the
repository by manifest, under the custodian's custody (1, 0). It would make G1 to G11 readable as cut without a
carrier, and it would be the first thing the documentation service projects. Its trust is T-0045-11's and no
better: one person cuts the manifests it is fed.

### 8. The trusted base

**T-0045-7 stays delegated**, and §1 is why. Three things here are new and none is a row:
- **The feeders** read and copy. A feeder that fails loses or mangles a copy, and A-4 and F-7 make each visible.
- **The mark** is ordered by the mechanism on its own recomputation (A-4). The archive's word is not taken.
- **Holding C's feeder** stands behind T-0045-11 and adds nothing to it.

ADR-0076 Annex B proposes that T-0045-7 become two rows, since "The archive and plaintext store" are two stores
with opposite duties.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **The store five records lean on has a description**: what is in it, where each thing came from, and on whose
  word.
- **"The record" has one meaning.** The log authors and the archive keeps, and a disagreement between them has a
  rule.
- **The log can be compacted without losing the record**, and cannot be compacted before the record is safe.
- **The archive cannot be made to say something happened.** It holds copies, and the mark is the mechanism's.
- **The corpus has a home that is not a carrier**, and every checkpoint verifies there as cut.
- **A missing piece of evidence is a recorded fact**, and not an absence someone has to notice.

**Negative**
- **The mechanism gains a log entry and a constraint on compaction**, and neither has a module or a record.
- **Replicas' storage is coupled to the archive's health** (A-5). A long archive outage fills disks.
- **Capturing every unit is large.** ADR-0037 already says raw emission "is the largest payload a round produces".
- **A captured unit is a second copy of erasable data**, which ADR-0075 then has to reach.
- **Holding C duplicates a repository** during genesis, to buy independence from a carrier that has not failed.
- **Denials die with their round.** After an erasure, the evidence that containment refused something in that round
  is gone, and ADR-0020 calls denials "the only evidence containment did anything".
