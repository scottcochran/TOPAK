# ADR-0018 — The typed lift splits: decoder in the Persona, validator in the mechanism

**Status:** Accepted (genesis) · 2026.09.12
**Pending:** none. E1 ratified 2026.09.23 (Batch 1, errata).
**Amends in effect:** the 09.12 scope correction on `Contain` — refines it rather than
overturning it
**Depends on:** ADR-0007 (semantic judgment is never a lift), ADR-0010 (verified object),
ADR-0016 (Persona), ADR-0004 (control-plane facts separated from cognitive payloads)

---

## Context

The 09.12 scope correction assigned syntactic envelope enforcement to `Contain` and semantic
concerns to Observer detection. Applied naively to the typed lift, that puts emission parsing
inside the mechanism.

Emission format is a model property. It changes when the Persona changes. The mechanism is the
verified object and changes at release cadence. Putting model-specific parsing in the mechanism
re-creates precisely the rate mismatch ADR-0010 exists to prevent, one level down — and worse,
it makes the verified object depend on the occupant, which inverts the thesis.

---

## Decision

**The typed lift is two components with different cadences, different owners, and different
trust requirements.**

| Component | Owner | Cadence | Model-specific | Trusted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decoder | Persona | Persona promotion | Yes | **No** |
| Validator | `Contain` mechanism | Release | No | Yes |

The **decoder** parses raw emission into candidate typed actions. It ships inside the Persona,
signed and versioned with it.

The **validator** is total over the floor's action alphabet. It stays in the mechanism and is
verified once. `Contain` never learns any model's output format; it knows the alphabet, which
is its own.

### Why the decoder need not be trusted

ADR-0007's syntactic discipline guarantees a finite, totally-extracted action alphabet, which
makes the validator total. A buggy or hostile decoder cannot produce an inadmissible action,
because there is no inadmissible action the validator accepts. Failure to parse is a deny —
local, no consensus, monotone.

Decoder trust therefore buys nothing and is not required. The verified object is unchanged by
this ADR, which is the test it had to pass.

### The downstream artifact chain

| Artifact | Nature | Admission role |
|---|---|---|
| Logits / top-k logprobs | Side channel, optional | Decoding attestation — may admit (ADR-0019) |
| Raw emission | Cognitive payload | None — it is the decoder's input |
| Typed action | Control plane | Boundary event; may admit |
| Interpreted output | Cognitive | **Deny-only**, per ADR-0007 |

### Reader scope correction

The Reader does not perform the typed lift. The lift is decoder plus validator, neither of
which is a principal seat. The Reader produces *interpretation*.

Three things follow immediately:

- The Reader is a cognitive seat, so it carries its own Persona under ADR-0016 §5.
- The seat-distinctness predicate applies to it, not only to the Thinker and Observer.
- Reader output is semantic, therefore deny-only. A Reader's interpretation may trigger
  quarantine; it may never satisfy an admission precondition.

An interpreted finding that triggers quarantine crosses into the control plane as a **fact** —
finding type, severity, epoch, principal — carrying no payload. That is ADR-0004's second
instance, and should be cited there rather than re-derived.

---

## Consequences

### Positive

- The verified object stays model-agnostic. The pinning claim and the cadence separation both
  survive contact with real emission formats.
- Adding a model family is a Persona promotion, not a mechanism release.
- It is the third instance of the ADR-0010 pattern — verified invariant plus versioned
  parameter — now applied to syntax after policy and deployment. Worth naming in the article as
  one move applied three times rather than three separate cleverness.

### Negative

- **The validator guarantees admissibility, not fidelity.** A hostile or broken decoder can
  mistranslate one admissible action into another — *read A* becomes *read B*, both within
  policy. No total validator catches this, and no amount of validator work will.

  The answer is placement, not detection: the decoder is part of the Persona, so its output is
  the principal's action, attributed to the principal's lease and epoch. The record keeps *this
  principal emitted admissible action Y*. It loses *the model meant X*.

  This is ~~ADR-0016 §8~~ ADR-0020's ⟨E1⟩ concession arriving from a second direction. That convergence is worth
  stating plainly — boundary-only replay is not a local compromise, it is where the system's
  epistemic limit actually sits.

- **Persona gains a proof obligation**: the decoder targets the floor's action alphabet.
  Checkable at promotion, since both are fixed at that moment.

---

## Open

- **Raw emission retention.** Retaining it allows after-the-fact verification that the lift was
  faithful; not retaining it shrinks the erasure surface. Directly between ADR-0003 and
  ADR-0006, and currently unassigned.
- **Decoder–alphabet compatibility.** The floor's alphabet changes at deploy cadence; decoders
  change at Persona cadence. A floor change can invalidate every resident decoder, which implies
  a compatibility matrix and a promotion check, not just a pairwise test.
- **Does the validator belong to the floor or the mechanism?** It is total over the floor's
  alphabet, which argues for coupling; it is model-agnostic and release-cadence, which argues
  for the mechanism. Consequential for ADR-0014. **Explicitly not blocking:** both candidate
  homes sit on the mechanism side of the Persona boundary, so the split this record decides
  holds either way. Noted at ratification so a later reader does not mistake an open question
  for an unratified premise.
