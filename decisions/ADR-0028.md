# ADR-0028 — The forensic floor: authority facts survive erasure and break-glass

**Status:** Accepted (genesis) · 2026.09.23 · confirmation required (ADR-0029 C1) ⟨B4⟩
**Status note:** Confirmation required under C1: the forensic floor is a proof obligation over
privileged paths, and NC-2 (ADR-0013 §4) is checked against the class list in §1. Four open
obligations are carried into confirmation rather than cleared first — the external auditor role,
raw-emission retention, F1's volume, and the re-genesis horizon. Until confirmed, anything
published citing this record's guarantees says "author-ratified".
**Ratification phase:** Genesis (ADR-0029 §2) ⟨R3⟩. **Confirmation required** on ratification —
C1 (proof obligation over privileged paths). Moved from post-bootstrap 2026.09.23, with ADR-0013;
the structural reviewer that phase would have supplied is a debt the confirmation promotion repays.
**Split from:** ADR-0013, by decomposition
**Depends on:** ADR-0004 (control-plane / payload split), ADR-0006, ADR-0010, ADR-0020
**Depended on by:** ADR-0013 (NC-2)
**Resolves:** the NIST Playbook MANAGE 2.4 contradiction (preserve materials for forensic review vs ADR-0006 erasure)

---

## Context

ADR-0006 makes erasure a modeled event. Its mechanism is deletion of sealed round topics (ADR-0003)
plus deletion from the separately governed plaintext store (ADR-0004). Crypto-shredding was rejected
as the primary mechanism. MANAGE 2.4 asks that materials be preserved for forensic, regulatory, and
legal review. As drafted, nothing prevents erasure from reaching the evidence of who exercised
authority.

Three later findings converge on one answer:

- **Stop attribution is a feedback path, not merely evidence** (floor-rollout session, 09.13).
- **Legibility, not immutability, is the archetype log property.** An admitted boundary event must
  remain readable by the declared audit authority for the declared horizon (log-immutability
  session, 09.13).
- **Votes and issuances are the authority chain** (ADR-0024, ADR-0025, ADR-0015 revision).

The 09.12 external assessment observed that a forensic floor has the same monotone structure as the
policy floor, ~~running in the opposite direction~~ with change one-directional in both: the policy
floor caps authority (bundles may only subtract), and the forensic floor guarantees retention
(exceptions may preserve more, never less) ⟨E1⟩. This record takes that option.

## Decision

### 1. Forensic-floor data classes

These classes are non-erasable for the declared horizon:

| Class | Source |
|---|---|
| **F1** Admit decisions (grants), with bundle version and epoch | ADR-0015 |
| **F2** Stop attribution: e-stop, lease-expiry eviction, `deny=all` | Floor-rollout session |
| **F3** Promotion votes | ADR-0024 |
| **F4** Anchor issuances, including genesis (epoch 0) | ADR-0025, ADR-0027 |
| **F5** Break-glass invocations | ADR-0009 |
| **F6** Erasure events themselves | ADR-0006 |

All six are **control-plane facts** (ADR-0004) and **boundary events** (ADR-0020). **No cognitive
payload is in the floor.** Prompts, reasoning, and review commentary remain erasable.

### 2. Enforcement reuses the policy floor

Each class is a **policy-floor denial on the erasure action's envelope**. Consequences:

- **Bundles may only narrow erasure further**; this is monotone.
- **Removing a class, or shortening the horizon, is a floor relaxation.** It requires an anchor
  issuance plus a promotion (ADR-0026 §5).

No new primitive is introduced.

### 3. Legibility horizon

Each class carries a declared horizon and a declared audit authority.

- **Archetype:** horizon is the lifetime of the rules-epoch chain. Audit authority is the anchor
  plus a declared external auditor role.
- **Shortening either** is a legibility degeneracy (ADR-0023).

### 4. Compartmentation is not erasure

Removing a class from a reader set (the SCI pattern) is admissible without relaxation, provided the
declared audit authority remains a reader.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Preserve everything (no erasure) | Rejected. Contradicts ADR-0006 and legal erasure obligations |
| Preserve payloads too | Rejected. Payloads carry the legal exposure; ADR-0004 exists to separate them |
| A separate forensic mechanism | Rejected. The policy floor already has the monotone shape; reuse it |
| Crypto-shred payloads, keep hashes | Not needed. ADR-0004 already keeps hash and pointer in the log |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **The MANAGE 2.4 contradiction becomes a recognized tradeoff with a mechanism.** Facts are
  preserved; payloads are erasable.
- **Demand types 1–4 remain satisfiable for payloads.**
- **NC-2 in ADR-0013 becomes a checkable property** over a finite class list.

**Negative**
- **Demand type 5 is structurally excluded at the archetype.** F6 records the erasure. See ADR-0013
  §5.
- **Retention cost grows with principals × rounds for F1.** Admit decisions are the high-volume
  class, which makes this the first candidate for a horizon degeneracy.
- **HIPAA's six-year retention and disclosure accounting** are additive requirements this floor
  does not express. The archetype log remains the maximum projection. That is still unresolved
  from 09.13.

## Bootstrap note

Genesis rules issue **no erasure roster**. Erasure is therefore disabled from epoch 0 until this
record and ADR-0013 are ratified. This is fail-closed, and it costs nothing, since at
bootstrap there is nothing to erase.

**Both records moved to the genesis phase on 2026.09.23** ⟨R2 · R3⟩. This record and ADR-0013 may
therefore be ratified before bootstrap, in that order: the floor first, then the mechanism whose
NC-2 is checked against it (ADR-0013 §6).

**Ratifying them does not by itself enable erasure** ⟨R3⟩. The genesis rules issue no erasure roster
because ADR-0029 §5, ceremony step 5, says so, and that is a clause of an Accepted record rather
than a consequence of either record's phase. Issuing a roster at epoch 0 would require amending
ADR-0029. The fail-closed posture argues against it: at bootstrap there is nothing to erase, and the
first erasure roster can be issued after bootstrap under ADR-0026 §4's two-key rule, by which point
both records have been through confirmation.

## Open obligations

- Define the external auditor role (GOVERN 2.3).
- Settle raw-emission retention between 0003 and 0006. F1 is decided here; raw emissions are not.
- State F1's volume in the cost model.
- **The horizon does not survive re-genesis** ⟨R1⟩. §3 ties the archetype horizon to the lifetime of
  the rules-epoch chain, and ADR-0027 §4 starts a new chain at re-genesis, which follows anchor loss
  or capture. The rule that a successor inherits read access to its predecessor's forensic classes
  is carried as an ADR-0025 re-genesis-migration obligation, and must be settled before this
  record's confirmation promotion (S6-14).
