# ADR-0064 — An autonomous plane's clock is its ticks: numbered from the charter, each signed by a quorum of replicas on their own clocks, with every interval a count of ticks from a named tick

**Status:** Proposed · 2026.11.10
**Status note:** No confirmation criterion applies on the drafter's reading. C1: the rules in §2 are conditions on a
replica's vote and add no grant. C3: ADR-0032 is kept, with a residual (§5). **It carries a premise about time, and
is not a safety theorem in the lattice analysis's sense** (§4). **Modelled, checked and proved in TLA+ ⟨R1⟩ (§8), in
discrete time and under §4's premise; nothing is built.** §4's argument changed once while it was drafted (Findings,
1).
**Pending:** none.

ADR-0063 (the first bound), ADR-0066 (a release act's freshness), ADR-0045 (the clock's row, and finding 2)
**Closes:** nothing.
**Carries a debt against:** nothing while this is an overlay. §7 lists what the archetype's records would owe.

## Context

ADR-0063 bounds revocation in effects. **A count does not stop a plane that idles and then acts on an old intent.**
The world an envelope was voted for changes with time, and a bundle's time windows need a clock under any design.
The custodian took a clock held by a quorum as the second bound, for duration ⟨13⟩, and a governor's own clock as a
watchdog only ⟨18⟩.

**The first revision asked that every clock be sound.** Each replica and each governor read an interval on its own
clock, and one slow clock held authority open. It also did not say when an interval starts, and an interval that
starts at receipt can be started late by whoever holds the message. That is the NATO reading's F1.

---

## Decision

### 1. The plane's clock is its ticks

Each replica keeps **one reading of its own monotonic clock**, taken when it applied the plane's charter.
Everything else is in the log.

- **A tick is a numbered entry.** Tick *τ* says that *τ* periods of `T_tick` have passed since the charter was
  applied. The numbers need not be consecutive. The charter gains `tick`, the period.
- **Ticks are numbered from the charter and not from the tick before** (Findings, 1).

### 2. Two rules on a replica's vote

- **A replica never signs a tick ahead of its own clock.** It signs tick *τ* only when its own elapsed time has
  reached *τ* periods.
- **A replica never signs a grant behind its own clock.** It signs no lease and no `ordered` admit while, by its own
  clock read with the early margin (ADR-0031 E1), the next tick is due.

**The second rule carries §4's bound, and the first carries availability** ⟨R1⟩. A tick ahead of real time moves an
expiry and an extent nearer, which only subtracts. What the first rule stops is a captured or fast quorum burning a
plane's intervals, which is ADR-0032's liveness side.

**No time crosses a boundary.** A replica reads its own clock and signs or does not. No timestamp is exchanged and
no external time is read, so ADR-0032 §3 holds as written.

### 3. Every interval is a number of ticks from a named tick ⟨Q13⟩

An intent's `expiry`, a lease's `ttl`, a charter's `expiry`, an entry's duration and a release act's freshness are
tick numbers. So each end is a comparison in the log. It is the same on every replica, and it can be checked again
at apply, which a per-replica clock cannot (ToO §15 found that of `T_obs`).

- **An intent names a tick the plane has handed up**, and its `expiry` counts from that tick.
- **A lease's `ttl` counts from the tick at which the plane ordered the grant**, and ends no later than its
  intent's `expiry`.
- **The first intent counts from the charter's application, tick zero** ⟨Q13⟩. It is granted by the promotion,
  before the plane has ticked.
- **Anything withheld for a day arrives a day old.** That is the answer to F1.

### 4. What that gives, and on what premise

**Premise:** captured and slow replicas together are fewer than a certificate. At seven replicas that is at most
four (ADR-0061 §1). A replica is slow when its clock lags by more than its margin.

Then every certificate includes a replica that is neither captured nor slow. That replica signed only because, by a
sound clock, the latest tick was current. So at every grant the plane's tick count is less than one period behind
real time, and **no grant is made under an intent more than one tick period past its `expiry`**. Since a lease ends
no later than its intent, that bounds effects too, up to §5's residual.

**A fast clock only subtracts.** It refuses grants early, and draws on the silence a plane tolerates.

**This is a declared bound and not a safety theorem.** It has a premise about time, and the lattice analysis keeps
such premises out from under safety theorems. With the premise false this bound fails (`M_TOOSLOW`) ⟨R1⟩, and
ADR-0063's bound still holds.

### 5. A governor's own clock decides one thing: that the next tick is overdue ⟨18⟩

A governor counts an entry's duration in ticks, and checks each tick number against its own elapsed time. **When
the next tick is overdue, the vehicle is alone and the lease ends** (ADR-0062 §5).

**The residual.** A slow governor clock finds a tick overdue late. So it can stretch the entry in progress, and
only when the plane's ticks have also stopped reaching it. This has the shape of ADR-0045's finding 2: a bound on a
declared value, plus drift.

**Overdue is by the governor's own clock** ⟨R1⟩, on the custodian's decision of 2026.11.10: the next tick is overdue
when that clock reaches the tick's due time. With a slow governor the residual is reachable (`NeverStretched` is
violated), and `NoStretchUnlessSlow` says nothing else reaches it.

Under a captured plane the ticks are the captor's, and the duration bound for an honest vehicle rests on that
governor's own clock, which treats a lagging tick as overdue.

### 6. The trades are declared

| Value | Small | Large |
|---|---|---|
| `T_tick` | A short loss of signal ends a lease | A longer stretch for an entry in progress when the plane is gone |
| `T_intent`, an intent's life in ticks | A plane stopped at `K_up` gives up waiting for the link sooner | An idle plane may act on an older intent |

`T_tick` has to be long against a round of the plane over its radios, or ticks fall behind and leases end with no
adversary present. Both values are placeholders.

### 7. Overlay position, and what is reported upstream ⟨Q11⟩

Reported under ADR-0058 §6: **a clock that is a quorum's fact and a log quantity.**

**If the archetype takes it, its records owe:** ADR-0032, a third row beside "local clock" and "trusted time", for
an interval read by a quorum of local clocks; ADR-0031, a `ttl` in ticks for a plane that has them; ADR-0045, the
clock's row and finding 2, restated for §5's residual.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **Each checker's own clock, with a margin**, the first revision | Not taken ⟨13⟩. Every clock is then a trust root in the slow direction |
| **A trusted time source** | Not taken. ADR-0045 refused one |
| **Timestamps exchanged and a median taken** | Not taken. A timestamp from another party is content (ADR-0032 §3). Here a replica signs or does not, and no time value is exchanged |
| **Ticks measured from the tick before** | Rejected (Findings, 1). `M_RELATIVETICK` |
| **An interval that starts at receipt** | Rejected. `M_RECEIPT`, the NATO reading's F1 |
| **The first intent counts from the first tick read above** | Not taken ⟨Q13⟩. One rule for every plane, at the price of a plane seated long before it deploys spending part of its first intent waiting |
| **No duration bound** | Not taken ⟨13⟩. An idle plane could act on an intent of any age |
| **Derive duration from the log's order** | Not available. "Causality orders events and bounds no interval" (ADR-0032) |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **One slow clock no longer holds authority open.** It takes a certificate's worth of captured and slow replicas.
- **Every interval has an origin an adversary cannot move.**
- **An expiry is a log comparison**, the same on every replica and checkable again at apply.
- **A governor's own clock is a watchdog**, and its failure stretches one entry.

**Negative**
- **A premise about time**, which the corpus had kept out of every bound below ADR-0032 §2's second row.
- **The plane must order one tick per period.** A plane that cannot complete a round in `T_tick` ends its own
  leases.
- **Drift accrues from the charter**, over the plane's whole life, and the margin has to cover it.
- **Under a captured plane the duration bound is one clock's again**, the honest governor's own.
- **One author, one day.**

---

## Findings

1. **Ticks must be numbered from the charter.** As first drafted, each replica measured a period from the tick
   before. An adversary that stalled the plane for a week and then let one tick through would have hidden the week:
   the count would have moved by one. Numbered from the charter, the first tick after a stall carries the week. The
   error was found by writing the rule down, and a module would have found it sooner.
2. **The duration bound and the effects bound fail differently**, and that is why they are two records. One has a
   premise about clocks and the other has none.
