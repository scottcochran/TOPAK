# ADR-0031 — The lease is the composite credential, and its grant is the instant

## Context

Three items were deferred from the corpus for months — the clock model, the trusted computing base, and container
vacuity — and when the 09.17 sessions finally opened them, **all three converged on the lease** (N17-3 §4):

- **Clock.** Lease expiry runs on a local monotonic clock with an asymmetric margin.
- **Container vacuity.** The lease is where a confinement-profile digest would bind.
- **TCB.** The lease's field list, serialized, *is* the enumeration.

A fourth direction reached the same place: the NIST measurement session found the identity gaps converging on a
lease schema. Four features met at one seam, which is why none of them could be closed alone.

**What exists today.** ADR-0022 requires every action to carry epoch and lease. ADR-0016 §6 makes Persona identity
a lease parameter, attested at grant and renewal. B6 uses lease grant as the instant at which a principal's type is
fixed; B13 uses it for the epoch fence. **Nothing says what a lease contains.** A credential that four subsystems
depend on has no schema, and the one boundary that most needs an instant — B12, confinement — has an empty cell.

**Why a schema rather than a field list.** The lease is not a bag of parameters. It is the one object that binds
*who* a principal is, *what* it may do, *where* it runs, and *until when* — and every one of those is an authority
claim. Writing it down makes the trusted base legible, because the fields are exactly what the mechanism believes
without checking again.

## Decision

### 1. The lease is a composite credential with a fixed schema

A lease is granted to a principal for the round it names ⟨R1⟩, and binds:

| Field | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `principal` | The principal's identity | ADR-0002 A2 |
| `seat` | The LTRO seat held for this round | ADR-0016 §5, ADR-0018 |
| `persona` | The Persona ID, attested at grant and renewal | ADR-0016 §6 |
| `epoch` | The epoch this lease is valid at. Any increment fences it | ADR-0022 §2 |
| `fence` | **The Paxos log index at grant.** The lease's position in the consensus order | §2 |
| `rulesEpoch` | The rules epoch in force at grant | ADR-0025, ADR-0026 |
| `bundle` | The bundle version this principal is admitted against | ADR-0010 |
| `confinement` | `[digest, rung]` — the confinement-profile digest and its attestation rung | §3 |
| `alphabet` | The action variants this seat may express | ADR-0007, ADR-0022 §1 |
| `round` | The round this lease is granted for. Never reused per seat ⟨R1⟩ | ADR-0003, §2 |
| `role` | The principal's role in that round: author or voter ⟨R1⟩ | ADR-0022 §7, ADR-0024 |
| `ttl` | Expiry, as a duration on the local monotonic clock | §7 |

**Every field is an authority claim, and the set is closed.** A capability not expressible as one of these fields
is not carried by the lease, and a mechanism that needs one is proposing a schema change, which is a mechanism
release.

### 2. The lease is round-coterminous, and the fence is a Paxos log index

A lease is granted for exactly one round, which it names in `round` ⟨R1⟩, and expires with it. There is no lease that outlives the round it was
granted for, and no round that outlives its lease.

**A principal holds at most one live lease, and a grant requires that none is live.** ⟨C1⟩ This was implicit in
"round-coterminous" and is stated because the check in §8 showed what its absence costs: if a grant may replace a
live lease, §4's monotonicity guarantees nothing at all, since any principal wanting wider authority simply takes
a new grant instead of renewing. Widening authority therefore requires ending the round.

**The fence is the Paxos log index at grant**, not a counter of its own. Ordering between leases, admissions, and
issuances is therefore read from one log rather than reconstructed across three, which is the same move ADR-0015
made for effects. A stale lease is detected by comparing indices, and the comparison is total because the log is
totally ordered.

### 3. Confinement is a lease field, and the grant is B12's instant

The lease carries a **confinement-profile digest** and an **attestation rung**. The digest identifies the
confinement profile the mechanism believes the principal is running under; the rung says how that belief was
established.

| Rung | Attestation |
|---|---|
| **0** | None declared |
| 1 | Asserted by the launcher |
| 2 | Measured boot |
| 3 | Confidential VM with an independent verifier |

**Rung 0 is `Mediated`, always.** A principal whose lease declares rung 0 is `Mediated` regardless of any other
field, and no theorem quantifying over `Contained` applies to it. This is fail-closed: an undeclared confinement
is treated as no confinement.

**This closes B12's instant without a new boundary.** Lease grant is already the instant for B6 and B13, so B12
borrows it. The moment `Contain` knows it is running inside the mechanism it assumed is the moment the lease is
granted, and the evidence is a field in the credential every action already carries.

**What this record does not do.** It does not define rungs 1–3, which need measurement semantics, a re-attestation
trigger, and a *Contested* verdict — signature valid, measurements diverge (Sokolov, arXiv:2608.00801). Those
belong to the attestation-rung record that discharges ADR-0022 §6 (§8). **The schema here is stable across that
record:** filling in rung semantics adds no field.

**Confinement is necessary and not sufficient for `Contained`.** The provenance axis must also clear (N17-2). An
attested external model is still `Mediated` because of prompt egress; an in-house Persona on a shared host is
still `Mediated` because of its substrate. A rung-3 lease does not by itself make a principal `Contained`.

### 4. Authority is monotone across renewal

For successive leases on the same principal, **leaseₙ₊₁ ⊑ leaseₙ**: a renewal may narrow the alphabet,
~~narrow the bundle,~~ ~~lower the rung,~~ or shorten the TTL. **It may never add authority.** A renewal keeps `round`
and `role` unchanged ⟨R1⟩, keeps `bundle` unchanged ⟨R2⟩, and keeps `confinement`, digest and rung, unchanged ⟨A1⟩.
None of the ~~three~~ four is ordered in the sense renewal needs. Author and voter admit
different actions, so a change of role is not a narrowing. Nothing orders two bundles: whether one Rego bundle
denies at least what another denies is semantic containment, undecidable in general, and ADR-0030 §1 removes only
the need to compare a bundle with the floor. A change of any of the ~~three~~ four is a grant.

**A new bundle reaches a live principal by ending its round** ⟨R2⟩. The lease names its bundle revision, and a
decision counts only from that revision (ADR-0033 §5), so a bundle promoted mid-round, including an incident deny,
does not reach a live lease. To apply it, end the principal's round, an ordered subtraction attributed under F2
(ADR-0028), and grant again against the new revision. The round's work in progress ends without a `Submit`. Across
the fleet, an epoch fence (§5) ends every live lease at once.

Adding authority requires a new grant, which is an admit decision at a fresh fence, consensus-ordered like any
other. **This clause is only as strong as §2's one-live-lease rule** ⟨C1⟩: the two must be read together, and the
model check in §8 is what established that. The distinction matters because renewal is a high-frequency path and grant is not: making renewal
non-monotone would put an authority-increasing operation on the hot path, and every other authority increase in
the corpus is consensus-ordered.

A renewal re-attests (ADR-0034 §3.6). If the evidence no longer reaches the lease's rung, the renewal is refused and
the lease lapses; it is never renewed at a lower rung. A lower rung under a live lease is a silent downgrade
(ADR-0034 §3.4): the principal leaves `Contained` without the refusal the grant path makes. ⟨A1⟩

This is monotonicity's sixth appearance, after grants and denials, floor refinement, authority direction,
deployment variation, and the forensic floor.

### 5. Revocation ends the revoked principal's lease; the epoch fences the fleet ⟨R3⟩

~~Persona revocation, confinement-profile failure, or operator revocation **increments the epoch**. The epoch fence
already exists and is enforced by the mechanism rather than by policy (ADR-0022 §2), so in-flight actions under the
old epoch are rejected without consulting a roster or a bundle.~~

**Revocation has two tools, chosen by scope** ⟨R3⟩:

| Tool | Used for | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Targeted revocation** | Persona revocation, confinement-profile failure, operator revocation of a known principal | Ends every live lease the target matches, and bars every later grant it matches. A target is a principal, a Persona digest or a confinement digest. The epoch, and every other lease, is untouched |
| **Epoch fence** | Scope unknown or fleet-wide: e-stop, suspected compromise of the mechanism or anchor, a bundle that must reach every principal now (§4) | Increments the epoch. Every lease at the old epoch is fenced (ADR-0022 §2), and every live round ends |

With one global epoch as the only tool, a single revoked principal ended every live round (ADR-0033 §7, finding 5).
Separating the tools costs one piece of replicated state, the **revocation set**, which every grant is checked
against. Either tool needs it: without it, a revoked Persona is simply granted again.

Both tools are enforced by the mechanism, not by policy, and ahead of the floor. A lease's liveness and its epoch are
mechanism state, so in-flight actions are rejected without consulting a roster or a bundle.

Consequences, all of which reuse ratified machinery:
- **The effect is immediate**, not bounded by remaining TTL. The register recorded passive expiry as an e-stop
  latency of up to one TTL; that path remains as the slow, self-healing case for an unreachable principal.
  Either tool takes effect at its log position, and an admit decided under a lease it ended is void at apply
  (ADR-0033 §4) ⟨R3⟩.
- **The round ends without a `Submit`.** The topic seals on the existing ADR-0003 path.
- **The revocation is attributed** and is stop attribution under the forensic floor's F2 (ADR-0028).
- **Revocation is still a deny** (ADR-0016 §7): monotone, and it needs no quorum, because both tools subtract
  authority. ~~local, no consensus,~~ Both change replicated state, so both are consensus-ordered (ADR-0033 §4) ⟨R3⟩.
- **Break-glass approvals for the seat lapse with a targeted revocation** ⟨R3⟩, as they lapse with the epoch under a
  fence. Otherwise the seat's next holder would inherit them.
- **A revocation is permanent within the rules-epoch chain** ⟨R3⟩. Lifting one would add authority, and no record
  says who may; a re-admitted Persona is a new Persona.

### 6. The lease's field list is the TCB enumeration, serialized

Each field is something the mechanism **believes without rechecking** for the duration of the round. That makes
the schema a per-round trusted-base statement, and it can be derived rather than rewritten: every field is either
discharged by a check at grant, or it is an assumption of use.

| Field | At grant | For the round |
|---|---|---|
| `persona`, `confinement`, `bundle`, `alphabet`, `round`, `role` ⟨R1⟩ | Checked | **Assumed** |
| `epoch`, `fence`, `rulesEpoch` | Assigned | Compared, not assumed |
| `ttl` | Assigned | Assumed to be read on a sound clock (§7) |

This does not replace the TCB declaration record, which covers the mechanism, substrate, and toolchain. It
supplies the per-principal, per-round slice of it, and it is the slice that is mechanically derivable.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Lease TTL independent of the round | Rejected. Two lifetimes require a reconciliation rule at every boundary, and the round already has a beginning and an end |
| A lease counter separate from the Paxos index | Rejected. A second order to reconcile, for no gain; ADR-0015 already made the log the frame |
| Monotone renewal *and* monotone grant | Rejected. Authority must be able to increase somewhere, and grant is the consensus-ordered place for it |
| Revocation by passive expiry only | Rejected (§5). Leaves a revoked principal acting for up to one TTL |
| Confinement deferred to the rung record | Rejected. B12's instant is the thing four sessions converged on; deferring it defers the convergence |
| Round and role outside the lease, in the grant entry at `fence` ⟨R1⟩ | Rejected. The floor keys on both, so they are believed for the round (§6), and keeping them out makes the field list something other than the TCB enumeration. Every renewal entry would also have to restate them, since a renewal sits at a new fence |
| Renewal may narrow the bundle (as first drafted) ⟨R2⟩ | Withdrawn. It names an order between bundles that does not exist (ADR-0033 §7, finding 2) |
| Bundles stack, and a renewal may only append a revision ⟨R2⟩ | Rejected. Monotone by composition, as ADR-0030 §1 is, but the policy in force becomes a function of lease and log position, so an audit has no single policy timeline to trace |
| Fleet-wide deny overlays, ordered in the log ⟨R2⟩ | Not taken. One timeline, and incident denies reach live rounds without ending them; it costs an overlay-head check at apply (ADR-0033 §4). Revisit if ending rounds to apply a deny proves too costly |
| A promotion-attested `narrows` claim between bundles ⟨R2⟩ | Rejected. A semantic claim attested and never checked: the refinement-at-promotion option ADR-0030 rejected, one level down |
| One global epoch as the only revocation tool (as first drafted) ⟨R3⟩ | Superseded. A single revocation ended every live round (ADR-0033 §7, finding 5) |
| A per-seat epoch ⟨R3⟩ | Rejected. Every action carries a pair, topic names and break-glass scoping become per-seat, and it still needs the revocation set, since a fresh grant at the new seat epoch would re-admit the revoked Persona |
| Renewal may lower the rung (as ratified at R3) ⟨A1⟩ | Withdrawn by A1. A lower rung under a live lease leaves `Contained` without a refusal, which ADR-0034 §3.4 forbids at grant; the renewal path was the way round it |
| Fence leases without ending rounds, and re-grant into the same round ⟨R3⟩ | Rejected. Contradicts §2's round-coterminous lease and ADR-0003's topic per epoch |
| The round derived from the lease, identified by the grant's fence ⟨R1⟩ | Rejected. Renewal moves the fence, so an origin-fence field is needed anyway; it gives the role no home; and it re-keys ADR-0003's topic names |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **B12's empty cell is filled** without a new boundary, a new component, or a new instant.
- **One credential, one schema, one instant.** Four subsystems that each needed something from the lease now read
  the same object.
- **The trusted base becomes legible per round**, and derivable rather than authored (§6).
- **`Contained` becomes reachable in principle.** It is still unreachable in fact until the rung record and the
  provenance criterion exist, but the field that carries the evidence is now defined.
- **Renewal is safe on the hot path**, because it can only subtract.

**Negative**
- **The schema is a new fixed surface.** Adding a field is a mechanism release, which is the right price but is a
  real cost at design cadence.
- **The clock assumption is now explicit and load-bearing** (§7). It was always there; this record stops it hiding.
- **Rung 0 will be the common case** for some time, which means `Mediated` will be the common verdict. That is
  honest and will read as a weakness in publication.
- **Tightening a live round ends it** ⟨R2⟩. A bundle promoted mid-round, an incident deny included, applies to a
  principal only at its next grant, so urgent tightening costs the round's work in progress. Fleet-wide, it is an
  epoch fence, which ends every live round (ADR-0033 §7, finding 5).
- **A fence storm is an availability event.** Each epoch fence ends every live round, and fencing is cheap but not
  free. ~~Each revocation fences an epoch.~~ A targeted revocation ends only the rounds it matches ⟨R3⟩.

## 7. The clock assumption, stated

Expiry is a read of a **local monotonic clock with an asymmetric margin**: a lease is treated as expired early
rather than late, so clock error subtracts authority rather than extending it.

**This record does not supply a clock model; ADR-0032 does**, and it generalizes the margin direction decided
here into a rule: an interval whose failure subtracts authority may use a local monotonic clock, and one whose
failure adds or preserves authority needs trusted time. Lease expiry is the first row. What that record also
establishes is that **clocks buy liveness and never safety** — an expired lease its holder still believes in is not
a containment violation, because the epoch fence rejects it whatever the holder's clock says.

Expiry is read locally and then ordered: the first replica to observe it proposes the round's end as a log entry, so
every replica agrees when §2's one-live-lease slot frees (ADR-0033 §4). ⟨E1⟩

The R1 rerun (2026.09.25) repeated every row; `Safe` held over 1,605,720 distinct states, against 436,512 before
the role was added. The `M_ROLE` counterexample grants a lease as author and renews it as voter. The R3 rerun, the

The selective witness is finding 5's case made reachable: a fence, a grant to one principal, then a targeted
revocation of the other, after which the first lease is still live at the current epoch. The `M_REVOKE`
counterexample revokes a principal and then grants to it again.

The fourth witness is the one that matters for §5: without it, `NoActionUnderFencedEpoch` could hold because a
stale lease never arises, and the fence would be protecting nothing.

The A1 run (2026.09.28) repeated every row with its result unchanged, at rungs 0–3; `Safe` holds over 2,136,720
distinct states. `M_LOWER` grants at rung 1 and renews at rung 0. The rung ≥ 1 witness is there so the rung clause
of `Refines` cannot hold only because rung-0 leases were the only ones renewed. ⟨A1⟩

### Finding: §4 was bypassable as first drafted

**The first run failed on the full mechanism**, and the counterexample was not a modeling slip. A `Grant` replaced
a live lease with wider authority, which satisfies every clause as originally written: §4 governs *renewal*, and
this was a grant. The monotonicity guarantee could be avoided by never renewing.

The record implied one lease per round in §2 but never said so, and nothing else in the corpus supplies it.
**§2 now states it** ⟨C1⟩, and the property holds. This is the clause the check bought, and it is the reason to
check a schema before freezing it as a closed set rather than after.

**A1 note (ratified 2026.09.28).** ADR-0034 §3.4 refuses a grant below the deployment's declared rung, and `Confinement.tla`'s
`NoSilentDowngrade` requires every live lease to sit at exactly that rung. §4 as ratified let a renewal lower the
rung, so a principal granted at rung 3 could be renewed at rung 0 and carry on as `Mediated` with no refusal
anywhere. The two records never met in a check, because `Confinement.tla` did not model renewal. It does now
(ADR-0034 R1), and its `M_RENEWLOW` row is this case: the verifier becomes unreachable after the grant, and the
renewal is either refused, so the lease lapses (A1), or renewed at rung 0 (as ratified).

*What A1 does and does not change.* The digest was already fixed across renewal in the reference
(`check_renewal`), but the text did not say so; A1 states it. A1 removes an option from renewal and adds none, so it
adds no authority and weakens no guarantee: the case it changes now lapses, which is a subtraction. On the ground
ADR-0022 A1 and ADR-0035 used, it is not C3. It states how a confinement claim survives renewal, so it is
**confirmation-required under C2**, scoped to A1 as ADR-0003's marking is scoped to A2.

*What ratifying it touched.* `check_renewal` in `mechanism/crates/topak-core/src/lease.rs` refused a raised rung
and permitted a lowered one. It now refuses any change, and `lease_renewal_is_monotone_and_m_renew_is_refused`
refuses `M_LOWER`. This record is re-pinned in `mechanism/corpus-pins.sha256` against that code. A1 was ratified
with ADR-0034, in Batch 10.
