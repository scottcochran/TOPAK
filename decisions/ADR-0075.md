## Context

**The retention rules exist, clause by clause, in a dozen records, and they have never been in one table.** F1 to
F6 are "non-erasable for the declared horizon", which at the archetype is "the lifetime of the rules-epoch chain"
(ADR-0028 §1, §3). Run's topics are kept "per use case" (ADR-0003 0003.4), and Build's "for the production lifetime
of any artifact promoted from that round" (ADR-0021 §7). Raw emission is kept "with the same retention (0003.4)"
(ADR-0037 §3). The inventory made for this set found no length for anything else: the facts outside F1 to F6, the
plaintext in the governed store, attestation evidence, gate evidence, lineage, or the corpus's own files.

**The same records disagree at the edges**, and an archive has to pick.
- T-0045-7 covers "The archive and plaintext store" with one identity, "object lock or equivalent". ADR-0004 wants
  the plaintext "in a separately governed store built for deletion". One store cannot be locked and deletable.
- ADR-0003's Context has it that "Replay requires that nothing ever be removed" and that erasure must destroy
  content "per subject, at arbitrary times, provably".
- ADR-0006 says an erasure is "recorded in the log it modifies", and ADR-0003 that "signatures and hash chains are
  never edited".
- ADR-0028's horizon is the chain's lifetime, and its own open obligation says "The horizon does not survive
  re-genesis".
- ADR-0028 lists "HIPAA's six-year retention and disclosure accounting" as "additive requirements this floor does
  not express", "still unresolved from 09.13".
- The open-findings register's CR-11 notes that the forensic floor keeps who exercised authority, and that "for
  human principals that is personal data".

**The custodian asked that the archive conform to these requirements and to good practice** ⟨K12⟩. Good practice
for records has a plain shape: a schedule that names each class, when its period starts and ends, who may shorten
it, and what happens at the end; destruction that is authorized, complete and itself recorded; and a hold that
stops destruction and never causes it. The corpus's own rule supplies the direction: the forensic floor
"guarantees retention (exceptions may preserve more, never less)" (ADR-0028).

## Decision

### 1. One schedule, a line for every holding ⟨K12⟩

**A deployment's retention schedule is one table in its declaration, with one line for each class below and no
class without a line.** A line has six fields.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Class** | A holding of ADR-0074 §2, or a named part of one |
| **Starts** | The event that begins the period. Always a fact in the log or a seal. Never a date someone enters |
| **At least** | What the class is kept for whatever anyone asks. Shortening it is the change the line's last field names |
| **At most** | When the class must be gone, if a limit applies. Empty for a class that is kept without limit |
| **Ends by** | *never*, *erasure* (§3), *period* (§5), or both |
| **Changed by** | What it takes to shorten *at least*: a floor relaxation, a declaration, or nothing |

- **SC-1. A period is counted from a fact, in the terms the deployment's clock model gives** (ADR-0032). The corpus
  fixes no length, and neither does this record. Where no record supplies a value, the line reads "the deployment
  writes this", and a schedule with such a line unfilled is incomplete.
- **SC-2. *At least* and *at most* are different promises.** The first protects evidence, and the second limits
  exposure. A line may carry both.
- **SC-3. Lengthening *at least* is always allowed**, and is how a deployment meets a retention duty a regulator
  sets (§8). This is ADR-0028's direction, "more, never less".
- **SC-4. The schedule is promoted with the declaration that carries it**, and each line states the basis on which
  the deployment keeps the class *(drafter's choice; the register's CR-11 asks for "a legal-basis line per class")*.
  The basis is the deployment's to write.
- ⟨R2⟩ **SC-5. A line may say where its class may be held.** ADR-0070 lists jurisdiction and residency as a
  boundary with no owner (B25), and gives the practice there as "Residency declared per data class". The schedule's
  classes are those data classes for everything the archive holds. A deployment that must declare residency
  declares it on the line, and chooses the fault domains of ADR-0076 §2 LS-3 and §3 GS-5 within it. **This record
  does not become B25's owner.** Residency of what is outside the archive (the transport, the replicas, a tool's
  target) is not touched.

### 2. The archetype's schedule

| Class | Starts | At least | At most | Ends by | Changed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A-F.** Facts in F1 to F6 | The entry | The life of the rules-epoch chain, and after it as §6 says | None | Never | A floor relaxation: "an anchor issuance plus a promotion" (ADR-0028 §2) |
| **A-o.** Every other log entry | The entry | As A-F *(drafter's choice)* | None | Never | A declaration (D-0075-1). The entry's hash stays in its segment either way (ADR-0076 §5) |
| **B-r.** A Run round unit | The seal (ADR-0042 §5) | The topic's own retention, per use case (ADR-0003 0003.4) | The period the deployment declares for the use case. **The deployment writes this** | Erasure, or period | A declaration |
| **B-b.** A Build round unit | The seal of the topic set (ADR-0021 §3) | "the production lifetime of any artifact promoted from that round" (ADR-0021 §7). For a round nothing was promoted from, as B-r *(drafter's choice)* | The period the deployment declares, counted from the end of that lifetime | Erasure, or period | A declaration, never below ADR-0021 §7 |
| **B-h.** A unit's header (ADR-0074 §3 F-3) | The seal | As A-F | None | Never | As A-o |
| **C.** A cut of the corpus | The cut | For as long as any chain's genesis bundle or promotions cite it. During genesis, without limit | None | Never | Nothing shortens it |
| **D-f.** Evidence a fact cites | The citing entry | As long as the citing fact | None | Never | As the citing fact's line. A deployment that keeps a large artifact's digest and not its bytes declares it (D-0075-2) |
| **D-r.** A corpus-root object returned from Run (ADR-0069 §4, Proposed) | Its intake | "the production lifetime of any artifact promoted from a round that read it" (ADR-0069 RR-4) | — | Period; erasure under ADR-0069 §5 LR-3 if labelled | Not in force |
| **E.** The archive's journal | The entry | As A-F | None | Never | A declaration |

- **AS-1. Every log entry is kept, and not only the six classes** *(drafter's choice)*. ADR-0028 fixes a horizon
  for F1 to F6 and the corpus none for the rest. Keeping all of them is the archetype in ADR-0028's own sense, "The
  archetype log remains the maximum projection", and it is what replay over "boundary events only" (ADR-0020)
  needs. A deployment that keeps less of A-o says so.
  ⟨R2⟩ Keeping every fact is tolerable only because a fact holds little. ADR-0070's row for the boundary between
  facts and payloads (B5) gives its practice as "Minimize by default", and this line leans on that practice.
- **AS-2. A unit is never kept for less time than its topic.** Otherwise the archive would be where a payload went
  to be lost early.
- **AS-3. A header outlives its unit.** That a round existed, under which principal and fence, and what its sealed
  bytes hashed to is ADR-0004's "hash and a pointer", and it is a fact.
- **AS-4. Evidence follows the fact that cites it.** Attestation evidence is cited by a grant, which is F1, so it is
  kept for the chain's life. This is the length ADR-0034 left unstated.
  ⟨R2⟩ ADR-0070 §3 (Proposed) would have a promotion cite more: a bill of materials the gate projects, the second
  builder's evidence, and provenance projected from the sealed round. Its §4 adds a stand-up declaration. Each is
  an object a fact cites, so each is on line D-f. The bill of materials has to be kept: that record's §7.3 turns an
  advisory into a revocation by finding the named component in a promoted artifact's bill, and the question of
  whether an artifact was affected is asked after it has left production as well as before.
- **AS-5. Nothing in class C has a limit.** A cut holds no payload (ADR-0074 §2 H-3). It does hold names: the
  custodian's, and a reviewer's when there is one. That is an open obligation here, as CR-11 is for the floor.
- ⟨R2⟩ **AS-6. A production lifetime ends at a fact.** Line B-b counts from the end of "the production lifetime of
  any artifact promoted from that round", and ADR-0021 §7 does not say what ends one. ADR-0070 §3 (e) gives every
  artifact "A declared end", which is "expiry, revocation or retirement", and its §7.2 makes an artifact with none
  a finding. Each of the three is an entry in the log or follows from one. So the lifetime of a round's artifacts
  ends at the last such entry among them *(drafter's choice)*, which is SC-1's rule that a period starts at a fact.
  If ADR-0070 is not ratified, the end is whichever entry the deployment's declaration names, and a B-b line that
  names none has no *at most*.
- ⟨R2⟩ **AS-7. A return restarts a lifetime.** ADR-0070 §6.7 has it that "return is a promotion": going back to an
  earlier artifact adds its reach back. That artifact is in production again, so its round's line is again inside
  its *at least*. **If the unit was disposed of in between, ADR-0021 §7 cannot be met for the returned artifact**,
  and nothing restores the unit. Its header's state reads *disposed*, and the promotion's voters can read that
  (ADR-0076 §7 RD-4). Whether a return to such an artifact should be gated is not decided here (Annex B, item 7).

### 3. Erasure in the archive

**Restated.** Erasure "is an event — consensus-ordered, modeled in TLA+, recorded in the log it modifies", and its
authorization "lives in the verified object" (ADR-0006). It is a quorum decision on an anchor-issued roster
(ADR-0013 §1). Its mechanism "is deletion of sealed round topics (ADR-0003) plus deletion from the separately
governed plaintext store (ADR-0004). Crypto-shredding was rejected as the primary mechanism" (ADR-0028). The unit
is the round's topic with its companion, "dropped whole" (ADR-0003 A3).

**New: how that event reaches the archive.**
- **ER-1. The archive erases on the fact and on nothing else.** The feeder of facts sees an F6 entry in a segment.
  The entry names units. The governed store drops each named unit's container, whole, in every fault domain. No
  operator, administrator or program has another path that removes a unit's bytes, because "deletion cannot be an
  out-of-band operator action" (ADR-0006).
- **ER-2. An erasure is ordered, then complete, and the difference is visible.** When every copy is gone the
  archive reports it, and the mechanism orders an archive mark of kind *erased* naming the unit and the F6 entry
  (ADR-0074 §4 A-2). Between the F6 entry and that mark the erasure is ordered and not complete. T-0045-7 already
  names this failure, "An erasure that fails to delete (ADR-0006) is a legibility residual in the other direction".
  This makes it a state a reader can list. **The mark records the archive's report and proves nothing more**
  (ADR-0074 §4 A-7): the mechanism cannot see that bytes are gone. The fixity audit looks for a container that a
  mark says was dropped (ADR-0076 §5).
- **ER-3. Deletion is the mechanism, and a key is at most an addition.** A deployment may also encrypt each unit
  under its own key and destroy the key at erasure. That reaches copies deletion cannot: a block not yet reclaimed,
  a replica that was offline. **It is never the evidence that an erasure happened**, and a unit whose key is gone
  and whose ciphertext remains is not erased under ER-2. This is ADR-0003's objection kept: ciphertext with no key
  is "a hole no auditor can distinguish from suppression".
- **ER-4. No copy of a unit exists that an erasure cannot reach.** A unit is copied only to the governed store's own
  fault domains, each of which receives the drop. A backup of the governed store that deletion would not reach is
  not taken, unless it is encrypted under the units' own keys (ER-3) and the deployment declares it (D-0075-3).
- **ER-5. Nothing derived from a unit's payload lives outside the unit.** An index over bodies, an extract, an
  embedding or a summary is stored inside the unit's container or keyed by the unit and dropped with it. The
  archive's index holds headers and facts and no payload-derived attribute (ADR-0076 §4). ADR-0019 gave the reason
  for logprobs, that keeping a derivative "may preserve a shadow of the thing erasure promised to destroy", and
  ADR-0038's finding 3 routed the correlator's case of it to ADR-0006.
  ⟨R2⟩ ADR-0070 §8 gives Run a way to reach Build as evidence: "Recorded traces enter the specification graph's
  runtime layer". A trace of admits is facts, and this rule does not touch it. A trace that carries bodies is a
  derivative, and is kept in its unit or not kept.
- **ER-6. What survives an erasure**: every fact, the F6 entry among them; the unit's header; and the digests the
  log holds of what was in the unit, since "Its digests in the log survive erasure" (ADR-0042 §3). **Restated:**
  demand type 5, "Hide that a record ever existed", is not supported at the archetype (ADR-0013 §5). ADR-0004's
  residual stands, that "the pointer is itself a disclosure".
- **ER-7. An erasure of a unit that is not in the archive completes at once**, with a mark saying the unit was not
  captured or was already disposed of.
- ⟨R2⟩ **ER-8. A drafting session that reads a unit's body is a copy no erasure reaches, and what it drafts is a
  derivative.** ADR-0070 (Proposed) puts the assistant's session between Run and the corpus. Its B29 is the
  boundary from a session to its provider, crossed by "Everything the session reads", with "No instant". Its §5.1
  expects a session to read "an intake object, a trace or a Report", and marks what such a session drafts, so that
  §8's removal test counts the draft as Run's. **The mark answers that test and does nothing for an erasure.**
  - **What a session reads of a unit stays with a provider.** The content of a `Report` is payload: ADR-0052 §5
    calls it "content on the topic". Once a session has read it, an erasure of its round leaves a copy in logs the
    deployment does not hold. So at the archetype **no body of a unit is read into a drafting session** *(drafter's
    choice)*. A deployment whose provider keeps nothing, or deletes on an order the deployment can verify, may
    declare otherwise (D-0075-4).
  - **A draft made from a body lands where nothing is erased.** A record, a derived document and a unit of
    documentation go into the corpus, which line C keeps without limit and a carrier's history keeps besides.
    ADR-0069 §10.1 (Proposed) already has a labelled return "Never in git", since rewriting history to erase it
    "would be the out-of-band deletion ADR-0006 forbids". The same holds of a sentence quoted from a body into a
    draft.
  - **Facts are another matter.** An admit, a stop attribution, a vote and an issuance are in the log and are not
    erasable, and a session may read them. ADR-0070 §5.1's mark then does its work. Who else reads them is B29's
    question, and it is one of confidentiality and not of erasure.

  This narrows a choice ADR-0070 §8 leaves open. That record recommends marking, and holds back the stricter rule,
  "keeping Run-derived material out of drafting sessions altogether". For bodies, erasure forces the stricter rule.
  For facts, marking is enough.

### 4. Every absence is explained

**This is the archive's form of the property ADR-0006 said erasure "Requires"**, one "distinguishing lawful erasure
from history rewriting".

- **AB-1. A unit's container is present, or a fact says why it is not.** For every header, exactly one holds: the
  container is in the governed store; an *erased* mark cites an F6 entry that names the unit; a *disposed* mark
  cites the schedule line whose period ended (§5); or the header says the unit was *not captured*. **A header with
  none of the four is a finding**: a loss, or a suppression, and the archive cannot tell which.
- **AB-2. A fact is never lawfully absent.** No erasure, disposition or hold removes an entry of holding A within
  its line's *at least*. An absent entry under a mark is always a finding. This is NC-2 (ADR-0013 §4) held at the
  archive, over every class of ADR-0028 §1 and not the three the module checks.
- **AB-3. Nothing is dropped before its fact.** A container is dropped only after the F6 entry or the end of its
  period is in the log. A drop with no such entry before it is a finding against the archive, recorded by whoever
  finds it.
- **AB-4. An erasure rewrites nothing.** No segment, root or mark changes when a unit is erased. The Merkle tree of
  ADR-0076 §5 is over facts, and no payload is a leaf of it. That is how an erasure can be "recorded in the log it
  modifies" while "signatures and hash chains are never edited": what an erasure modifies is the record of a
  round, and the log is not touched.
- **AB-5. Replay survives erasure.** Conformance replays admits (ADR-0005 §4), which are facts. ADR-0004's Context
  already had it that conformance "does not need prompt text". What replay loses with a unit is the unit's denials
  (ADR-0074 §2 H-4).

### 5. Disposition at the end of a period

- **DP-1. A unit whose *at most* has ended is dropped whole**, as an erased unit is, under ER-4 and ER-5.
- **DP-2. Disposition is recorded.** The mechanism orders an archive mark of kind *disposed*, naming the unit and
  the schedule line.
- **DP-3. Disposition is by the schedule in force and never by a person's choice.** A unit is not picked for
  disposal. The line's period ends, and the unit goes.
- **DP-4. A clock that fails must fail towards keeping** *(drafter's choice)*. A period is long, and nothing is lost
  by ending it late but exposure. Ending it early loses evidence that cannot be recovered. ADR-0032 trusts an
  interval "by failure direction", and for disposition the safe direction is late. Disposition therefore waits for
  the entry that says the period ended, and a replica's own clock disposes of nothing.
  - ⟨R2⟩ **This is ADR-0032 §2's rule turned round, and neither that record nor ADR-0070 states it for the
    record.** ADR-0032's table sorts an interval by what its failure does to authority, and reads one that subtracts
    with a margin "treated as elapsed early rather than late". ADR-0070 §1 says of a boundary that a crossing which
    subtracts "may run on a local monotonic clock". Both are about authority, and a retention period carries none.
    What its ending subtracts is the record, and the record's safe direction is ADR-0028's, "more, never less". So
    an *at most* is read with the margin on the other side: treated as elapsed late. ADR-0070's row for retention
    and erasure (B9) leaves its clock cell empty, and this is what belongs in it.
  - ⟨R2⟩ **No trusted clock exists to do better.** ADR-0045 §6 refuses trusted time: "a time source enters only as
    a trust root with an attested identity, which no record has written". So an *at most* in calendar time is a
    limit a deployment can overrun. **It is not a guarantee of deletion by a date.** A deployment may declare a
    period as a count of something the log orders instead, as ADR-0070 §6.5 counts a bake-in "in rounds of the
    ring". A regulator's term is in years, and mapping one to the other is the deployment's. Which clock, read by
    whom, proposes the entry that ends a period is not decided here (Open obligations).
- **DP-5. Only B and D-r are ever disposed of.** The other lines have no *at most*.

### 6. A closed chain

ADR-0028's open obligation: "The horizon does not survive re-genesis". The archetype horizon is the chain's
lifetime, and "Re-genesis (A-0025) starts a new chain, not a continuation" (ADR-0027). The rule for what a
successor may read "is carried as an ADR-0025 re-genesis-migration obligation". That rule is ADR-0025's to write.
**This section says what the archive does, so that the archive is not why the record lapses.**

- **RG-1. Everything is keyed by chain.** ADR-0003 A1 already identifies a topic "by its rules-epoch chain". Every
  segment, unit, header and mark carries its chain's id, and a successor chain never writes under a predecessor's.
- **RG-2. A closed chain's holdings are kept** *(drafter's choice)*. When a chain ends, its feeders stop and its
  holdings close. Each line's *at least* then runs as if the chain had continued for the life of the chain that
  succeeds it, and so on down the line of succession. A chain with no successor is kept until a person with the
  authority to decide says otherwise, which no record names.
- **RG-3. Who may read a closed chain is not decided here.** The archive holds reader sets (ADR-0076 §7). Whose
  issuance sets them for a predecessor's record is the migration rule ADR-0025 owes. Until it exists, a closed
  chain is readable by the audit authority its own chain declared, and by no one under the successor's authority.
- **RG-4. Erasure of a closed chain's units is the successor's question too.** An erasure is ordered in a log. A
  closed chain has no log to order it in. Whether the successor's roster may erase a predecessor's unit is part of
  the same migration rule. **Until it exists, a closed chain's units end by period only.** This is a real gap: an
  erasure duty does not lapse because an anchor was lost.

### 7. Holds

**A hold stops something from being destroyed.** The corpus has no hold, and good practice requires one.

- **HD-1. A hold names units, or a class and a range, and suspends disposition by period** (§5) for what it names.
- **HD-2. A hold may only preserve.** It ends nothing, shortens nothing, and gives nobody a reading they did not
  have. This is ADR-0028's direction once more.
- **HD-3. A hold also refuses erasure of what it names, and it does so as policy** *(drafter's choice)*. ADR-0028
  §2 has it that "Bundles may only narrow erasure further". A hold is a deny on the erasure envelope for the named
  units, carried in a promoted bundle. ~~Placing one is a promotion, and lifting one is a promotion that removes the
  deny. Both leave votes in the log (F3).~~ ⟨R2⟩ **Lifting one is a promotion that removes the deny. Placing one
  should not have to wait for a quorum** *(drafter's recommendation)*.
  - ⟨R2⟩ The first draft made both a promotion. ADR-0070 §1's orientation, which is the corpus's own, separates
    them. A crossing that adds is decided by "a quorum, an issuance or a promotion", and one that subtracts "is
    local, needs no quorum". A hold preserves the record and denies an erasure. It gives no one authority, reach or
    a reading. Lifting it gives an erasure roster back something it could not do. ADR-0050 §4 makes the same split
    for operators, under the heading that "adds need a lease and a peer, denies need neither".
  - ⟨R2⟩ **So a hold placed is an operator's deny, and a hold lifted is a promotion.** No variant of ADR-0050's
    alphabet places a hold, and that record is provisional. Until it gains one (Annex B, item 8), a hold is placed
    by promoting the bundle that carries the deny. The boundary then has the interval §8a gives it: an erasure or a
    disposition can complete while the promotion that would have stopped it is still being voted.
  - ⟨R2⟩ **What an operator's hold costs.** One operator could delay an erasure that a roster had the authority to
    order. That harms the person the erasure was for, and adds no authority. ADR-0070 §7.3 makes the same admission
    of a revocation: "Subtraction is safe for what the pattern claims, and not for availability". The pattern's
    answer for an operator is attribution and not prevention. The hold is a signed action on the record (HD-4), and
    only a promotion lifts it.
- **HD-4. A hold is on the record.** It is not secret from the audit authority. Whether the person whose erasure it
  refuses is told is the deployment's duty under its own law, and not the archive's.
- **HD-5. A duty to keep, set by a regulator, is a line's *at least* and not a hold** (§1 SC-3). A hold is for a
  named matter, and it ends when the matter does.

- **RB-1. Four of the six have an instant only on paper, and two have one that does not do the job.** B9-b's and
  B9-f's instants are accepted records', and nothing implements them: no roster exists and erasure is not built
  (`mechanism/` G11). B9-a's and B9-c's are this record's and ADR-0074's, which are Proposed. **B9-e has no instant
  for reading or for erasing, and B9-d's is too slow to stop what it is for.** ADR-0070 §1 has it that "A boundary
  with no instant is not a control. It is a finding". B9-e is a finding by that rule, and B9-d by HD-3's own
  account, until ADR-0025 and ADR-0050 are amended.
- **RB-2. The orientation holds on a second axis.** ADR-0070 §1 orients a boundary by "authority, reach or trust".
  Destroying evidence subtracts none of the three, so the orientation read alone would make erasure and
  disposition local acts with no quorum. ADR-0006 forbids that, because deletion out of band "is exactly the
  capability to rewrite history". The record has a direction of its own, which ADR-0028 states: the forensic floor
  "guarantees retention (exceptions may preserve more, never less)". **Towards less record, a crossing has an
  instant that a quorum, an issuance or a promotion decides. Towards more record, it is local.** B9-b, B9-c, B9-f
  and the lifting of a hold go towards less. B9-a and the placing of a hold go towards more.
- **RB-3. Disposition is decided when the schedule is promoted, and not when it runs.** No quorum meets to dispose
  of a unit. The promotion of the schedule was the decision (SC-4, DP-3), and the entry that ends a period carries
  it out. ADR-0070 §6.5 says the same of a staged promotion: "An advance adds nothing the vote did not."
- **RB-4. Being read is an adversary here too.** ADR-0070 §1 counts "an adversary to what crosses being read, not
  only to its being changed". For B9-b that is the reader who copied before the drop. ER-4 and ER-8 address it, and
  an export does not (ADR-0076 §8 EX-3).

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **Every kept thing has a line**, and the lines the corpus never wrote are marked as the deployment's to write
  and not left as silence.
- **The lock and the delete stop contradicting each other**: facts are never erased and units are never locked,
  and the header carries what must outlive the unit.
- **A missing payload reads as erased, disposed of, never captured, or lost**, and the last is a finding. This is
  the property ADR-0006 asked for, stated over things that can be checked.
- **An erasure that has not finished is visible**, where today a failed delete is a residual nobody would see.
- **A retention duty and an erasure duty are both expressible**, with the direction of each fixed.
- **Re-genesis no longer ends the record by default.**
- ⟨R2⟩ **The retention boundary is declared in the seven terms ADR-0070 asks of every boundary**, and its two weak
  crossings are named as findings and not left as silence.

**Negative**
- **Everything in the log is kept for the life of the chain.** That is the largest possible choice, and F1's volume
  is already an open cost in ADR-0028.
- **A person's name in a fact is kept, and no erasure reaches it.** The record says so and does not resolve it.
- ~~**A hold is as slow as a promotion** (HD-3), and a deployment under a court's deadline will feel that.~~
  ⟨R2⟩ **Until ADR-0050 gains a deny for it, a hold is as slow as a promotion** (HD-3), and an erasure can beat it.
- ⟨R2⟩ **A drafting session can be given what the log says of a round and not what the round said** (ER-8).
  ADR-0070's improvement loop learns from incidents, and the assistant's part in that is narrower for it.
- ⟨R2⟩ **An *at most* is not a deletion date** (DP-4). With no trusted time it is a limit that can be overrun.
- **A closed chain's units cannot be erased until ADR-0025 says who may** (RG-4).
- **No offline backup of payload** (ER-4) is unusual, and loses a protection operators expect.
- **Disposition waits on the log** (DP-4), so a stalled deployment keeps payload past its limit.
- **The schedule has lines no one can fill from the corpus**, and an incomplete schedule is all a deployment can
  write until it decides them.
