# ADR-0063 — Revocation below the intent boundary is bounded in effects: the intent boundary reads a plane's record and answers with watermarks, and a plane may have `K_up` effects outstanding and no more

**Status:** Proposed · 2026.11.10
**Status note:** Confirmation required under **C1**: the rule in §3 is a condition on every grant an autonomous
plane makes. C2 does not apply. **C3 does not apply, and this is the point of the record**: ADR-0032's first clause
is kept where the proposal's first revision reversed it (§6). C4 does not apply. **Modelled, checked and proved in TLA+
⟨R1⟩ (§8), with §4's argument as corrected there; nothing is built.**
**Pending:** none.

## Context

The custodian's second prompt made the TTL the safety parameter, bounded by the mesh fence ⟨2⟩. The proposal's first
revision built on that: an intent's `expiry` and a lease's `ttl`, each read on a local clock, ended authority below
the intent boundary, and ADR-0032's first clause was reversed there.

The custodian then asked whether a quorum of governors, a monotone log and each layer's fence could do it instead,
and took the drafter's answer: **revocation is bounded in effects, and that is the safety property** ⟨12⟩.

**The corpus has met a clock problem four times and has not once answered with a better clock.**

| Precedent | What it did |
|---|---|
| ADR-0034's nonce is a hash of epoch and log index, "so freshness comes from the log and not a clock" (ToO §2.6). ADR-0050's sign-on is fresh "because of what it names" (ToO §7.10) | Took an interval's origin from the log |
| ADR-0039's bound on unread effects is "a count, not a time" (ToO §5.4) | Counted the mechanism's own acts, which have no drift |
| ADR-0039 coupled liveness to admission, so that a dead Observer stops its Thinker | Made silence fail closed |
| R8, the break-glass TTL, was withdrawn because it needed trusted time, and ADR-0045 refused a time source (ToO §10) | Withdrew the interval |

**A log orders and does not measure.** ADR-0032 rejected deriving timing from the causal frame: "Causality orders
events and bounds no interval." A fence is a message, and a plane that is cut off cannot tell "not revoked" from
"revoked, and not yet heard". So it has to stop on something of its own. A count of its own acts is exact.

## Decision

### 1. The intent boundary is the plane's Observer ⟨12⟩

This is `R3-observer-live`'s shape (ToO §5.4), pointed upward. **The plane's record goes up as it is made**: its
log, with each lease's sealed record (ADR-0062 §6), is handed to the intent boundary while the link holds.

### 2. The intent boundary answers with a watermark, and the watermark is the mechanism's act ⟨Q12⟩

When the intent boundary's mechanism has archived a segment and run its checks, it orders a **watermark**: the
plane index it has read through, the digests of the lease records sealed in that segment, and the fences of every
plane above. The watermark comes down with a commit certificate (ADR-0059 §5).

**No person signs it** ⟨Q12⟩. A commander stops a plane by a fence or a revocation, and not by withholding
attention.

**Every watermark carries the fences**, so the fence is pulled as well as pushed (ADR-0059 §6).

### 3. A plane may have `K_up` effects outstanding, and no more

The charter gains `kUp`.

- **The plane charges what is outstanding.** A lease's whole count is charged when the lease is granted, and an
  `ordered` entry when it is admitted. A charge is released when a watermark covers it.
- **The rule.** The plane grants no lease, and admits no `ordered` entry, that would take the outstanding charge to
  `K_up`. That is the strict form: "at most `K_up`" lets one more through, which ADR-0039's model found of `K`.
- **So `kUp` is at least 2** ⟨R1⟩. The strict form makes the capacity `K_up` − 1, and a `kUp` of one grants nothing.
  A lease whose count is `K_up` or more can never be granted.
- **Each governor keeps the same account for itself.** It accepts a lease, or carries out an `ordered` entry, only if
  the counts of its own leases and `ordered` entries that no watermark covers, with this one, stay under `K_up` ⟨R1⟩,
  and it checks each watermark's certificate against the chain. A rule that named only leases would let a captured
  plane admit `ordered` entries without limit (`M_ORDEREDFREE`). A plane is a havoc issuer (ADR-0059 §4), so a
  governor does not take its plane's word for what has been read.

What is charged is an effect: a `local` entry and an `ordered` one, as ADR-0042 §2 counts effects for an Observer. A
report to a peer topic is not charged (ADR-0065), and a tick is not an effect.

### 4. What that bounds, with no premise about time

| Property | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Effects after a revocation are bounded** | After the intent boundary orders a revocation or a fence, the vehicles of an honest plane produce fewer than `K_up` further effects. Under a captured plane, each honest vehicle produces fewer than `K_up`. A vehicle revoked by its own plane produces at most what is left of its lease's count |
| **Stale rules are bounded** | A narrower intent or a tightened bundle cannot reach a plane that is cut off. Superseded rules govern fewer than `K_up` effects there |
| **Exposure is bounded** | An intent never grants more leases than its budget. A lease never permits more entries than its count. An entry never runs past its extent (ADR-0062 §4) |

**The argument for the first row** ⟨R1⟩. Every watermark ordered after the revocation carries the moved fence, and a
plane or a governor that hears one stops. **That stop carries the bound.** A later watermark releases a charge whose
lease may have produced effects after the revocation, and a hearer that released it and went on granting would break
the bound (`M_NOSTOP`). Every lease covered by a watermark ordered before the revocation is sealed and produces
nothing more. A watermark covers one segment and is not cumulative. So every effect after the revocation is charged
to a lease or an entry that no watermark ordered before the revocation covers, and those charges together stay under
`K_up`. An adversary can deliver such a watermark late or not at all, which only lowers what is released.

### 5. A cut link fails closed

With the link above cut, no watermark arrives, the charge only grows, and the plane stops granting before `K_up`.
Vehicles spend the leases they hold, and then hold. The first revision's pushed fence failed open: no news meant
carry on until `T_intent`.

**What reading buys besides.** What the intent boundary has read is archived above. A captured plane's log is not
evidence, and that now holds of the unread tail only, fewer than `K_up` effects.

### 6. ADR-0032's first clause is kept

"Clocks buy liveness, never safety." The bound in §4 reads no clock. The lattice analysis proposes that no safety
theorem have a timing premise among its ancestors, so that the clause is computed and not only stated; §4's
properties meet that. **What a clock can still extend** is one entry in progress, by the residual ADR-0064 §5
states.

### 7. Overlay position, and what is reported upstream ⟨Q11⟩

Reported under ADR-0058 §6: **liveness coupled to admission between planes**, and **a record read as it is made**.

**If the archetype takes them, its records owe:** ADR-0039, coupling restated for a plane and its parent; ADR-0031,
a grant condition on what is outstanding; ADR-0005 and the archive, a record that goes up in segments with a
receipt.

## Options considered

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| **A TTL on a local clock as the safety bound**, the first revision | Not taken ⟨12⟩. A slow clock preserves authority, which is ADR-0032's test for an interval that needs trusted time |
| **A trusted time source** | Not taken. ADR-0045 refused one: no issuance fixes a clock's identity |
| **The intent's budget alone** | Kept, as the absolute bound. It does not slide, so a long mission needs either a large budget or a new intent for every stretch |
| **A pushed fence alone** | Rejected. It fails open under a cut link |
| **A person signs each watermark** | Not taken ⟨Q12⟩. The plane's tempo would be that person's attention |
| **Count at each permit**, in the plane's log | Not available. A governor's permits are not ordered (ADR-0062 §2), so the charge is taken at the grant |
| **A governor that trusts its plane's account** | Rejected. `M_TRUSTMARK` |

## Consequences

**Positive**
- **The safety bound has no premise about time**, and ADR-0032's first clause stands below the intent boundary.
- **A cut link fails closed.**
- **A captured plane is bounded in effects**, per honest vehicle, by a check each governor makes itself.
- **The unread record is bounded** by the same number.

**Negative**
- **The bound is in effects and not in seconds.** A plane that idles and then acts on an old intent is inside it.
  ADR-0064 is for that.
- **A jammed plane stops after `K_up` effects.** The trade is declared: a small `K_up` stops it sooner, and a large
  one lets more through under a revoked intent.
- **The intent boundary's reading is a trust root** for the watermark. A watermark for a record nobody checked
  releases a charge nobody read.
- **`K_up` is a placeholder**, and nothing here says how to choose it.
- **One author, one day.**
