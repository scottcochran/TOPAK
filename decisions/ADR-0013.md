## Context

Erasure (ADR-0006) and break-glass (ADR-0009) are both ratified. Neither can be expressed as a
subtraction from the policy floor. Erasure is privileged, and break-glass adds authority. Both are
floor exceptions: verified-object carve-outs that change at deploy cadence. Every carve-out works
against ADR-0010's minimality, and minimality is what makes floor review trustworthy.

## Decision ⟨R2⟩

### 1. One grant-authorization model

Every **privileged** grant — promotion, break-glass, erasure — is a quorum decision on an
anchor-issued roster (ADR-0025 §1b), bounded by the rules floor (ADR-0026). Erasure and break-glass
differ only in roster and envelope, not in authorization model. ⟨R1⟩

The qualifier is load-bearing. Ordinary Run admits are grants too (ADR-0015) and are decided against
a bundle, not a roster; the unqualified sentence claimed every admit needed a quorum, which no
record intends and the mechanism does not do (S6-13).

*⟨A1⟩ ⟨B34⟩ **A break-glass grant is the one lease of an emergency seating, and it ends with its
round** (ADR-0052; the custodian's decisions, 2026.10.04). The break-glass roster's quorum seats a named
person on an `emergency` seat for one round. The lease ends at the round bound, at its `ttl`, at an epoch increment
or at a targeted revocation, whichever is first, and has no successor and no renewal. No TTL is issued: ADR-0026's
R8 is withdrawn (ADR-0026 W1), and the round's lease is the bound. §4's NC-1 and NC-2 are restated for a record per
activation in `spec/NonComposition_A1.tla` (2026.10.05; ADR-0052 R5), with a third mutation: an activation's record
kept only while its round is live.*

### 2. Exceptions are declared floor entries

An exception is a named, versioned entry in the policy floor. Each entry carries:
- its action envelope;
- its roster reference;
- its non-composition obligations (§4).

Adding an exception is a floor relaxation, which requires an anchor issuance plus a promotion
(ADR-0026 §5).

### 3. Exception-count bound

The maximum number of exception entries is **ADR-0026 R6**. Current count: 2.
*⟨A1⟩ ⟨B34⟩ with X1 retired (ADR-0052 §2), the count is 1, erasure. §2's three items still describe
it. Break-glass stays under §1: a quorum on an anchor-issued roster. It stops being an entry under §2.*

### 4. Non-composition properties ⟨R2⟩

Written as TLA+ invariants in `spec/NonComposition.tla`, each with a mutation that must fail.
Results in `spec/NonComposition-results-2026.09.23.txt` (§6).

| ID | Property | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **NC-1** | Break-glass chained with erasure never yields an effect that is authorized, erased, and unattributable | **Drafted and checked** ⟨R2⟩ |
| **NC-2** | Erasure never removes a record in a forensic-floor data class (ADR-0028), under any composition with break-glass | **Drafted and checked** ⟨R2⟩ |
| *(BG × promotion, BG × issuance)* | *Discharged by ADR-0026 R7; verified in the bootstrap spec* | Moved |

```tla
\* An effect is attributable while its admit decision survives, and - if it was
\* authorized under break-glass - while that invocation survives too.
Attributable(e) == /\ AdmitRec(e) \in retained
                   /\ (e \in viaBG => BGRecord \in retained)

NC1 == \A e \in admitted : (PayRec(e) \notin retained) => Attributable(e)

NC2 == \A r \in created : r.kind \in ForensicKinds => r \in retained
```

`ForensicKinds` is {F1, F5, F6} — admit decisions, break-glass invocations, erasure events. F2, F3
and F4 have the same shape as F5 in this model and are omitted to keep the state space small; the
property is stated over the class list, so adding them changes the constant, not the invariant.

### 5. Demand type 5 is unsupported at the archetype ⟨R1⟩

"Hide that a record ever existed" conflicts with NC-2: the erasure event itself is forensic-floor
class F6 (ADR-0028 §1), and destroying it is not an available option under that record. The
archetype therefore **does not support demand type 5**, and says so rather than carrying it as an
open demand. ADR-0028's negative consequences already state the exclusion; this record is where the
exception mechanism declines to provide a path around it.

**Compartmentation is not type 5.** Removing F6 from a reader set is admissible without relaxation
(ADR-0028 §4), but only while the declared audit authority remains a reader. That narrows who may
look; it does not hide that the record exists. A deployment that must satisfy type 5 in full is
outside the archetype's declared demands, and admitting it would be a legibility degeneracy voiding
every theorem that rests on F6 being readable — a declaration under ADR-0023 §6, not an exception
entry under §2 here.

**What this costs.** Demand types 1–4 remain satisfiable for payloads (ADR-0028). Type 5 is the
case the corpus declines, and the article should say so in those words rather than leaving a reader
to infer it from F6.

## 6. Non-composition check — run 2026.09.23 ⟨R2⟩

**Artifact:** `NonComposition.tla` · digest `119f9a7e878f8929` · TLC2 2.19 · raw results in
`NonComposition-results-2026.09.23.txt`.

**Model.** Two effects; break-glass invoked at most once; erasure removes any subset the envelope
admits and records itself as class F6. Records carry a class: F1 admit decisions, F5 break-glass
invocations, F6 erasure events, and erasable payload.

**Mutations.** The properties are fixed and state what the records claim; a mutation changes the
mechanism, never the property — the discipline ADR-0026 §6 established.

- **M1** drops F1 from the erasure envelope's denials, so the authority trail becomes erasable.
- **M2** lets a break-glass grant widen the erasure envelope instead of only narrowing it.

### Results

| Check | Mut | Invariant | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full mechanism | none | `Safe` (NC-1 ∧ NC-2) | **holds** |
| Reachability — erasure happens | none | `NeverErased` | counterexample ✓ |
| Reachability — break-glass authorizes an effect | none | `NeverBGEffect` | counterexample ✓ |
| Reachability — **the composition itself**: a break-glass-authorized effect is erased | none | `NeverBGErased` | counterexample ✓ |
| M1 → NC-1 | M1 | `NC1` | counterexample ✓ |
| M1 → NC-2 | M1 | `NC2` | counterexample ✓ |
| M2 → NC-2 | M2 | `NC2` | counterexample ✓ |
| M2 → NC-1 | M2 | `NC1` | counterexample ✓ |

**Verdict.** Both properties hold under the mechanism as the records specify it, both fail under a
mechanism that relaxes the forensic floor, and all three reachability witnesses fail as they must.
The third witness is the one that matters: without it NC-1 could have held because the two
privileged paths never meet in a reachable state.

**Scope.** Two effects, one break-glass invocation, small constants. This is non-composition and
reachability, not proof, and it models the envelope rather than the Rego that implements it. The
ADR-0028 class list is modeled as three classes, not six.
