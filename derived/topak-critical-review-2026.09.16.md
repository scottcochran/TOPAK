## 0. Verdict

TOPAK is an unusually coherent design. One premise (every agent is Byzantine at every position) is carried through six layers without a break: models out of the trusted computing base (TCB), syntactic lift only, deny-only findings, floor refinement, consensus for grants, a havoc trust anchor bounded by a verified rules floor. The field converged on "policy outside the model" in 2025–26, but almost none of it treats the **author of the policy** as the threat. That inversion, plus the refinement-bounded parameter model and "legibility, not immutability," are real contributions and survive comparison with the current literature.

The design is also, as of G1, **a specification of proof obligations rather than a proved system**. The corpus's register is "theorem," "proved," and "verified" throughout; the only checked artifact is a 30-line model of one promotion round with four humans, run under the TLC model checker at small constants. That gap is acknowledged in the corpus (rev 5 §12, MAP 2.3), but the register of the prose has not caught up to the acknowledgement, and outside reviewers will read the register, not the footnotes.

Six findings carry most of the weight of this review:

## 1. What holds up

Stated first, because the critical sections are long and the strengths are not small.

**1.1 The derivation is single-premise and the premise is the right one.** Everything in rev 5 §2–§7 follows from "the model is a havoc oracle." That is the same construction as Moon & Varshney's Listing 1, reached independently from a distributed-systems starting point, and it produces the design's best sentence: findings may subtract authority, never add it. The systematic review by Dantas, Cordeiro et al. (2026) concludes that **specification acquisition, not verification power, is the binding constraint on end-to-end agent safety**; TOPAK is the only design in the field that treats specification authorship as the hostile domain and builds enforcement and detection there. AgentFlow (Virginia Tech, Aug 2026), the most capable policy-outside-the-model system published this year, states its threat model as "the policy author and runtime are trusted." That single sentence is TOPAK's positioning paragraph.

**1.2 Refinement-bounded parameters are a real extension.** Moon & Varshney verify a fixed four-variant policy and never discuss change cadence. ADR-0010's floor plus `bundle ⊑ floor`, with stale bundles monotone-safe under partition, is a parameterization the paper lacks. Cedar Analysis (AWS, 2025) is prior art on *comparing* policy permissiveness, and the corpus already knows that; the staleness-safety framing and the use of refinement as an admission gate over a verified floor are TOPAK's.

**1.3 Non-vacuity as a gate, with a checked result.** The `RulesFloorNV` run is the best thing in the corpus. It found that ADR-0024 revision 3 hard-coded the values R1–R3 were supposed to bound (all three vacuous), and it demonstrated the empty-roster promotion: with R2 dropped, a havoc issuance sets `minGrant = 0`, unanimity over the empty set is vacuously true, and an artifact promotes with zero votes. That is the Moon & Varshney §4.6 silent-abstention failure reproduced one layer up, caught before ratification. The discipline of "every invariant ships with a mutation that must fail" is exactly right and is still rare (Khan 2026 and IronSpec are the peers).

**1.4 Legibility, not immutability.** Confluent's current positioning for compliant agents is an "Agent Decision Record" on an immutable SHA-256-chained log. That design cannot honor an erasure obligation without either lying about immutability or shredding keys, and ADR-0003 explains why crypto-shredding leaves a hole no auditor can distinguish from suppression. The forensic floor (ADR-0028) — six control-plane fact classes that survive erasure, enforced as policy-floor denials on the erasure envelope, with F6 recording the erasure itself — is the only answer in the field I found that keeps both obligations. It is TOPAK's most publishable artifact after the inversion.

**1.5 The authority regress ends somewhere.** Most governance designs stop at "an admin approves." TOPAK stops at one sentence (anchor intent), bounded by two verified floors, with custody declared as a numeric pair. The refusal to model anchor composition (ADR-0025 option C) is correct: it adds governance content, not proof content.

**1.6 The gaps are named by the author.** Rev 5 §12 lists six thin places; G1 §9 lists the rest; the narrative log recorded eighteen findings against the corpus, nine of them in the author's own bootstrap set. Very few design corpora carry a debt ledger. This review's job is made easier by it, and several findings below are sharpened versions of things the corpus already half-says.

## 2. The design, critically

### 2.1 Effect exclusivity and the sandbox gap (CR-1)

Rev 5 §4.1: enumerating effects "makes effect exclusivity a mechanism claim, which is strictly stronger than Moon & Varshney's assumed version and TOPAK's sharpest differentiation. It is also entirely contingent on the sandbox gap."

The second sentence cancels the first. Moon & Varshney's Corollary 3.3 has effect-faithfulness as its antecedent; their §5.1 lists exactly what is outside the theorem (direct calls, ambient filesystem or network access, kernel and hardware). ADR-0015's enumeration turns "every effect is mediated" into "every *named* effect is mediated," and the sandbox gap is precisely the set of unnamed effects. Until ADR-0022 §6 is discharged by a mechanism, the two positions are identical in strength. The article should not say "strictly stronger."

Three further points:

- **Sandboxing is commodity.** In 2026 every shipping coding harness runs its shell inside an OS-enforced sandbox: bubblewrap or Seatbelt for the filesystem, seccomp for Unix-socket blocking, and an egress proxy with a domain allowlist that also masks credentials at the boundary (the Claude Code sandbox documentation describes exactly this stack). MicroVM and gVisor runtimes are catalogued by the dozen. The "sandbox gap" is therefore not a missing technology; it is a missing **declaration of which sandbox class discharges §6, and an attestation path (RFC 9334, Remote Attestation Procedures; measured boot) by which the mechanism can verify the sandbox it is running inside.** The 09.16 container-vacuity session moves this direction (Class 2, confidential VMs, measured boot); it should become a record.
- **The "no shell" cost is a choice about where the modeled boundary sits.** ADR-0022 puts the alphabet at the action-type level (`Read`, `Draft`, `Invoke`). If the sandbox is the enforced effect boundary, a shell whose only reachable effects are workspace writes is observationally `Draft`. Moon & Varshney's own design pressure ("narrowness of the interface determines the strength of the guarantee") is about the *effect* interface, not the command syntax. TOPAK can keep its guarantee and give Build principals a hermetic shell by modeling the sandbox boundary as the alphabet's `Invoke` envelope with a hermeticity attestation. The capability cost the article is bracing to admit may be smaller than stated.
- **`Contained` may be empty for two reasons, not one.** The corpus names the sandbox. The second is provenance: at any affordable rung on the ADR-0017 axis, the independence criterion for two lineages is unwritten, so no deployment can be *declared* at archetype rung. Rung 1 (in-house fine-tune on possessed weights) is `Contained` with lineage debt, which is fine, but the article should say that the archetype's `Contained` set is currently unreachable on two axes at once.

### 2.2 Detection fails open (CR-2)

This is the finding I would put first if the sandbox gap were closed tomorrow.

The Observer is deny-only by construction (ADR-0007). In Build, "veto-seat silence is not a veto" and "an unavailable Observer vetoes nothing" (ADR-0024 §5 and open obligations). In Run, HOTL is "disabling only" (boundary register B10): the system proceeds unless something stops it. Put together: **an Observer that is dead, partitioned, starved of GPU (ADR-0008 names contention as an availability attack), or colluding with the Thinker produces exactly the same observable as a clean system.** The monotone rule that keeps semantic judgment out of the admit path also makes the absence of judgment invisible.

The corpus lists a "veto-seat liveness heartbeat" as open for promotion only. The same question applies to every Run admit, and it is admissible under ADR-0007 because a heartbeat is syntactic: *the Observer replica set attested liveness within the last `T`* is a totally-extractable fact, not a judgment. Coupling admission to Observer liveness puts the Observer on the hot path and adds a term to `W < L < D`, which is a cost; not coupling it means the detection layer has no fail-closed mode. Either is defensible. Neither is declared.

Collusion is the harder half. In AI-control vocabulary, TOPAK's Observer is an *untrusted monitor* (a model watching a model). Nelson et al. (Feb 2026) sketch the safety case: it depends on bounding honest error rates, collusion ROC curves, and *honeypot slack*, with model diversity, paraphrasing, and seeded backdoored inputs as the mitigations. TOPAK has the first mitigation (cross-family, ADR-0016 §5 seat distinctness) and nothing else: no honeypots, no elicitation testing, no independent scoring. Makins et al. (July 2026) show that coordinated agents fragment intent across instances so per-instance monitors miss it; Arike et al. (Jan 2026) report a "less-is-more" effect where curated excerpts beat full transcripts, which is mildly supportive of boundary-only observation but is evidence about *curation*, not about withholding. The honest position for the article: the Observer is a detection layer with an unmeasured false-negative rate, its independence is a mitigation rather than a guarantee, and the design has no mechanism for measuring it. ADR-0024's "independent evidence" obligation and the MEASURE 2.13 floor-fitness gate are the right hooks; a seeded-discrimination gate for *reviewers* (a known-vacuous bundle that must be denied) is the cheapest addition and mirrors G3 applied to humans.

### 2.3 No delegation or chain model (CR-3)

Shukla & Joshi (AGI 2026) prove three limits on runtime enforcement in multi-agent systems and report two measurements. The limits: policies over unbounded futures are not enforceable (TOPAK agrees; safety properties only, per Schneider 2000); properties depending on code semantics are not enforceable (TOPAK agrees; deny-only Observer); and **certain multi-agent coordination structures cannot be secured by purely local enforcement.** The measurements: sink-anchored monitors cannot distinguish self-suppression from non-emission, and asynchronous detection **decays as 1/L! with chain length**.

TOPAK's `W < L < D` has no chain-depth term. LTRO (Listener/Thinker/Reader/Observer) is a decomposition of one principal; there is no record for delegation, on-behalf-of attribution, or the provenance of a task that passed through three principals before reaching a tool (the 09.16 LTRO field assessment reached the same conclusion from the identity-standards side). ADR-0023 reverses ADR-0008 toward "per-principal Observers plus a cross-principal correlator," which is the non-local enforcement Shukla & Joshi call for, and Makins et al.'s fragmentation effect is direct empirical support for the reversal. But the correlator has no record, no alphabet, no placement rule, no seat on the promotion roster (S6-15), and no statement of what it reads. As it stands, the design's answer to the strongest published impossibility result is a noun.

A related seam: ADR-0015 says denials are not ordered. ADR-0020 says denials are "the only evidence containment did anything." For a correlator, the *order* of denials across principals is the probe signature (one agent tests the boundary; another exploits what it learned). Unordered denials cannot be correlated. Per-principal sequence numbers on denials, the same instrument ADR-0015 already proposes for the admit-to-emit residual, would close this at near-zero cost.

### 2.4 The formal layer (CR-4)

What exists: `RulesFloorNV.tla`, 26 TLC configurations, small constants, one artifact, one issuance, no veto seats modeled, no `Vote` envelope modeled (S6-3). What the corpus says exists or will exist: a `Contain` spec, five bootstrap modules with five invariants, trace conformance, non-composition invariants NC-1 and NC-2, and, per the 09.16 spec-set session, fourteen modules in total. What is claimed in prose: "Proved" (rev 5 §2.1, as a label), "containment properties are proven absolutely against the floor" (ADR-0010), "theorems" throughout §7–§8.

Three specific problems:

- **TLC versus TLAPS is undeclared** (MAP 2.3, open since 09.12). TLC over bounded constants is non-vacuity and discrimination, not proof, as ADR-0026 §6 itself says. Moon & Varshney's guarantee is universal because Dafny quantifies over the action type; a TLC result is not universal in that sense. Borrowing "absolutely" without declaring the method is the single easiest thing for a formal-methods reviewer to attack.
- **No refinement to anything that runs.** Moon & Varshney compile verified Dafny to the deployed Python. Khan (June 2026) models five concurrency anomalies in TLA+, exhibits TLC counterexamples for each, then mechanizes the detectors in Verus with spec-to-implementation refinement and ships three Rust runtimes. MAREF (Aug 2026) is a smaller example of the same shape: a few hundred lines of governance core model-checked, the rest tested. TOPAK's mechanism, validator, decoder, and refinement checker are all unwritten, and there is no stated plan for how a TLA+ spec will be connected to them (trace conformance under ADR-0004 is the closest thing, and it is itself an obligation).
- **"W < L < D is only partly expressible in standard TLA+"** (rev 5 §11.3) and the clock model is unstated. Basin, Klaedtke and Zălinescu (2013) show that once timing enters, what a monitor can enforce depends on the clock and observation model. TOPAK's leases, fencing, vote timeouts, and the proposed break-glass TTL all rest on an unstated clock. This is the standard hole in lease-based designs and the corpus knows it (register §9.1); it has been "cheap to close" for three checkpoints.

None of this is fatal. It is a sequencing statement: the article can present the *obligations* as theorems-to-be with mutations, exactly as ADR-0029 §5 does for the bootstrap set, but it should not use "proved" in the present tense anywhere.

### 2.5 The governance process reviews everything except the foundations (CR-5)

ADR-0029 is careful and self-aware, and it still has a hole. Confirmation-required criteria C1–C4 mark records that carry privileged-path proofs, exhaustiveness or confinement claims, reversals, or debt resolution. They do not reach:

- ADR-0007 (the central thesis and the totality claim),
- ADR-0010 (the definition of the verified object),
- ADR-0012 (the resident floor),
- ADR-0016 to ADR-0020 (the Persona series, including two accepted records whose citations are pending errata),
- ADR-0003 and ADR-0004 (the record's structure),
- ADR-0029 itself.

Section 6 then says "genesis-ratified records without the marker are not re-reviewed." So the most load-bearing decisions in the corpus are the ones guaranteed never to receive independent review, while the forensic floor and the exception mechanism (important, but downstream) will. The exception for the bootstrap set is justified (circularity); the silence on the rest is not. A **C5: load-bearing premise** criterion, or a blanket post-bootstrap confirmation sweep of all genesis records, closes it. The cost is a larger confirmation set; the corpus already concedes that set "can grow without bound."

There is a reflexive point here that the article should make before a reviewer does. The corpus was drafted at roughly six records a day by one custodian working with a large language model, and it was recovered, folded, normalized, and checkpointed by the same pair. TOPAK's own thesis is that Build agents authoring the specification and floor are the threat, and that Build needs an Observer. The corpus's Build has none. ADR-0029 handles the human half (genesis custody, (1, 0)) and says nothing about the model half. Declaring "records were LLM-drafted and custodian-ratified; that is the genesis Build with no Observer, and it is why the confirmation set exists" is consistent with the pattern and costs nothing.

### 2.6 Policy expressiveness (CR-6)

ADR-0007 restricts the admit path to syntactic lift, and the admit decision as the corpus describes it is a pure Rego evaluation over one action against a pinned bundle (ADR-0010; purity is what keeps EL zone 1 alive under partition). That is the right restriction, and it has a consequence the corpus does not state: **the floor and bundles are per-action envelopes and cannot express constraints over sequences or flows.** "Read from the confidential store, then `Invoke` the mailer" is two admissible actions. AgentFlow's whole contribution is path rules over exactly that shape; CaMeL and FIDES get the same result with capabilities and information-flow labels. All three enforce outside the model, all three trust the policy author, and all three can express something TOPAK cannot.

The fix is inside ADR-0007, not against it. A label that records *where a datum came from* (source topic, corpus ref, tool output) is mechanically extracted; it is provenance, not meaning. Carrying source labels as syntactic-lift fields on `Read`, `Draft`, and `Invoke` arguments, and letting the floor deny sinks by label, adds taint tracking to the admit path without a classifier. CaMeL's design shows the labels stay syntactic even when the data they label is untrusted text. This is one candidate ADR with unusually high leverage: it converts the field's strongest counterpart into a subset of TOPAK.

### 2.7 Component instantiations

**O (OPA/Rego).** Rego has no formal semantics, no verified evaluator, and no shipped permissiveness analysis. `bundle ⊑ floor` "decided over variants with symbolic parameters, Rego predicates checked by an SMT solver" (rev 5 §2.4; SMT, satisfiability modulo theories) is a program of work that would have to start by formalizing a Rego subset. Cedar has a Lean-verified semantics, a decidable analysis that classifies a policy change as more permissive, less permissive, equivalent, or incomparable, and, as of May 2026, is the policy language of Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, chosen for agent tool-call authorization on exactly the analyzability grounds TOPAK needs. TOPAK's "substitute any component" clause covers this, but the article's TL;DR leads with OPA as "a hard guardrail," and a reviewer who knows both languages will ask why. The strong move is to state the requirement (a policy language with a verified evaluator and decidable refinement) and name Cedar as the reference instantiation, keeping OPA where the deployment already has it.

**K (Kafka).** Two problems. First, topic-per-principal-per-round at Run cadence (ADR-0021: "sub-second to seconds") means tens of principals produce hundreds of thousands to millions of topics per day. KRaft (Kafka's own Raft-based metadata quorum) raised the practical ceiling to roughly 1.9 million partitions on a three-broker cluster (Instaclustr's measurements), and that is a *static* ceiling; per-round creation and deletion churn through the metadata log is a different and worse workload. ADR-0003 concedes "broker metadata pressure is a real operational cost and needs a lifecycle story," and nothing since has quantified it. Second, "Paxos over Kafka, not KRaft; three consensus groups" is an unrecorded decision (G1 §10). If Paxos messages ride Kafka, then Kafka's availability bounds grant liveness and Kafka is back in the liveness TCB after ADR-0005 removed it from the safety story. That may be fine; it needs a record, and the ADR-0005 narrative should stop reading as if Kafka's demotion were clean.

**P (Paxos).** The article offers "Basic Paxos, Multi-Paxos, Byzantine Multi-Paxos, or a global lock" as interchangeable. Rev 5 uses "Byzantine" for agents while the control plane runs crash-fault-tolerant Paxos among orchestrator nodes that are, implicitly, trusted. The fault model of the control plane is never declared; the trusted base is never enumerated (the 09.12 external assessment asked for this and rev 5 still lacks it). The recent Byzantine-fault-tolerant-among-agents literature (Lee et al., 2026, self-anchored consensus) is about a different problem, consensus over agent *outputs*, and TOPAK is right not to do that; but the contrast should be explicit so a reader does not assume "Byzantine" describes the orchestrators.

**A (Persona).** ADR-0016's binding of weights, decoder, system prompt, decoding parameters, and seat is stronger than the OpenSSF Model Signing specification (v1.0, 2025), which signs the model artifact alone. Cite it as prior art on the signature, and claim the runtime-configuration binding and the lease attestation (ADR-0016 §6) as the extension. The boundary register's B8 findings (no expiry, no re-attestation, revocation mid-round undefined) remain the largest empty cell and are still unaddressed by any record.

### 2.8 Authority, custody, and transparency

**The archetype custody may not be the maximum.** A-0025 is strict unanimity, (n, 0): any permanent key loss freezes the rules and the only exit is re-genesis, which ADR-0027 §4 makes a new system. Re-genesis is the largest authority event the design has, performed under duress, producing a new TCB. If "forced re-genesis on a single loss" is counted as a safety cost, then (n−1, 1) with a time-locked notice window in which an outside quorum may deny (D-0025-2's optional mitigation) plausibly Pareto-dominates (n, 0). ADR-0023's dominance rule says the archetype moves only to dominating values; the lattice's order should therefore include forced authority events, and A-0025 should be re-examined against it.

**Roster capture through people, not keys.** R5 keeps anchor keys off the promotion roster. Nothing keeps a k-coalition (under D-0025-1) from issuing a roster of its own subordinates. The field's answer to root-key capture is not a stronger quorum, it is **transparency**: Certificate Transparency, Sigstore's Rekor, and TUF's published root metadata make every issuance observable by outsiders so capture is detectable even when it is not preventable. ADR-0028 F4 makes issuances non-erasable *inside* the system; an external append-only publication of issuances is the missing declaration, and it is the natural instantiation of the "declared external auditor role" ADR-0028 leaves open.

**Unanimity for tightening is over-constrained.** ADR-0024 §1 requires promotion for everything crossing Build to Run, including strictly subtractive bundles. TOPAK's own first monotonicity layer is "consensus for grants, local for denials"; a tightening bundle is a denial-increasing change. Requiring every grant seat for an emergency tightening puts incident response behind the slowest human. The deferred fast path needs `new ⊑ deployed`, and the stated blocker is what "deployed" means under EL zone 1 staleness. Under staleness "deployed" is a finite set of live versions; requiring `new ⊑ v` for every live `v` is decidable with the machinery ADR-0010 already assumes. This is the highest-value deferred item in ADR-0024 and should be re-sequenced.

**The archetype-to-practice ladder currently has no middle rung.** S6-2 shows k-of-n (D-0024-1) is unreachable from revision 4's parameters; relaxing R1 yields any-of-n. So the only expressible degeneracies today are unanimity and one-of-n. Every real operator will want two-of-three or three-of-five, and the design cannot currently declare it. The `minGrantVotes` parameter the narrative log proposes should be treated as adoption-critical, not editorial.

### 2.9 Composition, and what "declared" can currently mean

ADR-0023 §7: composition of degeneracies is not assumed monotone; two degeneracies may void more than the union of their theorem sets. Every real deployment has at least three: custody, provenance, Observer scoping. Until a composition rule exists, **no shipped deployment can compute its theorem inventory from its declarations**, which means principle P10 ("what isn't proved is declared") is currently unsatisfiable for any deployment that exists. The corpus files this as an obligation on 0023; it is more than that. Two ways out: define composition conservatively (inventory equals the intersection, minus explicitly listed pairwise interactions, each pair carrying a model-checking obligation), or accept per-tuple inventories and require a TLC configuration per shipped tuple (the 09.16 spec-set session leans this way). Either is a decision; the absence of one is the finding.

### 2.10 Timing, for a design that calls itself real-time safety

Open in one place or another since 09.12: the clock model; `D` as a human-response budget (named, never budgeted); active e-stop latency (unbounded); in-flight effects after a stop (no quiesce semantics anywhere); break-glass TTL (R8 candidate); Persona expiry and re-attestation; floor rollout with two floors live. The register's own assessment calls the absence of a timing budget "uncomfortable." It is the right word. The Basin et al. result makes it sharper: timed policies are enforceable only relative to a stated clock and observation model, so several of TOPAK's enforcement claims are not yet well-formed, let alone proved.

## 4. The proposed ADRs

Status is as of G1. "Proposed" and "Draft" are unratified under ADR-0029; pending amendments to accepted records are included because they change meaning. Severity is this review's estimate of what a hostile-but-fair reviewer would make of ratifying the record as it stands.

### 4.1 The bootstrap set (queue B)

**ADR-0024 — Promotion requires a unanimous quorum.** *Sound in its core, incomplete at its edges.* The move from one signing key to a rules-read quorum with author recusal, gate-enabled voting, triple-bound votes, and "a timeout is never a grant" as a mechanism constant is right, and the revision-4 parametricity fix is the corpus's best example of a finding feeding back into decision text. Problems: (a) S6-2, k-of-n unreachable, is adoption-critical (§2.8); (b) S6-5, votes on erasable topics, contradicts §8 and F3 and the proposed fix (vote as consensus-log admit, commentary in the ADR-0004 store) is correct; (c) the deferred subtractive fast path should be un-deferred (§2.8); (d) "veto-seat outage" is an open obligation here and a design hole everywhere else (CR-2); (e) the record depends on ADR-0021, which still describes a single approver (S6-8). **Disposition:** revise per S6-2/S6-5/S6-8, add `minGrantVotes`, promote the fast path from Deferred to Obligation, then rerun `RulesFloorNV` with the `Vote` envelope and veto seats modeled. Ratify after the rerun, not before.

**ADR-0025 — The trust anchor is a havoc issuer; intent is the sole axiom.** *The best-argued record in the corpus, with one contestable annex.* Splitting infallibility into assumed intent, modeled integrity, and declared custody is clean, and rejecting a verified anchor as "relocating the axiom" is correct. Problems: (a) S6-6 (policy floor vs rules floor; rules set omits rules-floor changes) is an easy fix; (b) S6-9, `Resign` undefined, matters more than it looks under A-0025, where a resignation is a permanent loss and therefore a freeze; (c) the annex's ordering puts (n, 0) at the top of an axis whose cost function ignores forced re-genesis (§2.8); (d) the "single custodian (1, 0)" row exists in the axis table but has no declaration record as a *deployment* custody (ADR-0029 declares it for genesis only, and says genesis is not a degeneracy), and it is the row the corpus itself occupies. **Disposition:** apply S6-6 and S6-9; add a resignation row per custody record; add a sentence acknowledging that forced re-genesis is an authority event and stating why (n, 0) is still the reference; write the (1, 0) deployment declaration since operators will use it. Ratifiable after that.

**ADR-0026 — The rules floor.** *Strong, and the non-vacuity section should be a model for every other record.* R1–R3, R5, R7 are non-vacuous against revision 4; R4's withdrawal is justified by the run; the empty-roster finding (R2) is the corpus's best result. Problems: (a) S6-3, ADR-0022 hard-codes recusal, means the "R3 non-vacuous" verdict is conditional on a mechanism not yet modeled; the constant option (withdraw R3 like R4) is smaller and matches the fact that no degeneracy relaxes it; (b) S6-10, headcount four vs five, is editorial but the derivation of five should be recorded; (c) R6 waits on ADR-0013 and R8 (break-glass TTL) is a candidate the register has been asking for since 09.13 — it is the single cheapest monotonicity repair available; (d) "gates run on the rules floor" (§3) is right and currently has no floor-fitness gate to run. **Disposition:** settle S6-3 (recommend the constant option), record the headcount derivation, admit R8 now rather than as a candidate, rerun, ratify.

**ADR-0027 — Genesis rules are resident.** *Correct and short; two wording defects.* S6-4 (genesis is never promoted, so "checked at promotion" should name `GenesisSatisfiesFloor`) and S6-17 (later issued rules must also be resident, unstated) are both one-sentence fixes. One addition worth making: "a compromised genesis is a compromised system" is true and the field's mitigation is reproducible builds plus published provenance for the mechanism release that carries the genesis (Supply-chain Levels for Software Artifacts, SLSA; in-toto attestations), which the register notes TOPAK "re-derives without citation." **Disposition:** apply S6-4 and S6-17, cite SLSA, ratify.

**ADR-0022 §1–5, §7, §8 — Build action alphabet.** *The alphabet is right; the boundary it draws may be in the wrong place.* Six variants, three mechanism parameters, and "finite in variants, not instances" are well argued. Problems: (a) §2.1 above — modeling the sandbox boundary as `Invoke`'s hermeticity envelope would let Build principals keep a shell without weakening the claim, and the record should at least consider it in Options; (b) S6-3 and S6-9 touch §7 and §8; (c) `Invoke` hermeticity has "no owner" — this and floor fitness are the two unowned gates that decide whether the whole envelope is vacuous, and ratifying §1–5 while both are unowned is the ADR-0006/0009-against-unwritten-0013 pattern the record's own status note warns against; (d) the per-class floor evidence (Consequences) should be promoted from "bearing on an open question" to a decision request, since it now applies to both floors. **Disposition:** ratifiable per G1's plan with §6 noted on the face, but only if `Invoke` hermeticity gets an owner in the same batch.

**ADR-0002 A1 and ADR-0010 A1/A2 (pending amendments).** Keep "(HITL)" per S6-7; qualify "the anchor is at the gate" to archetype custody. A1/A2 on 0010 are consistent with 0026 and should ratify with it. One caution on A1: the amended verified object is now "mechanism, pinning, fencing, policy floor, rules floor," and every addition is a reviewability cost under ADR-0010's own minimality argument; the record should say what the reviewable size of the rules floor is expected to be (seven entries today).

### 4.2 Confirmation-required on ratification (queue C)

**ADR-0015 — Boundary effects audit.** *The most consequential Proposed record and the one whose headline claim must be softened.* Two exhaustive sets (effects, authority changes), admit as the orderable event, and the audit chain ending at the intent axiom are well constructed. Problems: (a) "strictly stronger than Moon & Varshney" (§2.1 above); (b) S6-12, break-glass and erasure invocations have no place in either set — the proposed classification (admits under exception entries) is right and must land before the C2 exhaustiveness claim is ratified; (c) "denials are not ordered" conflicts with the correlator's needs and with ADR-0020's evidentiary role for denials (§2.3, CR-7); (d) "Lower profiles … pending the profile axis" is stale since 0023. **Disposition:** revise wording of the exclusivity claim to "conditional on confinement (0022 §6)"; apply S6-12; add per-principal sequence numbers to denials; ratify as C2.

**ADR-0022 §6 — Confinement obligation.** *Correctly left unratified.* This is the sandbox gap in decision form. The 09.16 container-vacuity session's Class 2 (in-house Persona, attested substrate, hermetic `Invoke`) is the right shape for the record that discharges it; the recommended TLC reachability witness for `Contained` (same shape as `RulesFloorNV`) should be its ratification condition. **Disposition:** write the discharging record; do not ratify §6 on its own.

**ADR-0028 — Forensic floor.** *Publishable; two seams and one legal line.* S6-5 (votes) and S6-14 (horizon ends at re-genesis, which is when it matters most; successor inherits read access) are both right. Add: the legal-basis line per class for human-attribution data (CR-11); the external-transparency publication of F4 issuances (§2.8); a decision on ordinary denials (CR-7). The "no erasure roster at genesis" bootstrap note is a model of fail-closed reasoning. **Disposition:** revise, ratify post-bootstrap as planned.

**ADR-0023 — Degeneracy as the deployment-variation relation.** *The right relation; the record is currently self-contradictory and its heaviest obligation is unstaffed.* Premise loss rather than weakening, the admission test, per-axis rungs, and the declaration schema are all good. Problems: (a) S3-4/G1-2, clause 7 says composition is assigned to 0013 while the carried scope says it is owned here — the next revision must pick one and it should be here; (b) §2.9 above — without a composition rule, no real deployment can compute its inventory, which is a stronger consequence than "obligation not discharged"; (c) the parameter set is not enumerated and chain finiteness is unproven, and both are prerequisites for "declared sacrifice" to mean anything; (d) the dominance rule's cost function needs forced authority events (§2.8). **Disposition:** revise clause 7 and obligation 2; add a conservative composition rule or a per-tuple model-checking obligation; ratify as C3.

### 4.3 Plain (queue D)

**ADR-0005 — Kafka is neither ordering authority nor system of record.** *Right, and narrower than written.* ADR-0015 removed most of the vector clock's job; the 09.15 Paxos-plus-vector-clock session was right that a full vector clock is overscoped and that nothing in the agent can hold clock state under ADR-0007. Problems: (a) the record still says "Paxos plus a vector clock"; (b) "Paxos over Kafka" (unrecorded) changes what Kafka's demotion means for liveness (§2.7); (c) the Observer quarantine-gate question that gates this record is "the cheapest open question on the board" and has been since 09.12. **Disposition:** narrow to per-principal sequence numbers contingent on the gate decision; add a Kafka-liveness sentence; decide the gate; ratify.

**ADR-0021 — Build effect stream; Build has an Observer.** *The argument that Build detection is cheaper than Run detection is excellent and should be in the article.* Problems: the record predates 0024 (single approver, approver "at the gate," S6-8), repeats the crypto-shredding misstatement (S6-11), does not identify its sealed-topic-set digest with 0024's gate-evidence digest, and leaves the Build Observer's instance count unestimated. **Disposition:** revise for 0024, move to queue B or make 0024 §7 self-contained, ratify.

### 4.4 Draft and unnumbered

**ADR-0013 — Exception mechanism (Draft).** Highest-risk item on the board by the corpus's own count, and rightly post-bootstrap. The skeleton is sound (declared floor entries, bounded count, non-composition as invariants with mutations). Problems: S6-13 ("every grant" overreaches — scope to privileged grants); demand type 5 should be declared unsupported at the archetype, as the record itself expects; NC-1 and NC-2 are untimed and the register (§7.3) shows they need an interval; and the record's own open obligation to confirm that R6 = 2 is not already at its bound should be discharged before ratification, since R8 and the container-class work may each want an entry. **Disposition:** draft NC-1/NC-2 in TLA+ with time, declare type 5, keep post-bootstrap.

**ADR-0008 supersession (unnumbered).** Per-principal Observers plus a cross-principal correlator. This is the record that answers CR-3, and it must specify the correlator: what it reads (all round topics? admit log only?), where it runs (ADR-0008 placement applies), what it may emit (deny-only), whether it holds the veto seat (S6-15), and its own liveness coupling (CR-2). Cite Makins et al. and Shukla & Joshi as external motivation; the reversal is better supported by the literature than the original.

**ADR-0017 ordering-clause supersession (unnumbered).** Mechanical under 0023 §5; keep the admission clause; write the independence criterion or the supersession will carry the same unfalsifiable clause forward.

**Weakest-link provenance (candidate).** Correct and one sentence; it should be a clause of the 0008 or 0023 supersession rather than a record.

**ADR-0014 — Floor authorship (Reserved).** Narrowed to proposal eligibility by 0024. Still the natural home for `Invoke` hermeticity ownership and floor fitness (MEASURE 2.13), both of which are unowned gates that decide whether the envelope is vacuous. Reserving the number while the two gates it would own stay unowned is the corpus's most expensive deferral.

**Candidates from post-checkpoint sessions (unrecorded):** the containment-class taxonomy (09.16) as the §6 discharge record; an LTRO seat-semantics record (09.16 field assessment found seat semantics scattered across six ADRs); a syntactic provenance-label extension to ADR-0007 (§2.6); an Observer-liveness coupling record (§2.2); a mechanism-release provenance record (SLSA/in-toto, reproducible builds); a transparency-publication declaration for issuances.

## 5. What to change in the article, in one paragraph

Lead with the premise and the inversion, not the acronym; the five components are one instantiation and two of them (OPA, Kafka) are now indistinguishable from vendor positioning. Say "obligations stated as theorems, with mutations," never "proved." State effect exclusivity as an assumption of use with the sandbox class that discharges it, in Moon & Varshney's stack vocabulary. Name the detection layer's failure mode (silence is invisible) and what bounds it. Give the trusted base in a paragraph. Cite AgentFlow's "policy author is trusted" as the field's position and TOPAK's as the exception. Put the forensic floor and legibility-not-immutability ahead of the Persona series in the differentiation list. And write the reflexive sentence about how the corpus itself was built.
