# Part I — Principles

The 10.03 list had eight principles and did not say what kind each was. Seven of them hold of the architecture: a
floor entry, a lease field, a mechanism rule or a theorem enforces them, and a deployment inherits them. The eighth,
"recorded, decomposable, self-measuring decisions", holds of the corpus: a ratification rule, a convention or a tool
enforces it, and no deployment inherits it. That difference is what this part is organized by.

- **Endemic** principles are designed in. Breaking one changes what the mechanism admits, or voids a theorem.
- **Exogenous** principles are produced by process. Breaking one changes nothing that runs, and changes what the
  corpus can honestly claim about what runs.

The seven endemic principles keep their numbers and are restated at this tree. Three are added as P9 to P11,
because the records since G9 state them and none of the seven carries them. P8 is split into the nine exogenous
principles the corpus acts on, P8.1 to P8.9, the first three of which are its old clauses. Every principle names
the features that carry it (Part II). A principle marked **Proposed** rests on records not in force. The numbers
collide with the confinement profile's clauses P1 to P7 and with `RulesFloorNV`'s properties, as the 10.03 list's
did, so a principle is always written with the word: principle P9.

# Part III — What distinguishes the pattern

## 11. Distinguishing characteristics

Drawn from the 10.05 external and competitive assessment (§4 "Where TOPAK's design is alone", §11's summary table)
and the 10.09 alignment (§1.11's reverse deltas, Part IV against IBM's MCP guide), each checked against the records
at this tree. The comparables were read on those dates and are not re-read here; a claim that "no comparable" does
something is the assessment's, as of its date. Ranked by strength of basis: a record in force first, then a
provisional or Proposed record, then a derived document alone. **Design** characteristics are endemic, **process**
characteristics exogenous.
